# Heat Map — *The Blood Code*

Fifty-five explicit scenes across five books. Eleven per book.

**The rule this file enforces:** no two consecutive scenes may match on more than **one** of four axes — position, initiator, dominant, emotional core. Readers who devour three scenes in a sitting notice repetition even when they can't name it.

**The emotional core matters more than the position.** A rough scene followed by another rough scene is repetition even if the blocking is different.

**Ceiling:** Kent-filthy. Crude vocabulary, explicit dirty talk, degradation, edge play. If the dirtiest scene in a book wouldn't make a reader blush on a train, it isn't dirty enough. Vocabulary ratio and density in `STYLE_GUIDE.md` §7.

Every character in every scene in this file is an adult with their age recorded in `series/CHARACTERS.md`.

## 7. Drafting procedure

Before writing any scene:

1. **Read the row.** Position, initiator, dominant, core, register, plot function.
2. **Read the row above it.** Confirm no more than one axis matches.
3. **Check the plot function.** If the scene can be removed and nothing changes, it doesn't belong. *"They fucked because they're attracted"* is not a beat.
4. **Set the choreography lopsided.** One person drives, the other reacts. Never *then he… then she… then he.*
5. **Sentence length does not change.** Narration inside explicit scenes averages 11.8 words against 10.9 outside — that's nothing. The 8–14 rule holds. Momentum comes from short sentences stacking and from fragments, never from comma-chaining.
6. **Vocabulary:** crude by default, ~70:1 against euphemism. Clinical specificity — shape, curvature, texture, specific physical responses. No phonetic moans; named vocalization verbs.
7. **Consent work lives in framing and aftermath**, never in a mid-scene checklist. This project runs dubcon and non-con; a modern negotiation script destroys the register.
8. **Chapter length** runs 20–35% over baseline — 3,000–3,400 words for Book 1. Never pad, never trim to hit the number.

## 8. Scene-level failure conditions

A scene fails and gets rewritten if:

- It matches the previous scene on two or more axes.
- It can be deleted without changing the plot or the relationship.
- The choreography is symmetrical.
- Narration mean exceeds 14 words.
- Either party's agency is removed by hypnosis or by blood. **`CANON_LEDGER.md` §4 rules 1 and 2 — hypnosis cannot manufacture desire, and Selene's blood overwhelms Lucian without compelling him.** A scene that breaks either is wrong regardless of how well it's written.
- A soft euphemism substitutes for a crude word rather than sitting alongside one.
- The register matches the previous scene even though the blocking differs.
