# Comps — the Adeline duet, measured

H. D. Carlton, *Haunting Adeline* (2021, 148k words, prologue + 42 chapters) and *Hunting Adeline* (2022, 168k, 43 chapters + epilogue). The biggest BookTok dark romance of its era, and a duet, which changes the ending math. Measured 2026-09-25 with the same script as our manuscript (`craft/tools/audit.py --book` uses these numbers as ceilings). Stats only. No passages are reproduced here.

Kent figures are in `.claude/CLAUDE.md`. This file adds what Kent doesn't show.

---

## 1. Heat volume: the biggest gap between us and them

| | Haunting | Hunting | King of Teeth (v1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Units at ≥12 explicit terms/1k | **41%** (18/43) | **31%** (14/44) | 11% (5/43) |
| Peak chapter density | 30.8/1k | 31.3/1k | 18.4/1k |
| Hot chapter length | 3,500–5,800w | 3,000–7,000w | 2,400–3,000w |
| Hot words per scene | 3,000–5,000 | 2,500–4,500 | 300–1,800 |

**The lesson isn't "more scenes". It's longer scenes.** Their scene count is in our range. Their scenes run two to four times longer, and the reader who buys this shelf is buying the length. A 600-word scene labeled "filthy at full volume" under-delivers on the promise.

**Violence counts as heat on this shelf.** Several of Haunting's hot units are the MMC's POV torturing or killing traffickers (ch. 2, 9, 19, 28). The explicit density there comes from the violence, not from sex. The reader gets his competence at killing on the page, and it reads as foreplay because it's for her.

## 2. The ladder

*Haunting:* watched from outside in the prologue (0%) → the MMC declares his obsession in his own POV (ch. 2, 4%) → notes, then texts → first contact in her bedroom ~30% → the first full scene at **39%** (ch. 18) → then scenes every 2–4 chapters, the peak at 72–83% → the cliff ending is a kidnapping.

*Hunting:* opens mid-captivity; the reunion is withheld (the author's note warns readers of this); heat resumes ~51%; the peak cluster sits at 67–75% and the epilogue at 31/1k.

The MMC's presence (watching, texting, his own POV) is in **every early chapter** even though touch is withheld. That's the floor rule from CLAUDE.md §2, and it's why the late first scene doesn't stall.

## 3. The pet-name economy

| | uses | per 100k |
|---|---|---|
| "little mouse" (both books) | **171** | 54 |
| "baby" | 148 | 47 |
| "love" (mostly narration) | 217 | — |
| all address terms | — | ~171 |

**One brand name, repeated relentlessly, in threat and tenderness alike.** It titles the merch and is the most-quoted phrase in the fandom. The chapter-closing threat built on it (*"…little mouse."*) recurs as a closer about eight times across the duet, and readers treat the repetition as the brand, not as a tic.

This is the one repetition that sells. It's the difference between a repeated *address term* (brand) and a repeated *construction* (tic). Our v1 had the tics and no brand.

**Our decision (LO, 2026-09-25): "ghost".** Target 40–60 address uses in Book 1, dialogue only, zero in narration.

**"Not X. Y." and "the way…" are model signatures, not voice.** Neither author uses them at even a fifth of our rate. The fix is almost always to delete the negated half and let the positive statement stand alone.

## 6. Sentence shape

| | Haunting | Hunting | KoT v1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| mean words/sentence | 13.0 | 14.1 | 11.3 |
| median | 12 | 13 | **8** |
| under 11 words | 41% | 36% | **61%** |

Ours is far choppier. Most of the excess is corrective fragments (*"Not afraid."*). Cutting the tic lifts the median on its own, so no separate "lengthen sentences" pass is needed.

## 7. Dialogue share

Adeline's FMC chapters run 10–25% dialogue, with set pieces to 50–80% (phone calls, the friend banter). Seven of our Selene chapters ran 3–7%. **The best friend on the phone** is the engine that carries most of Adeline's non-heat chapters. It's the cheapest dialogue source on the shelf, and it keeps the FMC's voice funny and alive between scenes.

## 8. Packaging devices

- **Trigger warning as a dare.** It opens with the cliffhanger warning in caps, then the list, then an explicit "if you don't like X, don't read". It's marketing, and it's why the book went viral.
- **A playlist** in the front matter, song titles only.
- **Epithet chapter titles** per POV ("The Shadow"/"The Manipulator"). Optional for us: our `N · Name` headings are fine.
- **A back-matter ad** for the next book plus a reader-group call-to-action.
- **The author's note in book 2** managing expectations ("if you're expecting a quick reunion…"). That's the same function as CLAUDE.md §9.

## 9. What *not* to take

- Lexicon: Adeline carries core ×19, entrance ×30, length ×32. Those are the weakest lines in the duet; ours are cleaner, so keep them that way.
- Cliff-ending book 1 of a duet: our series is interconnected standalones, one couple per book, with an HEA plus a series hook. Keep that.
- Sentence-level looseness and typos. Readers forgive them in a phenomenon, but they won't forgive them in a debut.
