lucian

She comes through the front door.

The front, not the side, not the service entrance, not the courtyard gate, the one with the fanlight above it that makes a stripe of light on the floor in the afternoon.

She walks across my father’s stripe of light and she doesn’t know it’s his. I don’t tell her, because some things in a house belong to the house.

She’s carrying the bag, the small one, the one with no room for anything except the things she’s decided to keep, and the knife isn’t in it. I look for its shape like I’ve looked for it every time she walks in, and it isn’t there.

“I washed the cup,” she says.

“What cup? Did you finally kill something?”

“The cup in the sink. The one with something growing in it. Shit had a personality.”

“Congratulations. Was it a fight?”

“It fought back like hell.” She puts the bag on the table in the front hall, beside the lamp, in the spot where Cassian used to leave his gloves. “The plant is dead.”

“I’ll buy you a new one, ghost. What kind do you want?”

“Don’t. I’ll kill it. I kill every fucking thing green.”

“I’ll buy you one that’s harder to kill.”

“There isn’t one.” She looks at me. “I’m here, you know.”

“I can see that. For how long?”

“I mean I’m here, not for the night, not for the pact.” She holds my eyes. “There is no pact, and there hasn’t been one for weeks. I’m here because I’m choosing to be here, and I want you to hear me say it.”

I hear her say it. In four hundred years, nobody has ever chosen to walk into this house. They’ve been invited, commanded, summoned, employed. They’ve come for an audience, for a favor, for the pull of a name that opens doors.

She’s standing in my front hall with a bag over her shoulder and no reason to be here except the reason she just said out loud.

“Say it again,” I tell her.

“I’m choosing to be here.”

“Again.”

“Lucian—”

“Once more.”

“I’m choosing to be here.” She says it the third time with a weight behind it that wasn’t there the first two, a woman who has put a word down and is standing on it. “Now will you stop making me say things three times? It’s a habit.”

“It’s a good habit.”

“It’s an annoying habit.”

“Those are the same thing. Didn’t you know?”

She smiles, and it’s the real one. I’ve seen it three times, maybe four, with nothing in front of it and nothing behind it.

I cross the hall and I take the bag off her shoulder and I put it on the table, and then I take her face in my hands.
