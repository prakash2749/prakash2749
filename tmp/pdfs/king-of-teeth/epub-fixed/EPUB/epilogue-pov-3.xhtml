selene

He kisses me in the front hall.

There’s none of the command in it, none of the controlled pressure of a man who knows exactly what he’s doing to a body.

He kisses me like he read the ledger, slowly, carefully, from the corner, as though what’s in his hands might come apart if he’s not careful with the pages.

I hold onto his wrists, his pulse slow under my thumbs, the ancient, steady rhythm of a creature that has never once had its mouth on someone who chose to be there.

“Upstairs,” I say. “Now, before I lose my goddamn nerve.”

“Yes. God, yes.”

“Your room?”

“Our room.”

I pull back, because he said it — our room — and the word our sits between us like a key laid on a table.

“That’s new,” I say.

“Is it wrong? Tell me if it’s wrong.”

“It’s not wrong.” I look at him. “It’s a word I haven’t heard in either language.”

“Then I’m saying it in a third one.”
