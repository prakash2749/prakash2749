After.

The room is still, the ledger on the table, the crown somewhere in the hall, the window cracked and the door open.

He closes the wound with his tongue.

“Don’t,” I say. “Not yet. Can’t you leave it?”

“It’s done.”

“I wanted—”

“I know.” He puts his mouth against the closed mark. “It’ll still be there in the morning.”

I put my face against his chest, where the clock is still going, slow and steady.

“Are we done?” I ask. “With all of it?”

He says nothing for a moment, and then: “With tonight.”

“Tonight’s enough. Isn’t it?”

I close my eyes, his arm around me, the door open and the city outside doing what the city does.

“Shouldn’t somebody get your crown?” I say.

“Silas will find it. God help us both.”

“He’s going to be so unhappy. He’ll polish it for a week just to make a point.”

“He’ll polish it for a month.” He laughs into my hair. “He’s always unhappy.”

“Lucian.”

“Mm.”

“I’m choosing to be here.”

“I know.”

“I’m going to keep choosing it.”

“I know.”

“Even when you’re a fist. Even when the room gets loud.”

He tightens his arm, and his mouth is in my hair.

“Even then,” I say.
