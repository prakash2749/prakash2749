selene

He cleans me up first, a warm wet washcloth between my thighs, fetched at vampire speed and then denied. Then he turns me over, face to face, on our sides, my leg over his hip. He’s still shaking a little, and so am I.

He puts his hand on my throat, gently, cold fingers resting on my pulse.

I put my hand on his and hold it there.

“Ghost,” he says.

Not the way he says it at the bar, or in the car, or with my back on a wall. He says it like a prayer. Then he says something else.

“My witness.”

I go still, because he’s said it before, on the night of the window, in the glass, when he made me look.

Now he says it in a bed with nobody listening through any wall, and it means something new.

Ghost was the first word. In the hallway it meant you won’t remember this, and then it meant the one who did. Then mine, which I took apart and handed back to him, and then good girl, which I’m keeping.

My witness is none of those.

It means you saw everything: the club, the hallway, the dining room and the pantry door and the study floor, the man who said good boy with his last breath. You were there for all of it, awake, with both registers running, and you stayed.

It isn’t a word that locks from the outside. It’s the person who can testify to what I am, because she was in the room.

“Say it again,” I tell him.

He almost smiles. “My witness.”

“And the other one? You know the one.”

He knows which one, of course he does.

“The ghost that stayed,” he says.

“That’s me.” I pull him closer. “Both of those. That’s what I am.”

“Now,” I say.

He knows what I mean. He puts his mouth on my throat, and I tilt my head. The offering is the simplest thing I’ve ever done.

He bites.
