Epilogue

we’re not finished

selene

The apartment is empty and the house is full, and I’m standing between them with a bag over my shoulder.

It’s the same bag I carried into Perdido the night I went looking for a way across the water, small, no room for anything except keys and a phone and the ready willingness of a woman who has decided where she’s going.

The knife is in the drawer, and the ledger is in the house. The plant is dead and the cup is clean. I washed it this morning, standing at a sink I’ve stood at for two years. The washing felt like the last sentence of a chapter.

Less an ending than a translation: the same life, in a different language.

Delphine called this morning, and it wasn’t about Rémy, or the pack, or the bench I sat on. She called because the tea woman in Tremé had a batch ready. Delphine had been buying from her too, and did I want to split the order.

“You drink the tea?” I said.

“My aunt drank it. I drink it because she did, God knows why.” A pause. “Is that stupid?”

“No. Is it any good? Tell me the truth, because the last thing you recommended was that bait shop and I’m still mad about the shrimp.”

“It tastes like dirt.”

“Good dirt. Hell of a recommendation.”

She laughed, a young sound, younger than anything else about her. It came through the phone like her water came across the bench, offered without preamble, without expectation.

I said yes, so we’re splitting the order. A wolf and a woman who smells like something a wolf can’t place, buying tea from a woman in Tremé who has never once asked either of us why.

Is that a friendship? I don’t know what it is. Pack has a word for two people who share a thing without naming it. The word has adjacent in it, and parallel, and something that means walking the same direction without being on the same road.

I lock the apartment with one lock, and the chain stays off.

The walk to the house takes no time at all anymore, because I’ve been making it for a week — back and forth, apartment to house, house to apartment, living in both places and belonging to neither.

That ends tonight. Who told me to? Nobody. I decided this morning, standing at the sink with a clean cup in my hand and a dead plant on the windowsill.

I’m not leaving it. I’m keeping it: the lease, the key, the one lock on the door, my shitty little kingdom, a room in the city that’s mine, that has my shape in it, that I can go back to if the house gets loud.

But I’m going to sleep somewhere else, in a room with a window that sticks and a door that stays open and a man who reads ledgers from the corner.
