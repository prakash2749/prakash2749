interlude V

Cassian

He knows.

How much? Not all of it, but enough. He has stopped telling me where he is going, and a boy who stops telling you where he is going has already decided something.

He sent no word about the dining room. I heard it from Marguerite, who heard it from a physician. By the time I reached the house they had the doors off the small room, and men washing a wall.

The whole raiding party, and he did it with his hands.

The household is frightened of him this morning, and it is not the ordinary fear. They have always been frightened of him as you are frightened of the weather in a place you have chosen to live.

Now they stop talking when he passes.

He walked one of them to the kitchen door himself, the linen girl. He put her in a car with a month’s pay and told her to go to her sister in Lafayette, and he stood on the step until the car was through the gate.

He did that because he nearly killed her.

I have watched him since he was an afternoon old, and I know what that particular courtesy is made of.

What is he, then?

He went through that room like a story from the old country, and then he stopped, and then he gave orders about paying the staff.

Both of those are him.

His father never believed it. I have known it since the boy was a child. He is not a good man wrestling an animal, and he is not an animal wearing a coat. He is both of them at once, all the way down — and the Code was written to make a creature like that spend his life apologizing for half of himself.

His father put the leash on him at the age when a boy will take anything from the man who says it kindly.

Last night the leash came off in a dining room, and this morning he is walking his own servants out to cars.

He is going to be the most complete king this species has had. Whatever happens in the week in front of us, I made that, in a courtyard, every evening, for as long as it took.

Aurelian gave him a philosophy.

I gave him his hands.

There is a very easy way out of this, and I keep being surprised by how easy it is.

There is a house near Bruges that has been in my name since before this city was surveyed, and there are others, and there is a ship on Thursday.

I could walk out of the gate this evening and be gone before he has a name to put on a warrant. He would spend a lifetime looking, and he would not find me.

I have had that thought several times today and it does not stay. It cannot find anywhere to sit down.

A man who runs is a man who was only ever sad.

If I go to Bruges, then there is a king in this city who tells a story about an old man who went strange with grief and disappeared. The story ends with poor Cassian, and the question at the bottom of it is never asked by anybody again.

Why did I do it, then? Not to be forgiven, and not to get away. I did it to be answered.

So I will be in the study when he sends for me.

Will he send for me? He will, soon — this week, I think, and in the evening, because he is his father’s son about certain furniture. He will want the door closed, and he will want to hear me say it again.

I will say it again. I will not deny a word of it and I will not ask him for anything, and when it is done I will give him the rest, because there is a broker with a name and he is going to need it, and I would rather he had it from me.

I will not give him the other thing. The broker never quoted me a price. He told me it had already been paid, by someone who had been waiting a long time for a man like me to walk through his door, and by then I was too far along to ask who.

Who paid it? Let the boy find that one himself. He will need something to do with his hands.

Then he will do what the statute requires.

He will drain me first and take the head after, in front of witnesses, in a room with the door standing open, exactly as it is written, exactly as it was done in the courtyard, by his father, personally, because that man would not hand the ugly part to anybody else.

Will he notice whose hands he is about to do it with?

He will not notice, not for years.

That is my last gift to him. He will hate me for it when it finally arrives, and he will be a great king by then, and it will be too late for either of us to take it back.

I raised him better than his father did.

He’ll do it properly.
