interlude III

Cassian

It should have been finished tonight.

It is not finished, and the men I sent for her are not coming back.

They were competent and paid properly, and they understood what they were being asked to do. Now there is a car in a canal off Chef Menteur, and a woman asleep in my friend’s house.

I have spent the evening with that, in a room with the lamps off.

Do I enjoy this? I do not.

There is nobody to say that to, so it can be said here and go no further. She has done nothing to me: she poured drinks in a club and went home afterward and got a man out of a room.

For that I have now put hands on her, and not for the first time, and if it continues I will put more.

When did I stop needing to be angry at a person first?

Is the boy in love with her? He is.

He has not said so, and he will not say so for months, and he may never manage it in a sentence with a verb in it.

But he has moved her into his father’s house, and she walks the west hall without an escort. Last week an Aldric cousin put two fingers under her chin, and Lucian looked at the man until he left the city.

I have seen him do that before, for a dog he had as a boy.

And does she look back at him? She does.

That is the part I could not have planned for: she is not frightened of him, and she is not impressed by him. Nothing of that description has ever stood in front of that boy in his life.

He is going to tell her things. How many has he told her already?

So she has to go. I have tried, and the trying has cost me good men and a quantity of goodwill I cannot replace.

And the evening in this house?

I came home and the girl who keeps the front rooms had put flowers on the hall table. She does it because she believes I like them.

She is right, and I stood in the doorway with my coat still on and looked at them for a while.

There are portraits at the turn of the stair, and I go up past them most nights without seeing them. A man can learn to do that in his own house.

Isabeau’s is on the left, done in the year she stopped coming here.

Why don’t I go up and stand in front of the other one? I could, on any night I chose.

There is a small chair on the landing, and no visitor would ever work out what it is for. It has been there since the year of the courtyard.

I have sat in it about as often as I have taken ships.

I am not going up tonight.

There is a simpler road, and I keep finding myself at the mouth of it.

What if I went to him in the morning and told him?

He would sit very still, and then he would ask me to say it again, because that is what he does. I would say it again.

Then he would put me in a chair and send for the council, and do what the Code requires of a man who has confessed to what I would be confessing to.

It would take the rest of the night.

And at the end of it he would be a king who had executed his father’s murderer inside the month, under the law, properly, with witnesses.

His father’s law, and his father’s ending.

Everything I have done would become a story about a grieving old man who went strange, and was dealt with correctly by a young king keeping faith with a dead philosophy.

The truce would hold, and the wolves would be handed another long stretch of years to sit on our border and breed.

And nothing in this city would be examined again inside his lifetime or mine.

And they would be kind about me. Can a man be buried under kindness? He can.

That is the part I cannot carry. Marguerite would say tragic, and she would mean it, and the whole of what I am would come to rest inside that word.

The question my friend never asked himself would go into the ground behind me and stay there.

I was right about him, and I have been right about him since before that boy was born.

And if I stop now? Then I was never right.

I was only sad.

So, no.

In the morning I will go to the house and ask after Marguerite’s chest, and she will tell me about the damp. Lucian will come downstairs and find me being kind to his aunt in a hallway.

I will ask him whether the woman is eating.

He will be surprised that I asked, and grateful, and he will tell me more than he intends to.

And I will begin looking for someone who can get close to her without being seen to be mine.
