Before You Begin

This is a dark romance. It is not a safe book, and it is not trying to be one.

Lucian Croix is a vampire king who feeds on the people of his city and erases their memory afterward, and he does not apologize for it. He is not a good man. He is not going to become one. If you are looking for a hero who learns to be gentle, put this down now and go in peace. He learns other things.

What you’ll find inside, so you can decide:

  • Blood drinking, on the page and often. Feeding as sex. Feeding without sex.
  • A first encounter where he feeds on her in a back hallway, believing she’s under his hypnosis. It doesn’t take, and he doesn’t know that yet. His power could never make her want him. She wants him anyway, and that is the whole problem.
  • Dubious consent, from his side of it: he thinks she can’t refuse. She could, and she doesn’t.
  • Explicit sex, a lot of it, described plainly. Oral. Anal. Rough sex and tender sex.
  • Breath play and choking, with a signal and a man who keeps to it.
  • Restraints. Degradation and praise. Name-calling she asks for.
  • Blood play, including a scene where she opens her own wrist to feed him.
  • Graphic violence. A massacre in a dining room. A beheading under the law.
  • Murder of the heroine’s parents (in the past, described).
  • The death of a young secondary character, and sex as comfort in the raw aftermath of it.
  • Grief, revenge, and a heroine who spends the whole book planning to kill someone.
  • Stalking, attempted murder, poisoning.
  • Strong language. She’s a bartender. He’s four hundred years old. Neither of them is careful with their mouth.

Every character in every sexual situation is an adult.

There is a happy ending. It is earned, and it is not soft, and the door is left open for what comes next.

If you’re still here, you already know what you are.

Welcome to New Orleans. Mind your throat.
