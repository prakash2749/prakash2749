Prologue

cassian

The boy should see this. He never will, and he’ll spend the rest of his life deciding what it was.

Lucian is across the river tonight, holding a line his father drew this morning, and he asked no questions about it.

He has never once asked Aurelian a question, and his father has never once needed him to.

I came in through the east door, which has been open to me since before this city had a name.

The chamber smells of a slaughterhouse and a kennel at once. Wolves came through the gallery windows in numbers nobody credited. They are dying in numbers that make my point for me.

Gaspard’s young, most of them.

Boys.

Somebody told them there was a door, and they believed it. Why wouldn’t they?

Aurelian stands in the middle of it with his coat off.

He is beautiful when he works, and a man his age has no business still moving like that. There are wolves on their knees at his feet, every throat open, who learned it too late to use it.

His shirt is ruined, and his forearm is open to the bone and closing while I watch.

Nobody gets ahead of my friend. Who would be fool enough to try?

You can only be somewhere he isn’t looking, and I have spent a long time learning where that is.

I cross the floor, and he sees me, and his face does what it has always done.

Relief.

He turns his shoulder to bring me in beside him, where I have always stood.

It is the one place at his side he has never had to check.

He’s already talking, something about the gallery, something about sending for the boy. Does he hear me breathing? He never has.

I am not listening to the words, only to where he is standing.

I take the blade out of my coat.

He gave me his blood once, as a promise, and I kept the jar. Steel quenched in a man’s blood will not let his body close.

It goes in under the ribs, upward, the way he taught me.

His fingers come down on my wrist, slowly.

The grip of a man steadying someone who stumbled into him.

Then he looks down.

I have seen him take a spear through the chest and keep talking. I have seen him lose a hand on a Thursday and use it on Friday.

He looks at my grip on the hilt and waits for the wound to close. Why wouldn’t it?

It does not close.

It will not close tonight or in an hour. He has minutes, and he will be awake for all of them.

He looks up at me with blood at his mouth and his knees starting to go, and I wait for it. Rage, I think, or grief? Which would be worse?

I have imagined the accusation on that face for longer than his son has been alive. I built a life out of expecting it.

What he does instead is recognize the blade.

“You kept it.”

lucian

The hall doors have been open since morning, and every mourner who came through them was let in by somebody.

I can’t put that down, and I’ve been trying since the courier found me. My father died in a room he’d opened himself. So who walked in?

They’ve come from everywhere, all in black, every one of them early enough to be seen arriving.

At the front, the account of his death is being given by the only person here who is actually destroyed by it.

Cassian’s voice carries without effort, and there’s no performance in it.

I’ve watched men perform grief, and they leave little pauses for the room to fill. He isn’t leaving any, and his grip is on the rail because he needs the rail.

He is telling them that Aurelian Croix fed wolves from his own table, and that they came through his windows and killed him for it.

The room agrees.

The room has been waiting three hundred years to agree with that.

It’s also the last fucking thing my father and I ever fought about.

I told him mercy was a thing you could afford until the night you couldn’t. I told him the Code was a leash he’d put on his own throat.

He heard me out, as he always heard me out.

Then he said the only justification for power is what it declines to do, and asked me to pass the wine. Who says a thing like that and then asks for the wine?

Eight days later somebody opened a door.

Somebody sees me, and the silence moves up the hall like something spilled, and Cassian turns.

Whatever I see when he finds me is not politics: his whole expression breaks open. He comes down off the platform and the Elders part for him, and then his hand is on the back of my neck, the way it was when I was small enough for that to mean something.

“Boy,” he says.

“Were you there?” I ask him. “Did you see it?”

“I was there. God help me, I was right beside him.”

He says nothing else and doesn’t need to, and I lean into the hand for a second, because he has earned it.

“They’re saying wolves,” I tell him, low.

“They’re saying a great deal, boy. Let them. Grief makes people loud.”

“And you?”

“Grief makes me tired.” His thumb moves once on my neck. “Go and look at him. I’ll be right here.”

Then I go to the front and look at what’s left of my father.

What I feel, looking at him, is a question.

Wolves came through the windows, and every account says so. My father was still standing when the ones who ran got out.

Somebody finished him after that, somebody who was already inside the room.

Who was close enough? Who did he let that close?

I turn and face the room instead of the body. Every House in the city and half the continent, all of them dressed for a war they want me to declare.

Marguerite is already rising to talk about the crown. Of course she is.

Who among them opened the door? I tell them I’m glad they came.

Every one of them.
