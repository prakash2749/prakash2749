I could tell him tonight.

He is sitting where his father sat, in the chair his father never once turned toward the window, and he has finally asked the right question: who was in the room. He asked it plainly, as Aurelian asked things, and then he did what Aurelian never learned to do.

He waited.

The lamp is lit and the decanter is down a finger and the house has gone quiet around us, as it does after the household eats. A boy and his uncle in a warm room, and there is nothing in this evening that is not exactly what it appears to be, except for me.

I taught those hands to hold a blade. Aurelian was busy being a king, so it was me in the courtyard every evening, through all the years that takes, and nobody has ever thanked me for it and I have never asked.

“Who was in the room?” he says again.

He has asked it again, because he makes men repeat themselves. He has done it since he was small, because a man who hears himself say a thing has to live with having said it.

Across this desk, in this light, before he has spent a single life on the wrong answer.

The apartment is empty and the house is full, and I’m standing between them with a bag over my shoulder.

The knife is in the drawer, and the ledger is in the house. The plant is dead and the cup is clean. I washed it this morning, standing at a sink I’ve stood at for two years. The washing felt like the last sentence of a chapter.

He looks at the floor as though it’s been accused of something. Then he gets himself up the cabinet, one piece at a time, and I don’t help him. I want to. My hands actually move, and I put them in my back pockets so they’ll stop.

He wouldn’t forgive me for it. You don’t hold a door for a man who owns every door in the city.

He makes it to the bedroom on his own. It takes him longer than the hallway is long.

He sits on the edge of my bed. My cheap frame and my good sheets, the only thing in this apartment I ever spent real money on, and now there’s a vampire on them with his shirt half open and blood dried brown down one forearm.

My mother would have had a lot to say about this. None of it in English.

“How do you want to do it?” I ask.
