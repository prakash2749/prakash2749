# Magic and Power Rules — *The Blood Code*

Hard limits. The purpose of this file is to make every fight's outcome inevitable in hindsight and to kill the question *why didn't they just —* before a reader can finish asking it.

Rule zero: **the reader never reads these rules.** They are enforced in the drafting and surfaced only as consequence. A character who explains a mechanic is a character who stopped being frightening.

---

## 1. Vampire power tiers

Power is a function of age × blood quality × individual gift. Age is the floor, not the ceiling — a gifted eighty-year-old beats an unlucky three-hundred-year-old, and someone should say that out loud once.

| Tier | Age | What it means physically |
|---|---|---|
| **New** | 0–50 | Faster and stronger than any human. Loses to a shifted wolf. Regenerates a cut in minutes. |
| **Established** | 50–200 | Matches a shifted wolf. Regenerates a stab wound in minutes, a broken bone in hours. |
| **Elder** | 200–600 | Beats most wolves without difficulty. Regenerates around a bullet in seconds. |
| **Ancient** | 600+ | Beats packs. Regenerates faster than a human eye tracks. |
| **Apex** | — | One at a time. Aurelian was it. Lucian is becoming it. |

**Lucian is 412 and fights like an Apex.** This is his defining anomaly and nobody in the court has a comfortable explanation. The true one is Book 5's: his father's blood carried more of Verity than anyone's, and Lucian inherited the concentration. Never stated before Book 5. Before then, it's just fear.

**Cassian is 690 and loses to Lucian.** Establish this early and honestly — in a straight fight Cassian was never the threat. He was the threat because he was standing close. That is the whole point of the book.

## 2. Killing a vampire

Regeneration is the entire defensive system. Every kill is a way around it.

**What works:**
- **Decapitation**, if complete. A partial cut regrows.
- **Heart destruction**, if the heart is destroyed rather than pierced. A hole closes.
- **Fire**, if it outpaces regeneration. Takes considerable fuel on an Elder and is unreliable on an Ancient.
- **Exsanguination to nothing**, then anything. Slow, requires restraint, and is how executions are performed.
- **Blood-keyed steel** — see §4.

**What does not work:** silver, garlic, holy ground, running water, invitation rules, stakes as such (a wooden stake through an Ancient's heart is a splinter), sunlight beyond a bad sunburn on the young, and any single bullet ever fired.

**Sunlight:** uncomfortable, not lethal. The New burn and blister. Elders tan. Ancients are fine and find the whole subject tedious. Vampires keep night hours because night is when the city is awake and the money moves, not because morning is fatal. This removes an entire tier of scheduling excuses from the plot and forces characters to have real reasons for being places.

**The execution protocol** matters for the Blood Code to have teeth: the condemned is drained, then beheaded, in front of witnesses. Juliette Vale died this way and Cassian watched. Aurelian performed it himself, which was both mercy and refusal to delegate.

## 3. Killing a wolf

Wolves heal fast and do not regenerate. Nothing regrows.

Enough damage delivered fast enough kills any wolf, which is precisely what an Elder vampire is for. A shifted wolf survives injuries that would kill a human outright and dies of injuries an Elder vampire shrugs off. Blood loss is a real clock.

Silver does nothing. Say this once, dismissively, in dialogue — it's a good beat and it buys credibility for every rule that does hold.

**Shifting** costs energy and takes a few heartbeats, which is the whole tactical problem: a wolf caught mid-shift is a wolf losing. Experienced wolves either shift early or not at all.

## 4. Bloodsteel

Three sentences. Never more.

> Aurelian gave Cassian his blood as a promise. Cassian kept it for four hundred years and had a blade quenched in it. Steel that remembers your blood does not let you close.

That's it. No forge scene, no ritual, no witch's recipe on the page. The mechanism is *a promise, kept wrong*. Explaining it further converts the most devastating image in the series into a crafting system.

**Rules of use:** keyed to one vampire's blood. Useless against anyone else — against Lucian it is an ordinary knife. Wounds it makes do not close, so the target dies of the wound at a human rate: minutes, conscious, aware. Aurelian had time to look at Cassian and understand. He used it.

**Only one exists.** Cassian had one vial and used all of it. This is why he cannot simply repeat the trick on Lucian, and it's why the climax has to be something else entirely.

**What happens to the blade:** Lucian recovers it mid-book. He keeps it. He cannot use it on Cassian — wrong blood — and carrying a weapon that can only kill his father is a physical object that says everything about his grief. It should be in his coat in half the scenes and never once explained.

## 5. Hypnosis — limits are absolute

Requires eye contact, proximity, and intent. Edits recent memory, suppresses alarm, and compels simple physical compliance for the duration of contact.

**It cannot manufacture desire.** Not diminished-ability, not hard-to-do — cannot. A hypnotized human can be made to hold still and forget. They cannot be made to want.

This is the single most important rule in the series and it is stated plainly, once, early, in Lucian's own narration. Everything the romance does afterward depends on the reader having it. It is also why every sex scene in five books involves choosing.

**Fails against:** meaningful supernatural blood (all hybrids, all wolves, all witches), comparable-or-greater vampire power, anyone carrying another vampire's fresh mark, and the sufficiently drunk. The last one is played for what it is — an ongoing irritation in a city built on drinking.

**Selene's immunity** is not partial and not a resistance roll. Hypnosis simply does not reach her. Lucian has never in four centuries failed to erase a feeding. Write his reaction as vertigo, not curiosity.

## 6. Feeding

A vampire needs blood every few days. Hunger degrades control before it degrades strength, which is the correct order for drama: a starving vampire isn't weak, he's unreliable.

**Three registers, and they are not interchangeable:**

1. **Hypnotized.** Lawful, clean, forgettable, and quietly obscene. The default.
2. **Taken by force from someone aware.** A Code violation short of murder. Punishable, and the punishment is real.
3. **Voluntary, aware, remembered.** Rare. Intensely reciprocal — the donor feels it as pleasure rather than injury, and the vampire gets a flood of the donor's emotional state. This is the entire architecture of the pact and the reason it destroys both of them.

**Hybrid blood** hits Lucian harder: stronger, longer, and it carries Selene's feelings with it. It does not control him. It is a drug he chose to take and keeps choosing. Keep the line between *overwhelming* and *compelled* visible at all times — the moment her blood removes his agency, the romance stops being a romance.

**Feeding does not turn anyone.** Turning requires deliberate exchange and near-death and is not something anyone in Books 1–3 does. Selene cannot be accidentally turned, and the question should never come up.

## 7. Witch magic

Costly, contractual, and small. Witches do not throw fire.

**What a witch can do:** brew, ward, bind, scry with a personal object, hold a name, enforce a debt, and make something that suppresses a body's rules for a while.

**What a witch cannot do:** raise the dead, undo a death, break a blood bond, compel a vampire directly, or out-fight anything. Ondine Marchand in a room with a hostile Elder is a dead woman unless she prepared that room first. She always prepares the room.

**The price is structural, not flavor.** Every working costs the client something enforceable — a year, a name, a memory, a future favor with no stated ceiling. Witches are dangerous because of what they hold, never because of what they throw. A witch's real weapon is her ledger.

**The poison** (canon from source §14): suppresses a vampire's abilities temporarily. Cannot kill. Strength drops, regeneration slows, senses go unreliable, coordination degrades — for long enough that an ordinary wolf becomes lethal. Ondine made it. Absalom Cray brokered it. Cassian paid for it. That chain is Book 2's spine and Book 1 gets only the first link.

**Why Ondine agreed:** she owed a debt inherited from her grandmother, and Cray held the paper. She did not know who the end client was and made a point of not knowing. That habit is the flaw Book 2 breaks her on.

## 8. Hybrids

Selene is the only living human–wolf hybrid in the narrative. This is rare because pack custom kills the relationships that would produce one, not because the biology is difficult.

She gets: wolf senses at maybe two-thirds strength, faster healing than human and far short of wolf, real physical resilience, hypnosis immunity, and blood that reads as neither species to a vampire's palate.

She does not get: a wolf form. **Selene cannot shift, ever.** This is a wound, not a limitation to be overcome — do not let a later book give it to her as a power-up. Her arc is about being both things, not about becoming one of them.

Her scent confuses wolves, who read it as wrong rather than as *half.* Several of them find it physically unpleasant, which is part of why she has no pack.

## 9. Questions a reader will ask

Answers exist. None of them get explained on the page unless a character has a reason to say it.

**Why didn't Aurelian dodge?** He saw his friend coming to help. He didn't defend against Cassian because defending against Cassian was not a thought he had available.

**Why can't Lucian smell a lie?** He can smell fear, arousal, and adrenaline. Cassian has been grieving genuinely for four hundred years. The grief is real. That's what makes it work.

**Why doesn't Lucian just kill the Alpha?** He nearly does. What stops him is the captured wolf's claim, and then evidence. The question *is* the book.

**Why doesn't Ondine just say who bought the poison?** She doesn't know, by design, and she is holding a debt that makes her useless as a witness. This costs her in Book 1 and nearly kills her in Book 2.

**Why doesn't Cassian kill Selene immediately?** He tries. Twice. The first attempt is why she stops sleeping at home; the second is why Lucian finally believes her.

**Why doesn't anyone leave New Orleans?** Nobody asks. Nobody has ever asked. That's the Book 5 reveal and its setup is an absence, not a hint.
