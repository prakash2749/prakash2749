# Seeding Matrix — *The Blood Code*

Book 1 has to carry four future couples, one series antagonist, and one absence — without ever reading like an advertisement.

**The two-level test, and it is the whole file.** Every seed must work as character texture for a reader who has never heard of Book 2, *and* as foreshadowing for a reader who has. **If it only works as foreshadowing, it's too heavy and gets cut.**

**Seeds are 3–8 line inserts, not scenes.** A jaw going soft. A refusal. A name that makes someone look away.

**They start in Act 1.** The epilogue tease is the *payoff* of a seeded thread, never the introduction. If the epilogue is the first time a reader meets the next couple's tension, it reads as an ad.

## 2. Silas Roche × Ondine Marchand → Book 2

Five moments. He-falls-first, so every seed is **him**, and every one is a small failure of professionalism.

| # | Ch | POV | The insert | Reads as (no B2) | Reads as (with B2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **5** | L | Mid-raid. Silas walks a line of houses and skips one without breaking stride. Lucian notices and doesn't ask. | A competent man working a list. | He has been protecting that house for a year. |
| 2 | **14** | L | Silas reports where the wine came from. He has the supplier, the route, the hands it passed through — and he gives Lucian all of it except the name of who brewed it. Says he's still confirming. | Thoroughness. | The first lie he has ever told his king, and he tells it for her. |
| 3 | **19** | L | Lucian sends him back to the Marchand house with three questions. Silas already knows the answers to two. He goes anyway. | Diligence. | He is manufacturing reasons to stand in her kitchen. |
| 4 | **23** | S | Selene, at the Marchand house. Ondine hands Silas a cup without asking how he takes it. He drinks it. Neither of them looks at the other the entire time. | Two people who've met before. | A year of Tuesdays. |
| 5 | **33** | L | Cassian's court is collapsing and Silas asks for one thing: a writ of protection for a witch. Lucian signs it without reading it. | Loyalty rewarded. | He spent his only favor on her. |

**Balance:** four Lucian, one Selene. Weighted to Lucian because Silas is his enforcer, and the Selene one is the only seed that shows *Ondine* choosing anything.

**The line not to cross:** Silas never says her name with feeling in it, and nobody teases him. The moment a character says *you like her*, the seed becomes an ad.

## 3. Absalom Cray → Book 2 antagonist

Four lines and a ledger entry. **He must not be introduced twice** — Book 2 needs his arrival to land as a payoff.

| # | Ch | POV | The insert |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **22** | L | A bar. A man buys Lucian a drink he doesn't touch, says three sentences about how sorry he is about the King, gets the condolence formula subtly wrong, and leaves. Lucian never learns his name in this scene. |
| 2 | **31** | L | Silas's report on the poison money names a broker: **Cray.** One word, in a list, in a chapter where a boy has just died and nobody has room to care. |
| 3 | **Epi** | S | The page Silas puts on the table. *The poison was paid for through a broker named Cray, and the account behind Cray has been buying in this city since before you were born.* |

Reads without Book 2 as a loose end the sequel will handle. Reads with Book 2 as the fact that the man who ends up owning Ondine's life stood close enough to Lucian to buy him a drink.

## 4. Rémy Thibodeaux → Book 3 hero

The hardest seed in the book, because a reader has to leave Book 1 hating him *and* unable to stop thinking about him.

| # | Ch | POV | The insert |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **4** | S | Selene has been watching him for four years. She knows he takes his boat out alone on Thursdays and that he doesn't drink. She hates that she knows the second thing. |
| 2 | **18** | S | She finally gets close. He's unloading a catch, laughing with two men, and his right arm hangs wrong. She had not planned for him to have a life. |
| 3 | **28** | S | Cornered. He doesn't defend himself, doesn't beg, and doesn't say he's sorry. What he says is what he was told, by whom, and what he believed when he did it. Then he tells her to go ahead. |
| 4 | **36** | S | Gaspard exiles him in front of the pack. Rémy accepts it without argument and does not look at his father. He looks at Delphine. |

**Load-bearing rule:** he is guilty and he says so. No scene softens what he did, no scene asks for sympathy, and Selene does not forgive him. Book 3 has to *earn* him, which only works if Book 1 refuses to start.

## 5. Isabeau Vale → Book 3 heroine

Three appearances. She never explains a look, and a reader on reread will see she knew.

| # | Ch | POV | The insert |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **20** | S | A court reception. A woman at the piano stops mid-phrase when Cassian enters, finishes the bar, and closes the lid. Nobody else notices. |
| 2 | **25** | S | Cassian says something warm about Aurelian's memory and the whole room agrees. One woman is looking at the floor. When Selene catches her eye she looks back steadily, and says nothing. |
| 3 | **35** | L | After. Lucian tells her her father is dead. She says *"Thank you for telling me yourself"* — and it is courtesy, and it is not grief, and Lucian is too wrecked to hear what's missing from it. |

Two of the three are in Selene's POV, which is right: Selene notices people who don't fit a room, because she never has.

## 6. Tobias Vale → Book 4 hero

Three administrative scenes. Quietly competent, and nobody in the book ever considers him dangerous — which is the entire point of his book.

| # | Ch | POV | The insert |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **13** | L | Lucian needs four hundred years of House records and a functionary has already pulled them, indexed, with the gaps marked. Lucian doesn't learn his name. |
| 2 | **24** | L | The council wants a ruling recorded in a form the continent will accept. Tobias writes it in one pass. Marguerite calls him *the Vale boy* and he doesn't correct her. |
| 3 | **36** | S | The crowning. Someone has to certify it. Tobias does the paperwork that makes a king a king, and then leaves before the party. Selene is the only person in the room who watches him go. |

**The two-level trick:** a reader sees furniture. A reader who's read Book 4 sees that the man who saves House Croix from the Conclave was standing in every room, holding the documents, being ignored.

## 7. Delphine Thibodeaux → Book 5 heroine

Four appearances. The pack's conscience, and the only wolf in the book who speaks to a vampire as if he's a person.

| # | Ch | POV | The insert |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **4** | S | Selene at the edge of Barataria. A young woman brings her water and says *"You smell like my aunt."* It's the first kind thing a wolf has ever said to her. |
| 2 | **18** | S | She's the one laughing with Rémy on the dock. Sister, obviously. Selene has to sit down. |
| 3 | **28** | S | She arrives while her brother is cornered, sees exactly what is happening, and does not interfere. She says one sentence to Selene: *"Ask him who told him."* |
| 4 | **36** | S | The exile. Delphine is the only person in Barataria who walks Rémy to the boundary. |

Seed 3 is the one that matters — she hands Selene the question that takes her revenge away, and she does it on purpose.

## 8. Verity → Books 2–5

**This seed is an absence.** The only technique in the book that works by omission, and the easiest to accidentally ruin.

| # | Where | The insert |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ch. 3 | Marguerite, on why the court won't relocate during a war: *"We don't leave."* No reason given, none asked for. |
| 2 | Ch. 15 | A wolf, on why the pack stays in a bayou that floods: *"Thibodeauxs have always been here."* Selene notices it isn't an answer and doesn't pursue it. |
| 3 | Ch. 22 | Lucian, on why his father chose this city over a continent: he starts to explain, and finds he doesn't have the end of the sentence. He changes the subject. |
| 4 | Ch. 27 | Two enemies in one room discover they agree on exactly one thing — neither side will abandon New Orleans — and both treat the agreement as obvious. |
| 5 | **Ch. 34** | Cassian, dying: **"Verity."** |

### Rules

- **No character asks why.** Not once in thirty-six chapters. The absence of the question is the plant.
- Nobody investigates, wonders, or finds it strange.
- No dreams, no ancient texts, no scholar with a theory. A character who suspects has broken the seed.
- **"Verity" is spoken exactly once**, by a dying man, and on first read it lands as a common noun — *truth*. The reveal is a word a reader has to go back and find.
- Seed 3 is the load-bearing one: a four-hundred-year-old man reaching for a reason and coming up empty, then not caring that he did.

## 10. Seed audit

Run before Book 1 is called done.

1. **Two-level test, every seed.** Cover the future-book column. Does the insert still read as character texture? If no, cut it.
2. **Act 1 presence.** The binding commercial rule is that the *next* book's couple is seeded by Ch. 20 with moments starting in Act 1. Silas–Ondine: ✅ Ch. 5, five moments. Rémy ✅ Ch. 4 · Delphine ✅ Ch. 4.

   **Isabeau (Ch. 20) and Tobias (Ch. 13) both land in Act 2, and that's structural rather than an oversight.** Both are court figures, and neither POV character is inside House Croix during Act 1 — Selene first enters at Ch. 12. Forcing either earlier would mean either handing the seed to Lucian, who has no reason to notice a functionary or a woman at a piano while hunting his father's killer, or inventing a court scene in Act 1 that the plot doesn't want. Tobias's case is doubly so: his whole character is being overlooked, so a Ch. 6 moment that draws attention to him would contradict the seed it's trying to plant. Their books are third and fourth out; Act 2 is early enough. Logged in `CANON_LEDGER.md` §6.
3. **POV distribution.** Lucian carries Silas, Cray, and Tobias's first two — the institutional threads, seen by the man who runs the institution. Selene carries Rémy, Isabeau, Delphine, and Tobias's last — the people who don't fit their rooms, seen by the one person who never has. Every seed's POV letter matches the §1 map; verified chapter by chapter.
4. **Nobody comments on a seed.** No character remarks that Silas is fond, that Isabeau is odd, or that Delphine is kind. A commented seed is an ad.
5. **Density:** 24 inserts across 36 chapters. No chapter carries more than two, and no seed exceeds eight lines.
6. **The absence holds.** Zero characters ask why neither species leaves. One utterance of *Verity*.
