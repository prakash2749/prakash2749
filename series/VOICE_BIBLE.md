# Voice Bible — *The Blood Code*

Eleven narrators across five books. The number-one AI tell in dual POV is both narrators sounding identical, so this file exists to make that failure detectable instead of a matter of taste.

**Effort is allocated honestly.** Book 1's three narrators get full treatment with sample prose, because Book 1 is what gets drafted next. The eight narrators of Books 2–5 get system, tic, one sample line, and their specific drift risk — enough to seed correctly in Book 1, and they get promoted to full treatment when their book goes to chapter level.

---

## The swap test

Take any narration paragraph. Put another narrator's name on it. **If it still reads fine, the voice has collapsed** and that passage gets a metaphor pass.

Every sample in this file has been swap-tested. §5 shows the test running, including what failure looks like.

---

## Rules that bind every narrator

- First person, present tense.
- Narration mean 8–14 words. Dialogue mean ~6, median 5 — a separate, tighter target.
- Roughly half of all paragraphs are a single sentence.
- The base rhythm unit is **a full sentence followed by one short fragment**, about fourteen times a chapter. Not two fragments. Not three.
- Fragments go on their own line. The white space carries as much as the words.
- No narrator measures anything. No distances, no counts of steps, no seconds. Numbers are vagueness devices — *a second*, *a minute*, *a step* — or they're about money, age, deadlines, and body counts.
- **Banned as metaphor for all eleven:** *arithmetic*, *math*, *load-bearing*, *file/filed/filing* as a cognitive verb, *pricing* as a way of assessing people.
- No narrator accurately diagnoses their own psychology. Dark romance leads misread themselves.
- Never name the feeling right after showing it. "My hands shook. I was terrified." Cut the second sentence, always.
- No retrospective narrator. No "I found out later," no "little did I know." Stay inside the scene.
- A verbal tic belongs to exactly one person. If the narrator has a habit, nobody else gets it.

# 1. LUCIAN CROIX — Book 1

**412 · vampire · thresholds and doors**

## The system

Permission. Who gets let in, what should have been locked, what a room's exits say about the man who chose it.

**This is not spatial measurement.** He does not count paces or clock distances. He notices *access* — who has a key, which door was left open, what somebody had to walk past to be standing here. A four-hundred-year-old who has survived by controlling entry, now standing beside the man who walked through the last door his father left open.

The system is the theme. His arc is learning to look at the doorway instead of the wolves outside it.

**Sentence targets:** narration mean 10–12. He is the colder of the two leads and his sentences are shorter than Selene's. Dialogue mean 5 — he is the most clipped speaker in the book.

## Samples

**Observational.**

> The door to my father's study has been unlocked since Tuesday.
>
> Someone has been in here. Not searching. Visiting. The decanter is a finger down and the chair faces the window instead of the desk.
>
> My father never sat facing the window.
>
> Whoever sits in it thinks of himself as welcome.

**Under pressure.**

> The wolf's throat is in my hand and I have not decided anything yet.
>
> That should trouble me more than it does.
>
> "Say it again," I tell him.

**Tender** — anchored, because tender is where voices drift.

> She sleeps with the bedroom door open.
>
> Four years of waiting for someone to come through it and she still won't shut it. I stand in the hall where she can't see me and understand that she left it open for me. She doesn't know she did that.
>
> I'm not going to tell her.

## Only Lucian says

- *"Say it again."* — his tic. Control through repetition. Nobody else gets it.
- *"ghost"* — **the brand.** Coined Ch. 11: she's the woman the whole city looks through, and the blank he tried to make of her and couldn't. Threat, filth and tenderness all run through the one word. **Dialogue only**, 40–60 per book, and no narrator ever uses *ghost* for anything else.
- *"good girl"* — praise, sex only, never outside a bed or a wall.
- *"my witness"* — loaded and rare (≤6). The only person he failed to erase. Threat, then possession, then literal.
- *"chère"* — retired to ≤3 flavor uses.
- Dismissals built on access: *"He has a key. That's the entire problem."*

## Lucian never says

- Any distance, any count, any duration in numbers.
- A therapy word. No *boundaries*, no *processing*, no *trauma*.
- His own emotional state named accurately.
- An extended metaphor. One image, then he moves.
- A sentence needing two commas to stand up.

## Drift risks

**Over-articulation.** He is intelligent, which tempts a drafter into letting him explain himself. He observes precisely and concludes wrongly. That gap is the character.

**Doors becoming a floor plan.** If he starts noting *how far* the door is, the system has become a rangefinder. Permission, never geometry.

**Him sounding like Cassian.** Both are old, both are courteous, both are dangerous. Separation: Cassian is warm and Lucian is still. Cassian asks about your mother; Lucian says nothing and waits for you to fill it.

# 2. SELENE WARD — Book 1

**25 · hybrid · translation**

## The system

Two languages, native in neither. She hears what was said and what it would mean in the other register, and converts constantly while nobody in the room knows a conversion is happening.

**Semantic, never numeric.** *He said "family." In pack, that word has a fence around it.*

**Sentence targets:** narration mean 11–13, slightly longer than Lucian's — the doubling takes an extra beat. Dialogue mean 6, and she is the one character who occasionally runs long out loud, because correcting a translation takes words.

## Samples

**Observational.**

> He says *arrangement* like it's a piece of furniture.
>
> There's no word for it in wolf. You're in, or you're outside, and outside has teeth. He thinks he's offering me a job.
>
> He's offering me a fence.

**Under pressure.**

> "Family," he says.
>
> That's not what he said. In pack, *family* is a gate, and I have been on the wrong side of it since I was born.
>
> "Say that again in wolf," I tell him.
>
> He can't. There isn't a version of it that includes me.

**Tender** — her anchor is the cessation of translating.

> I don't convert it. I don't check it against the other version first.
>
> "Stay."
>
> It comes out in one language and it's mine.

## Only Selene says

- *"In wolf, that means—"* / *"That's not what you said."* — her tic. She corrects translations out loud.
- **"Lucian"**, flatly. From her, using his name without the title is intimacy.
- **"Your Majesty."** Weaponized courtesy, anger only.
- She invents no pet names. That's characterization, not an oversight.

## Selene never says

- Anything measured. Not social counts either — no *nineteen people told me*. Enumeration in her POV is the exact voice collapse to watch for.
- A word about her own resilience. She doesn't know she's brave.
- Her feelings named on the beat after they're shown.
- *Half.* Other people call her that. She doesn't think it.

## Drift risks

**Enumeration.** Her narration must never start counting. If both leads quantify — his spatial, her social — the voices have collapsed even though the units differ. Varying the units is not varying the voice.

**Competence porn.** She is not a fighter and cannot shift. Her power is that she understands two rooms at once.

**Self-pity.** She has been alone her whole life and narrates it as ordinary, because to her it is.

# 3. CASSIAN VALE — Book 1, 5 interstitials

**690 · vampire · inheritance and what is owed**

## The system

Lineage, debt, obligations that pass down whether you signed for them. What he was left, what he is owed, what he intends to leave behind.

**He never calculates.** Not once. The moment he weighs anything numerically he becomes an accountant instead of a grieving father.

**Sentence targets:** narration mean 12–14 — the longest in the book, because he is the oldest and the most formal. Still nothing over about twenty. Dialogue mean 7, courteous and complete.

## Samples

**Observational.**

> The boy has his father's hands.
>
> I taught those hands to hold a blade. Aurelian was busy being a king, so it was me in the courtyard, every evening, for eleven years. Nobody has ever thanked me for it and I have never once asked.
>
> A man leaves his son a city and a philosophy.
>
> One of them is furniture.

**Choosing** — what his interstitials do. Never explaining.

> I could tell him tonight.
>
> He's sitting where his father sat and he's asking the right question, finally, and I could hand him the whole of it.
>
> I ask about the Marchand woman's health instead.
>
> He answers. He trusts me, so he answers, and the door closes without either of us touching it.

**The last interstitial, before the climax.**

> He knows.
>
> Not all of it. Enough. He has stopped telling me where he's going, and a boy who stops telling you where he's going has already decided something.
>
> I raised him better than his father did.
>
> He'll do it properly.

## Only Cassian says

- **"boy"** — for Lucian. Affectionate, possessive, unbearable on reread. His alone.
- *"How is your mother's house?"* — his tic. Answers questions with a question about your family. Deflection dressed as care.
- Everyone else's full name and correct title, always. Scrupulous courtesy as distance.
- *"Your father—"* as the opening of every manipulation, and every one of them true.

## Cassian never says

- His ideology, in narration. Ever. The reader assembles his politics from what he does.
- Any number. Not sums, not spans, not counts.
- Juliette's name. **Five interstitials and he never says it once.** That absence is the character.
- A sentence about strategy. A villain who explains his own plan is a professor.

## Hard drafting rules

- **600–900 words each.** Brevity is the menace.
- Placed immediately before he manipulates Lucian, so the reader knows what's coming and Lucian doesn't.
- He shows himself **choosing**. Five times he could tell the truth. Five times he doesn't.
- His warmth is not an act. He genuinely remembers your mother's name. This is what makes him unbearable.
- His critique of the Code carries real weight. A villain whose argument is stupid isn't frightening.
- He is 690 and loses a straight fight to Lucian at 412. He was never the threat. He was the threat because he was standing close.
- **Final line carries both halves** — that he meant it, and that he wasn't first. The only utterance of *Verity* in Book 1.

## Drift risks

**The professor.** The single biggest failure mode in the book. If an interstitial explains a strategy, cut it and rewrite it as a choice.

**Villainous relish.** He is not enjoying this. He is a father who has been right for four hundred years and needs to keep being right.

**Sounding like Lucian.** Both old, both courteous. Cassian is warm and asks questions. Lucian is still and waits.

# 4. BOOKS 2–5 — compact entries

Promoted to full treatment when each book goes to chapter level. What's here is enough to seed them correctly in Book 1.

### Silas Roche — Book 2 hero · 231 · vampire
**System: sign and trail.** What passed through, how long ago, whether it was carrying something. Reads rooms like ground — direction and intent, never distance.
**Tic:** does not finish sentences about himself. Starts, stops, changes subject.
> Somebody stood here a while and didn't want to. Weight on one foot, then the other. I was—
>
> It doesn't matter what I was.

**Drift risk:** becoming a forensics readout. He reads intention, not evidence.
**Full entry (Book 2 at chapter level, 2026-09-26):**
- **Register:** economy. The fewest words that will do. Narration runs slightly longer than his speech — he thinks in trails, speaks in verdicts.
- **Looks at the window** when he's hiding something. Lucian has learned the tell; the reader should too.
- **Brand pet name: *witch*** — to Ondine, in dialogue only, 40–60 uses across the book, from Ch. 1. Never in narration as an endearment. Menace early, tenderness late, the same word.
- **Profanity:** menacing weighting — *fuck*, *bastard* — ~300–420/100k. Flat between sex and plot chapters.
- **The unfinished sentence** is his; nobody else gets it. He finishes exactly one sentence about himself in the whole book, in Ch. 33 (*"I was a dog. I'm not yours."*) — earn it, don't spend it early. Ch. 18's honest conversation lets him get *most* of the way.
- **Never:** measuring distance, reciting dates, explaining his past unprompted, saying *I love you* before Ch. 34.

### Ondine Marchand — Book 2 heroine · 41 · witch
**System: substitution.** What can stand in for what. What spoils. What has no replacement. A cook's mind, warm with it.
**Tic:** *"That'll cost."* **Spoken only — never in narration.**
> You can use salt for bone if you have to. Bone for hair, at a pinch, if the hair was willingly given.
>
> There's nothing you can use for a name.

**Drift risk:** *pricing* leaking into her interiority. Her system is substitution; the transactional register lives in her mouth and stays out of her head. A drafter who imports pricing into her narration has broken the rule.
**Full entry (Book 2 at chapter level, 2026-09-26):**
- **Register:** warm, dry, funny under pressure, a cook's specificity. She notices what spoils and what keeps. Kitchen verbs (*render, reduce, cut, proof, catch*) are hers.
- **Burns rosemary when she's worried**; the house smells like her mood. Named smells in every chapter.
- **Profanity:** funny-modern weighting — *shit*, blasphemy, *fuck* as standalone expletive — ~550–750/100k.
- **What she calls him:** *Silas*. *Silas Roche* when she's angry. No pet name — the asymmetry is the point until the epilogue.
- **Her FMC life:** she runs a house, keeps a ledger, has clients, and shapes the plot — the substitution in Act 3 is her plan, not his.
- **Never:** pricing people in narration, throwing magic like a weapon, being rescued without having prepared the room first.

### Absalom Cray — Book 2 antagonist interstitials · claims 300 · vampire
**System: introductions.** Who can be brought to whom, what a person will give for the next door to open, what they already owe without knowing it. He sees people as rooms he could introduce them to.
**Tic:** the right form, one register off. Overpays. Brings a gift to every threat.
> I have never sold anyone anything. I introduce. A man meets the thing he wanted, and afterward he remembers me fondly, or not at all.
**Interstitial rules (five, 600–900 words each):** never names the client or gives it a pronoun · never swears · never uses a number · shows himself *choosing*, never explaining · no strategy monologue · courteous to the end. Collision check: not Cassian's *inheritance* (Cray owes nothing and inherits nothing), not Severine's *testimony*. **Banned: *pricing*.** He never states what anything costs — he describes what it opens.

### Rémy Thibodeaux — Book 3 hero · 31 · wolf
**System: water and draft.** What floats, what's aground, what the tide is doing under a still surface.
**Tic:** *"Yeah, no."* Bayou verbal shrug.
> Looks calm. It's running out under there and everything on it is going the same direction whether it picked one or not.

**Drift risk:** self-flagellation. He is not asking to be forgiven. He killed two people and he says so plainly, which is the only honest thing he has.

### Isabeau Vale — Book 3 heroine · 214 · vampire
**System: tuning.** What's in tune, what's flat, who is playing too loud for the room.
**Tic:** over-formal courtesy as a blade. *"If you'd be so kind."*
> Everyone in this room is a half-step under and nobody will say it.
>
> My father is the only one in tune. He has always been the only one in tune. That is not the compliment it sounds like.

**Drift risk:** nothing in Book 1. She appears three times, silent, and never explains a look.

### Tobias Vale — Book 4 hero · 158 · vampire
**System: repair** — what's cracked, what was mended badly, what will hold under weight.
**Ledger item O.01:** his placeholder system ("precedent and filing") violated the banned-metaphor rule. *Repair* replaces it. Resolved.
**Tic:** to be assigned in Phase D.

### Severine Aldric — Book 4 heroine · 611 · vampire
**System: testimony** — what counts as evidence, what a room would actually believe.
**Drift risk:** she and Tobias are both institutional minds. Separation: he mends, she judges.

### Delphine Thibodeaux — Book 5 heroine · 24 in Book 1 · wolf
**System: scent and weather** — what's coming, what it smells like before it arrives.
**Drift risk:** must not overlap Silas's trail-reading. He reads what already passed; she reads what hasn't arrived.

### Elias Roux — Book 5 hero · 33 · the second hybrid
**System: mirrors and halves.**
**Drift risk:** he cannot share Selene's translation system. Both are hybrids and that's exactly why their voices must not rhyme. He was raised human and told nothing, so he has no second language to convert into — he has a reflection and no original.

# 5. The swap test, running

## Pass — Lucian's paragraph with Selene's name on it

> *Selene:* The door to my father's study has been unlocked since Tuesday. Someone has been in here. Not searching. Visiting.

**Fails correctly.** Selene has no study, no father's house, and no interest in who holds keys. Her version of this observation would convert what the intrusion *means* in two registers. The paragraph cannot be worn by anyone else. ✅

## Pass — Selene's paragraph with Lucian's name on it

> *Lucian:* He says *arrangement* like it's a piece of furniture. There's no word for it in wolf.

**Fails correctly.** Lucian doesn't speak wolf and would never check a word against another register. He'd notice what the offer gives the man access to. ✅

## Pass — Cassian's paragraph with Lucian's name on it

> *Lucian:* I taught those hands to hold a blade. Nobody has ever thanked me for it.

**Fails correctly.** Lucian is owed nothing and is keeping no ledger of unthanked service. ✅

## Failure — what collapse looks like

> *Either of them:* I watch him cross the room and I know exactly what he's going to say before he says it. Something cold settles in my chest.

Wearable by any of the eleven. No system, a named feeling, and a body beat doing the work a voice should do.

**The fix is not more description.** It's one metaphor from that narrator's system:

> *Lucian:* He crosses the room like a man who's never been asked to wait in a hall.
>
> *Selene:* He crosses the room and says the polite version. There's a rougher one underneath and we both speak it.
>
> *Cassian:* He crosses the room the way his father did. I gave him that, and I have never said so.

# 6. Per-chapter check

Before drafting, read that narrator's entry. After drafting, run these:

1. **Swap test** on two random narration paragraphs. Both must fail.
2. **Tic audit** — did anyone else use this narrator's tic? Did the narrator use someone else's?
3. **Number census** — any measurement? Any counting, spatial *or* social?
4. **Banned metaphors** — *arithmetic*, *math*, *load-bearing*, *file* as a verb, *pricing*.
5. **Feeling-naming** — any sentence naming an emotion immediately after showing it? Cut it.
6. **Tender passages** — one anchor metaphor per page from that narrator's system. This is the highest-risk zone in the book.
7. **Cassian only** — does the interstitial explain anything? If yes, rewrite it as a choice.
