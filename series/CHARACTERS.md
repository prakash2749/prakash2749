# Characters — *The Blood Code*

Every character in a sexual situation anywhere in this series is an adult, and their age is stated in their sheet. No exceptions, no vagueness.

**Sheet format:** age · species · look · work · wound · want vs need · cognitive metaphor system · address terms · verbal tic · book.

**Canon note — vampires reproduce.** The source bible calls Lucian "the King's son" and Juliette "Cassian's daughter," so vampire birth is canon by implication. Locking it with a constraint that pays off politically: vampire conception is rare and difficult, and a born heir is the most valuable thing a House can have. This is why Lucian's claim is unquestioned, why Juliette's execution gutted Cassian beyond ordinary grief, and why Isabeau matters. Turning exists but is separate, deliberate, and not something anyone in Books 1–3 does. Logged in `CANON_LEDGER.md` as an inference.

# BOOK 1 LEADS

## Lucian Croix

**412** · vampire, Apex-class · reads mid-to-late thirties · heir to House Croix, now King of New Orleans · **Book 1 hero**

Tall, black-haired, built like someone who has never once needed to hurry. Dresses expensively and without vanity. The unsettling thing isn't his face, it's his stillness — he doesn't shift his weight, doesn't fidget, doesn't blink on a human schedule. People fill the silence for him. He lets them.

**Wound:** he spent four centuries telling his father that mercy was a weakness, and his father died with his guard down. He believes his own argument killed the man who refused it.

**Want:** to find who killed Aurelian and take the city.
**Need:** to learn that the answer to being betrayed by someone you let in is not to stop letting anyone in. His father's philosophy was correct; his father's *judgment* about one man was wrong. Lucian has to separate those, and he has all book to fail at it.

**Cognitive system — thresholds and doors.** Who gets let in. What should have been locked. What a room's exits say about the man who chose it. This is not spatial measurement — no distances, no counts of steps. It's *permission*. He walks into his own court and thinks about who has a key. The system is thematically exact: he is standing beside the man who walked through the last door his father left open, and he will spend the book learning to look at the doorway instead of the wolves outside it.

Sample register: *Someone left this unlocked. Someone always leaves it unlocked, and then acts surprised at what comes in.*

**Address terms** (three-term rule, each doing one job — revised 2026-09-25, see `CANON_LEDGER.md` change log):
- Default and **brand**: **"ghost"** — first said in Ch. 8 as a dismissal (*ghosts of an evening*, the ones who never remember him), answered in Ch. 11 (*"The ghost that stayed"*). Everyone in this city looks through her, and he is the one man who can't stop seeing her. It's also the thing he tried to make her, a blank where a night should be, and she refused to become it. It works as threat, filth and tenderness in the same breath. **Dialogue only, never in anyone's narration**, 40–60 uses in Book 1. The word belongs to her.
- Sex only: **"good girl"** — praise, reserved, never used outside a bed or a wall.
- Loaded: **"my witness"** — she is the only person in four centuries he failed to erase. Rare (≤6 in Book 1). It starts as a threat, turns into possession, and pays off when she becomes the literal witness against Cassian. Nobody else ever gets called this.
- **"chère"** retired to flavor: ≤3 uses, and only where he's being Louisiana at somebody.

**Verbal tic:** *"Say it again."* Control through repetition. Nobody else in the series gets this.

**Feral moment** (mandatory, exactly once — Ch. 31): the arithmetic doesn't break, the *door* does. He stops asking who let them in and simply removes a room full of people. The return to control afterward is colder than the loss of it. He is not sorry. He wanted to keep going. See `books/book-1-king-of-teeth/OUTLINE.md`.

## Selene Ward

**25** · half human, half wolf · reads entirely human · bartender at a Frenchmen Street club, and before that a translator's assistant · **Book 1 heroine**

Small, fast, and impossible to intimidate in a way that reads as either courage or damage depending on the day. Dark hair, her mother's eyes, her father's plain American surname. Dresses like someone who might need to leave through a window.

**Wound:** the Alpha's son killed her father and her mother, and she has spent four years building a life entirely out of the intention to kill him back. Underneath that: she has never once been fully at home in a room. Wolves smell something wrong on her. Humans notice nothing and that's worse.

**Want:** Rémy Thibodeaux dead.
**Need:** to survive learning that her revenge was aimed at another of Cassian's instruments — and to find out who she is when the thing she organized her life around is taken from her. **She does not get the kill.** That is the book's cruelest turn and it's the one that earns her Book 3's presence.

**Cognitive system — translation.** Two languages, native in neither. She hears what people say and what it would mean in the other register. *He said "family." In pack, that word has a fence around it.* She is constantly converting, and the exhausting part is that nobody else in the room knows a conversion is happening. Never numeric, never measured — it's semantic. Her tender-scene anchor is the moment she stops translating and just says the thing.

**Address terms:**
- For Lucian, default: his name, flatly, which from her is intimacy — nobody else uses it without the title.
- Angry: **"Your Majesty."** Weaponized courtesy.
- She does not invent pet names. That's characterization, not an oversight.

**Mouth:** she's a bartender on Frenchmen Street and swears like one. Funny and modern: *fuck*, *shit*, blasphemy, ~550–750 per 100k. Her narration is dirty and her dialogue dirtier. She does not say *damn* as a crutch. Lucian swears less and meaner (*fuck*, *bastard*, ~300–420 per 100k). Cassian never swears, and that's part of his menace.

**Verbal tic:** *"In wolf, that means—"* / *"That's not what you said."* She corrects translations out loud. Only her. Ceiling: *In wolf* ≤15 in a book, or the tic eats the device.

**Cannot shift. Ever.** This is a permanent wound and no later book gives it to her as a power-up. Her arc is being both things, not becoming one.

## Cassian Vale

**690** · vampire, Elder · reads fifties, deliberately · the King's oldest friend; Lucian's uncle in everything but blood · **Book 1 antagonist. Dies Ch. 34.**

Silver at the temples because he chose to stop looking young around the time it started reading as authority. Warm. Genuinely warm — this is the point. He remembers your mother's name. He asks about your knee. The reader watches him murder a man in the prologue and then spends thirty chapters watching him be kind, and the kindness is not an act.

**Wound:** Aurelian executed Juliette. Drained her and took her head, personally, in front of witnesses, because the law does not bend for the powerful. Cassian begged. Aurelian said no and meant it as love.

**The layer under the wound — this is the whole character.** Cassian was a quiet Code-skeptic for three centuries before Juliette was born. He raised both daughters inside that contempt. Juliette killed a human woman for pleasure because she had been taught, at home, patiently, that the Code was a leash invented to make predators apologize for teeth. **He built the thing that killed her.** He has never once let himself look at that directly, and every year he doesn't look at it he needs Aurelian to be guiltier. That's why sixty years of grief turned into four hundred years' worth of ideology — the ideology was already there, waiting for a reason.

**Want:** to break Aurelian's philosophy, restart the war, and forge Lucian into the king the species needs.
**Hidden want:** for someone to tell him he was right, so he never has to ask what he taught his daughter. Something has been telling him exactly that for four hundred years.

**Cognitive system — inheritance and what is owed.** Not numbers. Lineage, debt, the obligations that pass down whether you signed for them. *A man leaves his son a city and a philosophy. One of them is furniture.* He thinks about what he was left, what he is owed, and what he intends to leave behind. He never once calculates.

**Address terms:**
- For Lucian: **"boy."** Affectionate, possessive, and unbearable on reread. Exclusive to Cassian.
- For everyone else: their full name and correct title, always. He is scrupulously courteous, which is a kind of distance.

**Verbal tic:** he answers questions with a question about your family. *"How is your mother's house?"* Deflection dressed as care.

**Drafting rules, non-negotiable:**
- His interstitials show him **choosing**, never explaining. No strategy monologue, no ideology stated in narration. The reader assembles his politics from what he does.
- 600–900 words each. Brevity is the menace.
- He is 690 and Lucian is 412 and **Cassian loses a straight fight.** Establish this early and honestly. He was never the threat. He was the threat because he was standing close.
- His critique of the Blood Code has real weight. A villain whose argument is stupid isn't frightening.
- Final line carries both halves: he meant it, *and* he wasn't first. The only utterance of the name **Verity** in Book 1.

# THE DEAD AND THE PAST

## Aurelian Croix

**died at roughly 1,100** · vampire, Apex · Lucian's father · King of New Orleans for three centuries

Never appears alive except in the prologue and in memory. Has to be the most likable person in the series or the whole engine fails.

He enjoyed humans. Not as food — as company. He kept a standing Tuesday table at a restaurant where nobody knew what he was and the waiter called him Aury. He believed power's only justification is what it declines to do.

**Last words:** *"You kept it."* Four hundred years of trust, reduced to a fact about a jar.

**Function:** the moral center, permanently offstage. Every character is arguing with a dead man. Lucian's arc is realizing his father was right about everything except one friend.

## Juliette Vale

**died at 78** · vampire · Cassian's younger daughter · executed roughly 60 years before Book 1

She killed a human woman for the pleasure of it, was caught, and was tried under the Code. She was not framed, not manipulated, and not sorry. **Do not soften her.** If Juliette is innocent, Aurelian is a monster and Cassian is right, and the book collapses. She did it, she meant it, and her father still had to watch her die.

## Daniel Ward · Marisol Boucher

**human, 44 at death · wolf, 39 at death** · Selene's parents · killed four years before Book 1

Daniel was a court translator who did work for Aurelian and became something like a friend. Marisol left the Thibodeaux pack to be with him, which pack custom treats as contamination.

The official story is that Rémy killed them over the relationship. The real reason: Daniel had learned Rémy was meeting a vampire from the King's inner circle, and said so to the wrong person. **The clue he left** — a bound ledger of translation jobs, in which one recurring client is recorded only by the house-mark Cassian uses on private correspondence. Selene has had it for four years and thought it was her father's paperwork.

# BOOK 2 LEADS — *Vow of Poison*

## Silas Roche

**231** · vampire, Elder · reads early forties · Lucian's enforcer and tracker · **Book 2 hero**

Scarred hands, short hair, speaks in the fewest words that will do. Where Lucian is stillness, Silas is economy. He was a slave catcher's dog for the first decade after he was turned and it is the thing he will not discuss; Aurelian bought him out of that arrangement and Silas has never once said thank you.

**Cognitive system — sign and trail.** What passed through here, how long ago, whether it was carrying something. Reads rooms like ground. Never measures distance — reads *direction and intent*.

**Verbal tic:** he does not finish sentences about himself. Starts, stops, changes subject. Only him.

**Book 1 function:** present in roughly eight chapters as Lucian's right hand. Gets 4–6 charged moments with Ondine starting in Act 1 — a jaw going soft, a refusal to search her house, the fact that he keeps going back with questions he already knows the answers to.

## Ondine Marchand

**41** · human witch, ages slowly · reads early thirties · head of the Marchand line · **Book 2 heroine**

The strongest witch in New Orleans because her line has been collecting for two hundred years, not because she was born brighter. Runs her grandmother's house as neutral ground and keeps a kitchen that smells like a working. Tired in a specific way: she inherited obligations she never agreed to.

**Wound:** she brewed the poison that nearly killed Lucian to service a debt her grandmother signed, and she deliberately did not ask who the end client was. That habit — not knowing on purpose — is what Book 2 breaks her on.

**Cognitive system — substitution.** What can stand in for what. What spoils. What has no replacement. A cook's mind, and warm with it. *You can use salt for bone if you have to. You cannot use anything for a name.*

**Verbal tic:** *"That'll cost."* Spoken, always — **never in narration.** Her cognitive system is substitution, not pricing; the transactional register lives in her mouth and stays out of her head. A drafter who imports "pricing" into her interiority has broken the rule in `STYLE_GUIDE.md`.

## Absalom Cray

**age unknown, claims 300** · vampire · broker · **Book 2 antagonist**

Sells introductions, not goods. He brokered Ondine's poison to Cassian and holds the paper on her grandmother's debt. Appears once in Book 1 — a name on a ledger, a man at a bar, no more than four lines — so his Book 2 arrival is a payoff and not an introduction.

# BOOK 3 LEADS — *Son of Wolves*

## Rémy Thibodeaux

**31** · wolf · Gaspard's son and heir · shrimp boat captain by day, genuinely · **Book 3 hero. Survives Book 1.**

The hard sell in this series and the reason it works. In Book 1 he is the man who killed Selene's parents and the target of her revenge. He did kill them. He was also convinced by Cassian that Daniel Ward was a vampire informant whose report would get Gaspard assassinated, and he has spent four years understanding what he actually did.

Big, sunburned, quiet in a way people mistake for slow. Arm permanently weakened from where Aurelian injured it.

**Cognitive system — water and draft.** What floats, what's aground, what the tide is doing under a still surface. Barataria in his head.

**Verbal tic:** *"Yeah, no."* Bayou verbal shrug. His alone.

**Book 1 arc:** hunted through the middle of the book, cornered in Ch. 28, and he does not defend himself. What he says instead is what takes Selene's revenge away from her. He ends Book 1 alive, exiled by his own father, and owing a debt he cannot pay.

**Book 3 requires him to earn a redemption he does not deserve,** with Selene alive and in the room, and it has to cost. If it's easy, the series has cheated Book 1's heroine.

## Isabeau Vale

**214** · vampire · reads late twenties · Cassian's elder daughter · **Book 3 heroine**

Juliette's older sister by about seventy-five years. She was roughly a hundred and fifty when Juliette was executed — old enough to know her sister was guilty, and to say so out loud, which ended her relationship with her father permanently. Cassian never forgave her for agreeing with the King.

**Why she matters structurally:** she was raised in the same house, by the same man, inside the same contempt for the Code that produced Juliette. And she came out of it knowing her sister deserved to die. Isabeau is the proof that what Cassian taught his daughters was not destiny — one of them chose it and one of them didn't. That is the fact Cassian cannot look at, and it is why he can be warm to every person in the city except this one.

Court-raised, over-educated, plays piano well enough that people assume it's her whole personality. Her father's killer is Lucian; her sister's killer was Lucian's father. She is standing in the middle of the worst possible family.

**Cognitive system — tuning.** What's in tune, what's flat, who is playing too loud for the room. Distinct from anything else in the cast.

**Verbal tic:** over-formal courtesy as a blade. *"If you'd be so kind."* Hers only.

**Book 1 function:** three appearances. She is the only person who looks at her father with anything other than trust, and she never says why. A reader on reread will see she knew.

# BOOK 4 AND 5 — LEAD SLOTS

Per the plan, these open once the B1–3 cast exists. It now does, so here are the candidates. Locked in `SERIES_BIBLE.md`.

**Book 4 — *Heir of Ash*** · continental scope, the vampire Order beyond New Orleans.
- **Recommended:** **Isabeau's cousin Tobias Vale** (vampire, 158, Book 1 minor) × **the Order's envoy sent to judge Lucian's kingship**. Enemies-to-lovers with a woman whose entire job is to find his House guilty. Puts a Vale on the hero side, which does real work after Book 3.
- Alternative: Silas's tracker protégé × a wolf from an out-of-state pack.

**Book 5 — *God of Blood*** · origin mythology, Verity.
- **Recommended:** **Rémy's younger sister Delphine Thibodeaux** (wolf, 24 in Book 1) × **the second hybrid**, found in Book 4 and the only other person alive who is both halves. Puts Selene's condition at the center of the finale without making Book 5 a Lucian–Selene retread.
- Alternative: Ondine's apprentice × a vampire born after the truce.
