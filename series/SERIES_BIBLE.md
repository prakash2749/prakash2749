# Series Bible — *The Blood Code*

Five books. Five couples. One question.

Built on `reference/source-bible-v1.md`, which wins any conflict unless `CANON_LEDGER.md` records a dated override.

---

## 1. Identity

**Series:** *The Blood Code*
**Genre:** dark paranormal romance · urban fantasy · supernatural political thriller
**Tense and POV:** first person, present tense, alternating
**Setting:** New Orleans, present day
**Per book:** 95,000–115,000 words · own antagonist · own HEA · 10–13 explicit scenes
**Heat ceiling:** Kent-filthy. Crude vocabulary, explicit dirty talk, degradation, edge play.

**Reader promise:** you will be given the murderer in the prologue, and then you will watch the hero thank him.

**The moral engine:** a law that says *take what you require, never take the life that carries it* — and a man who enforced it against his best friend's child, and died for it.

---

## 2. The through-line

> **Cassian was a hand. Whose?**

Book 1 answers *Cassian killed the King*. The remaining four climb toward what put the idea in his mouth four hundred years before he acted on it.

**The disclosure schedule.** What the reader knows, and when. Nothing arrives early.

| Book | What the reader gets | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The **name** — once, in Cassian's dying line. Reads as a common noun on first pass. | A word |
| 2 | The **pattern** — someone has been buying in this city under different names for centuries, and what they buy is always grief. | A ledger |
| 3 | The **place** — both species' oldest customs orient toward the same ground, and neither knows it. | A geography |
| 4 | The **institution** — the Conclave has always known, which is why it has never once let New Orleans be abandoned. | A verdict |
| 5 | The **thing** — Verity. Named, awake, and the origin of both species. | A body |

Book 1 plants this as an **absence, not a hint**. No character asks why neither species will leave. Nobody has ever asked.

---

## 3. The rule that cannot break

**Cassian chose.**

Verity did not create his grief, install it, or puppet him. Aurelian executed Juliette, Juliette was guilty, and Cassian never forgave him. That is real, it is his, and it is sufficient motive on its own.

What Verity did was **find** it, four hundred years ago, and agree with him patiently until agreement became ideology.

If any later book reduces Cassian to a puppet, Book 1's climax stops meaning anything on reread and the series has cheated its own best scene. His last line carries both halves: *he meant it,* **and** *he wasn't first.*

---

# BOOK 1 — *King of Teeth*

**Couple:** Lucian Croix (412, vampire) × Selene Ward (25, hybrid)
**Narrators:** Lucian · Selene · Cassian (5 interstitials, 600–900 words each)
**Budget:** ~95,000 · prologue + 36 chapters + 5 interstitials + epilogue
**Heat:** 11 scenes, first at Ch. 8
**Antagonist:** Cassian Vale, dies Ch. 34

**Premise.** The strongest vampire alive is murdered by his oldest friend during a wolf attack that friend engineered. The wolves take the blame. The son comes home to burn Barataria down — and the only person who can prove he's aiming at the wrong target is a half-wolf bartender he fed from and failed to erase.

### Act structure

**Act 1 (Prologue–Ch. 11).** Cassian kills Aurelian and walks away grieving honestly. Lucian arrives at the funeral, takes the city, and retaliates into wolf territory. He captures Thibault, who tells him the wolves didn't land the fatal blow. Destabilized, he goes to a club, feeds from Selene, and cannot erase her. Poisoned wine, a wolf attack, and he wakes in her house. They make the pact: his help reaching Rémy, her blood freely given.

**Act 2 (Ch. 12–28).** The investigation. Lucian re-examines his father's wounds and confirms one heart wound stopped regeneration — which means an insider. He tells Cassian nothing, and Cassian notices. Selene's father's ledger surfaces: a recurring client recorded only by a house-mark. Two attempts on Selene's life. The hunt for Rémy, closing at Ch. 28 — where Rémy doesn't defend himself, and what he says instead takes Selene's revenge away from her.

**Act 3 (Ch. 29–36).** Cassian moves to force a full war before the evidence lands. Bastien dies in Ch. 31 and Lucian's control breaks — once, completely. The ledger's house-mark matches. Ch. 34: Lucian kills Cassian, and Cassian's last line is the only thing in the book Lucian cannot make sense of. Aftermath, crowning, a city that now has a king who understands what his father was actually saying.

### What Selene gets instead of her kill

Rémy is guilty and he is not the author. He killed her parents believing Daniel Ward was a vampire informant whose report would get Gaspard assassinated — because Cassian told him so. Selene spent four years building a life out of an intention, and the intention turns out to have been someone else's tool.

**She does not kill him.** She chooses not to, in front of him, and it costs her everything she had instead of a self. That's the book's cruelest turn and it's what earns her presence in Book 3.

### Seeds planted

| Seed | Where | Pays off |
|---|---|---|
| Silas × Ondine — 5 charged moments | Ch. 6, 14, 19, 25, 33 | Book 2 |
| Absalom Cray — a name on a ledger, four lines at a bar | Ch. 22 | Book 2 |
| Rémy survives, exiled, owing a debt | Ch. 28, 36 | Book 3 |
| Isabeau looks at her father wrong, three times, never says why | Ch. 9, 20, 35 | Book 3 |
| Tobias Vale, quietly competent, three administrative scenes | Ch. 13, 24, 36 | Book 4 |
| Delphine Thibodeaux, the pack's conscience, four appearances | Ch. 10, 18, 28, 36 | Book 5 |
| Nobody asks why neither species leaves | everywhere, as absence | Book 5 |
| **"Verity"** — Cassian's last word | Ch. 34 | Books 2–5 |

### Ending state

Lucian is King and understands his father was right about everything except one friend. Selene has no revenge and, for the first time, a room she's actually in. HEA.

**Final paragraph hook:** Silas puts a page in front of Lucian. The poison was paid for through a broker named Cray, and the account behind Cray has been buying in this city since before Lucian was born.

# BOOK 2 — *Vow of Poison*

**Couple:** Silas Roche (231, vampire) × Ondine Marchand (41, witch)
**Antagonist:** Absalom Cray
**Heat:** 11 scenes · first by Ch. 3 (~6%) · ~30% hot units (matches Book 1, LO 2026-09-26)
**POV:** Silas / Ondine alternating (clusters allowed) + five Absalom Cray interstitials
**Brand pet name:** *witch* — Silas to Ondine, dialogue only, 40–60 uses from Ch. 1
**Trope stack:** forced proximity · he-falls-first · touch-her-and-die · morally gray enforcer × the woman who nearly killed his king

**Premise (revised 2026-09-26 against the Book 1 manuscript).** Ondine's guilt is already known — she confessed the poison to Lucian and named Cray in Book 1 Ch. 20, and the crown has let it stand. What nobody knows is what she was paying off. Her grandmother Honorine signed a debt before Ondine was born and put the unborn child's *name* up as collateral. The night Silas finally brings Ondine the writ he's carried for three months, Absalom Cray calls the debt: the collateral falls due at the new moon. Six weeks. The engine is the **debt, not the discovery.**

**The rules of the paper.** A witch debt can be discharged by substitution — a name of equal weight, freely given. It cannot be discharged by killing the holder: if the holder dies with the paper in force, it passes up to the principal, the account. That is why Silas can't just kill Cray, and why Book 1 left him "in sight, not in custody."

**Act shape.** *One (Ch. 1–11):* the debt called; Cray names his price with his eyes on Silas; Silas moves into the guest room with the lock; the grandmother's ledger is warded against Marchand blood. *Two (Ch. 12–26):* the ledger opens with Silas's blood and shows what Honorine bought — the death of the wolf who killed her husband, delivered by the account; if Ondine's name is taken, every name the Marchand line holds (half the debts of both courts) goes with it, which is the access the account is buying. Cray offers Silas the substitution: *his* name, which puts an instrument inside the crown. Silas considers it and lies to his king a third time. Ondine reads his intent with a working that costs her a memory and plans her own way out. *Three (Ch. 27–34):* Silas asks Lucian for help — the thing his whole life trained him against. Ondine binds him to her house with the writ's own words and goes to Cray alone, into a room she prepared. She substitutes Cray's name into the collateral line — salt for bone — and pays with her memory of her grandmother's face. The paper takes its own holder. Silas finishes it.

**Advances the through-line:** Cray's ledger shows the account behind the poison has been placing orders in New Orleans for centuries under a rotating set of names. Every order, across four hundred years, purchased the same thing: a grieving person with access.

**Payoffs collected:** the five Book 1 Silas–Ondine moments (skipped house, the withheld week, the errands, the coffee, the writ) · Cray's drink at the bar and the wrong condolence formula · *Cray* in Bastien's list · the Ward-estate pipe · the page Silas took in the Book 1 epilogue · Cassian's "a broker between us."

**Ending state.** Cray dead, the Marchand debt void, Ondine's name her own. HEA.
**Hook:** the rotating names go back further than the city. One of them is on a wolf grave in Barataria.

# BOOK 3 — *Son of Wolves*

**Couple:** Rémy Thibodeaux (31, wolf) × Isabeau Vale (214, vampire)
**Antagonist:** the pack's succession war — and Gaspard's refusal to die usefully
**Heat:** 11 · first by Ch. 9 (later than usual; the distrust has to earn it)
**Trope stack:** enemies to lovers · forbidden across species · exiled heir · the murderer and the murdered man's daughter, reversed

**Premise.** The wolf who killed Selene's parents and the daughter of the vampire who told him to. Both of them are what's left over from other people's convictions. Rémy is exiled, owed a debt he can't pay, and the only honest thing he has is that he isn't pretending he didn't do it.

**Act shape.** *One:* the pack fractures over succession; Rémy is summoned back by wolves who want a weak Alpha and finds Isabeau already in Barataria, auditing what her father left. *Two:* they are each the only person who cannot be lied to about what happened, which turns out to be a kind of intimacy nobody wanted. Selene is in this book, in the room, and does not forgive him. *Three:* the succession turns violent, Gaspard chooses the pack over his son in public, and Rémy has to win an Alpha's authority he does not believe he deserves.

**Advances the through-line:** the pack's oldest ritual ground and the vampires' oldest interment vault sit above the same point. Isabeau notices because she was raised to notice when two things are in tune.

**The hard requirement.** Rémy must earn a redemption he does not deserve, with Selene alive and refusing to grant it. If it's cheap, the series has cheated Book 1's heroine. He ends the book forgiven by exactly one person, and she isn't Selene.

**Payoffs collected:** Rémy's Ch. 28 confession · Isabeau's three silent looks · Thibault · Delphine's four appearances.

**Ending state.** Rémy is Alpha, Isabeau is a Vale in a wolf house, and the truce is now a marriage. HEA.
**Hook:** a Conclave envoy arrives to review whether Lucian's kingship, and his treaty with a wolf pack, will be permitted to stand.

# BOOK 4 — *Heir of Ash*

**Couple:** Tobias Vale (158, vampire) × Severine Aldric (611, vampire, Conclave envoy)
**Antagonist:** the Conclave — and the verdict she was sent with, already written
**Heat:** 11 · first by Ch. 7
**Trope stack:** enemies to lovers · she's his judge · touch-her-and-die inverted · the functionary and the executioner

**Premise.** The continental authority that ratifies vampire kings sends a woman to find House Croix unfit. The only person willing to help her read four hundred years of House records is the cousin of the traitor — a court functionary nobody has ever considered dangerous, who has spent his life repairing what this family keeps breaking.

**Act shape.** *One:* Severine arrives with a finding she is expected to reach, and Tobias is assigned to her because he is harmless. *Two:* the records don't support the verdict, which means the verdict was about something other than Lucian. She begins investigating her own institution, and the only person she can say that to is the one man in the city with no standing to betray her. *Three:* the Conclave moves to remove them both, and Tobias — who has never once been the dangerous person in a room — becomes it.

**Advances the through-line:** the Conclave has forbidden the abandonment of New Orleans for six hundred years. Not protected it. **Forbidden leaving it.** Something is being kept occupied, and occupation is a form of containment.

**Cognitive systems:** Tobias thinks in **repair** — what's cracked, what was mended badly, what will hold under weight. Severine thinks in **testimony** — what counts as evidence, what a room would actually believe.

**Payoffs collected:** Tobias's three administrative Book 1 scenes · the Vale name rehabilitated after Book 3 · Book 2's rotating account names, now traced into Conclave paper.

**Ending state.** The Conclave's New Orleans mandate is public, Severine is exiled from her own order, and House Croix has a continental enemy. HEA.
**Hook:** the containment order names what it contains. One word, in a language that predates both species.

# BOOK 5 — *God of Blood*

**Couple:** Delphine Thibodeaux (28 by now, wolf) × Elias Roux (33, the second hybrid)
**Antagonist:** Verity
**Heat:** 11 · first by Ch. 8
**Trope stack:** fated-adjacent but chosen · touch-him-and-die · the last two of a kind · the whole city as the third party

**Premise.** Before there were two species there was one thing, and it is under New Orleans, and it has been starving for centuries. It cannot eat anything as small as a person. It eats death at scale — so it finds grief in someone powerful and waters it for four hundred years. It found Cassian. It has found others. The only creatures it cannot consume are the ones that are both halves at once, and there are two of them alive.

**Act shape.** *One:* the containment fails, and the city starts producing the kind of violence that has a shape. Delphine — the pack's conscience, now its diplomat — is the only person both species will still hear. Elias is found: the second hybrid, raised human, told nothing. *Two:* the origin. One thing, split, and three hundred years of hatred that is a family argument nobody remembers starting. Every faction wants to use Verity, feed her, or be her. *Three:* she wakes fully, and the answer isn't a weapon — it's the two of them being what she cannot digest, in a city that finally has a reason to stop.

**Resolves the through-line:** Verity fed Cassian's grief. He still chose. Both things are true, and Lucian has to hear it in a scene that makes Book 1's climax retroactively worse and better at once.

**Payoffs collected:** every book. Selene's condition made central without a Lucian–Selene retread. Cassian's last word paid in full.

**Cognitive systems:** Delphine thinks in **scent and weather** — what's coming, what it smells like before it arrives. Elias thinks in **mirrors and halves**.

**Ending state.** Verity ended or re-buried at a cost that isn't cheap. Both species know what they are. The truce is no longer a treaty, it's an acknowledgment. HEA.
**Series close:** a door in the city stays unlocked on purpose, and Lucian is the one who left it that way. His father's philosophy, chosen rather than inherited.
