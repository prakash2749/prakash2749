# World Bible — *The Blood Code*

Expanded canon. Built on `reference/source-bible-v1.md`, which wins any conflict unless `CANON_LEDGER.md` records a dated override.

---

## 1. New Orleans — "The City of Devils"

Two predator species have contested this city for roughly three hundred years. Neither can finish the other. Neither will leave.

The city's public face sits directly on top of the hierarchy. Clubs, hotels, restaurants, shipping, real estate, parish politics — all of it is owned upward. A bartender on Frenchmen Street may not know who signs his checks four layers up. The tourist economy is the perfect cover, because a city where strangers arrive drunk and leave hungover absorbs missing time without asking questions.

**Why here** — the surface answer both species give: the port, the money, the old families, three hundred years of accumulated infrastructure.

**Why here** — the true answer, which nobody alive knows: Verity went dormant under this ground. Both species feel the pull and neither examines it. It reads to them as tradition, as stubbornness, as pride. Book 5 reveals it was never any of those. *Seed this as an unexamined compulsion, never as a mystery a character is investigating.* Characters should say things like "we don't leave" and never once ask why not.

### Territory

| Zone | Held by | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| French Quarter, CBD, Garden District | Vampires | Money, court, old houses. House Croix seat is in the Garden District. |
| Barataria, the West Bank, the bayou parishes | Wolves | The Thibodeaux pack. Water, land, room to run. |
| Tremé, Bywater, Marigny | Contested / neutral | Where the truce actually gets tested. Most street-level friction happens here. |
| The Marchand house | Neutral by custom | Ondine's ground. Both species observe it because both need her. |
| Below the water table | — | Nothing is down there. Everyone is certain of this. |

Crossing a boundary armed is a truce violation. Crossing unarmed is rude. Crossing to feed is a war crime.

## 2. Humans

Humans are not going extinct and are not livestock. They are the majority population and the ground both species are standing on.

Knowledge is **partial, uneven, and actively suppressed** — four tiers:

1. **Tourists and transplants.** Nothing. The city is haunted in a fun way.
2. **Folk knowledge.** Old locals hold real information shaped as superstition. Don't invite a stranger in. Don't take the second drink. Don't walk the levee at moonrise. The rules work, and nobody remembers why.
3. **The arrangement.** A small number of humans know precisely — certain NOPD ranks, two coroners, a handful of old-family lawyers, some club owners. They are paid, protected, and hypnosis-marked at intervals. They are not slaves. Several of them like the arrangement.
4. **The exposed.** Humans who found out on their own. Vampire society's standard response is erasure of memory, not of person — killing them would violate the Blood Code. This is the Code working as intended, and it is worth showing on the page at least once.

Humans can and do form relationships with both species. Pack custom treats it as contamination. Vampire society treats it as a hobby. Both positions are on the page; neither is endorsed by the narrative.

## 3. Vampires

- Do not die of age or ordinary illness. Not invulnerable.
- Pass completely as human. Most live under human identities with human jobs, because centuries are long and boredom kills more vampires than wolves do.
- Enhanced strength, speed, senses, regeneration, hypnosis.
- Power scales with age, and it scales unevenly — an unlucky three-hundred-year-old loses to a gifted eighty-year-old. Age is the floor, not the ceiling.
- Aurelian Croix was the strongest alive. Not the oldest. That distinction matters and someone should say it out loud.

### Feeding

A vampire needs human blood and does not need to kill for it. The lawful method: hypnotize, drink what you need, close the wound, alter the memory, walk away.

**Keep the moral contradiction live.** This is lawful predation performed on people who did not agree to it and will not remember it. Vampires genuinely consider it clean. A human who understood it would call it something else. Both readings are true at once and the narrative never resolves it. When a POV vampire feeds casually early in a book, do not soften it.

Voluntary feeding — a human or wolf who knows, remembers, and says yes — is rare, intimate, and considerably more intense for both parties. It is the entire architecture of the Lucian–Selene pact.

### Hypnosis

Eye contact plus proximity plus intent. Works on most humans. Fails on:

- Anyone with meaningful supernatural blood, including hybrids
- Vampires of comparable or greater power
- Anyone already carrying another vampire's fresh mark
- Anyone drunk past a certain point, which is a running problem in this city

A vampire cannot hypnotize someone into wanting what they don't want. It edits memory and suppresses alarm. It does not manufacture desire. **This limit is absolute and the whole romance rests on it** — Selene's immunity means every choice she makes is hers, and Lucian's inability to compel desire means the same is true of every human he has ever fed from. Say it once, early, in plain language.

## 4. The Blood Code

The legal and moral spine of vampire civilization. Not a philosophy — a statute with a body count.

> **Take what you require. Never take the life that carries it.**

- Feeding is lawful.
- Killing requires recognized justification: self-defense, defense of another, lawful war, sanctioned execution.
- Killing an innocent human for pleasure, cruelty, temper, sport, convenience, or sloppy feeding is capital.
- The Code binds by rank, not despite it. The more powerful the vampire, the less discretion they get.
- Judgment sits with the King, or with a council when there is no King.

Aurelian enforced it against his own house and against his closest friend's child. That is the single fact the whole series turns on.

**Cassian's reading, which he is not simply wrong about:** the Code takes a predator species and teaches it to be ashamed of its own mouth. It was written to make vampires legible to humans. It asks a wolf to apologize for teeth. Give this argument real weight — a villain whose critique is stupid isn't frightening.

## 5. Wolves

- Fully human form, full participation in human society. Most Barataria wolves have licenses, mortgages, and boats.
- Pack structure, Alpha authority. Loyalty is the organizing virtue and the organizing wound.
- Shifted form is stronger, faster, and harder to govern. Control is a learned skill and not everyone learns it.
- Wolves heal fast but not like vampires. Nothing regenerates a severed limb.
- Silver is a folk story. What actually kills a wolf is enough damage fast enough, which is exactly what an ancient vampire is for.

The pack contains the disciplined, the political, the violent, the loyal, the frightened, and the ambitious, in roughly the same proportions as the vampire court. **Neither species is the moral one.** If a draft starts implying wolves are earthy and honest while vampires are decadent and false, that's drift.

Wolves consider vampires an insult: a thing that used to be a person and refuses to finish dying. Vampires consider wolves an embarrassment: an animal that learned to sign a lease. Neither has any idea these are sibling grievances.

## 6. The Truce

Negotiated by Aurelian Croix and Gaspard Thibodeaux roughly forty years before the series opens, after the last open war cost both sides enough to matter.

Terms:
1. Recognized territorial boundaries.
2. No open warfare.
3. Disputes settled in council, not in retaliation.
4. Each leader is answerable for their own people.

Aurelian believed the truce was the only thing keeping the city survivable. Lucian believed it handed wolves four decades of legitimacy they hadn't earned. **Both of them were arguing about something neither could see** — the truce is why Verity has been starving.

Critically: the truce is politics. It is not what made the King killable. Nothing about the truce weakened him. He was killable because he'd once loved somebody enough to hand over his own blood.

## 7. Witches

Small, independent, hereditary, and expensive. Not a faction — a trade.

Witches are human-descended and mortal, though a strong one ages slowly. Power passes down a line, and what passes down is not raw ability but **standing**: accumulated bargains, held collateral, debts owed inward and outward. A witch is a ledger that walks. Ondine Marchand is the strongest in the city because her line has been collecting for two hundred years, not because she was born brighter than anyone else.

The magic economy — which is Book 2's whole scope — runs on three things:

- **Names.** A true name given up can be held. Whoever holds it can compel its owner. This is the most expensive thing a witch can sell and the most dangerous thing anyone can buy.
- **Debts.** Enforceable across generations. A witch can inherit an obligation she never agreed to.
- **Salt, bone, blood, and hair** as ordinary working materials. Unglamorous. Kitchen magic, mostly.

New Orleans's real spiritual traditions are not the source of this system and must not be used as set dressing for it. The Marchand line is fictional, Creole, and its own thing. Real practices and real historical figures stay out.

Both species need witches and neither can afford to own one. Hence the Marchand house is neutral ground by custom — an arrangement that holds right up until Book 2, when it doesn't.

## 8. Verity — the bottom of the series

**Nothing in Books 1–3 states any of this. Book 1 contains exactly one instance of the name, in Cassian's dying line.**

Before there were two species there was one thing. It drank, and it ran on four legs, and it was not a person. It came apart — the how is Book 5's business — and the split produced two halves that each kept part of it. Vampires took the thirst and the stillness and the long memory. Wolves took the body, the speed, and the pack.

They have hated each other for three hundred years in this city and considerably longer before it, and the hatred is a family argument nobody remembers starting. Each species is looking at the half of itself it lost.

Verity is what's left of the original. She is under New Orleans, she has been dormant and starving for centuries, and she cannot feed on anything as small as a person. She feeds on death at scale. So she cultivates it: finds grief in someone powerful, and patiently makes it the center of a life. She does not create the grief. She never has to. **She found Cassian's grief four hundred years ago and spent four centuries watering it.**

Cassian chose. That has to stay true or Book 1's climax means nothing. He was handed a reason to want what he already wanted, and a knife, and four hundred years of someone agreeing with him.

Selene is the first creature in centuries to be both halves in one body. In Book 1 that makes her useful to Lucian. In Book 5 it makes her the only thing Verity cannot eat.

## 9. Canon-critical don'ts

- Don't give hypnosis the power to create desire. Ever.
- Don't make Bloodsteel a magic system. It is a blade quenched in the King's blood and it gets three sentences.
- Don't let any character ask why neither species will leave New Orleans.
- Don't let wolves be the honest species or vampires the decadent one.
- Don't build rules for consecrated ground, silver, garlic, running water, or invitation thresholds. Every absent cliché is a gift.
- Don't let a Book 1 character say the name Verity except Cassian, dying, once.
