Selene is in the courtyard, on the bench by the fountain my father put there because he believed courtyards should have places to sit, even courtyards nobody sat in. Her father's ledger is open on her knee, and she's wearing the boots she bought on Magazine Street.

Marguerite invited her to the coronation, stiffly and in writing, which from Marguerite is a bouquet, and Selene said no. *That's their language. Let them speak it. I'll be in the courtyard.*

I go down. She looks up when I cross the stones, and sees the crown. Her face does something I've never seen it do: a woman who has been looking at a man for weeks, seeing what he was always going to become.

"It's smaller than I thought," she says.

"So I'm told, ghost. Disappointed?"

"Does it come off? Please tell me it comes off."

"It comes off."

"Good, because it's ugly. It looks like a hubcap with ambitions."

"It's four hundred years old."

"So's the ugly."

I sit beside her. The bench is cold, and the fountain makes the sound it's made since my father built it.

A door opens on the gallery above us: Roche, the younger one, leaving the chamber. He looks down and sees his King on a stone bench with a woman, her knee against his, no distance between them.

I don't move, and I don't create space.

He nods once, precisely, and goes back through the door, and by morning the whole court will know what that nod means.

"The pact," I say. "The terms are met. The arrangement—"

"The arrangement ended weeks ago and we both know it." She closes the ledger. "I stopped counting the night you turned my father's pages from the corner."

"Selene."

"Don't. Don't say anything kingly, and don't say *mine* or *stay* or anything with a title in front of it. Say what's underneath."

"I don't want to do this without you, ghost."

"Do what? Say it."

"Any of it." I take the crown off and set it on the bench between us, a piece of metal on a piece of stone. "The house, the city, the morning, and the one after that."

"No."

She picks up the crown and turns it over, and then she puts it back on my head.

"Still ugly as hell," she says, "but it fits."
