# 24 · Selene

I've been reading the ledger in two registers and he's only been reading it in one.

That's the sentence I've been carrying since the archive. He sees the mark and he sees his friend's name underneath it and the weight of that is enough to keep him staring at the same page for an hour.

I see the mark and the name and a third thing: what the mark is doing in my father's handwriting. It's a frequency, more than a signature. The jobs with the mark get more regular as the years go on, and the fees climb, and the climbing tells me what the mark by itself doesn't.

Whoever this client was, my father became more valuable to him over time. Not less. Not steady. *More.* The payments rise as a salary rises when the employee starts carrying a secret the employer can't afford to lose.

My father was holding something for Cassian Vale. Something worth more every year. Something worth killing to keep buried.

God. I've known it a day longer, because I read the second register while he was still reading the first.

I didn't tell him.

I wasn't protecting Cassian, and I wasn't being kind. I wanted to see Lucian's face when the name arrived on its own. I wanted to know whether the knowledge would break him or sharpen him, and the answer is: it sharpened him, and the sharpening cut me, and I don't regret the waiting.

---

There's a gathering in the east wing.

Vampires this old don't throw parties. They hold receptions. The court assembles in a long room with good light and indifferent music and glasses of a red that isn't wine, and they stand in clusters and talk about the things they talk about, which are power and money and who has too much of each.

I'm here because Lucian asked me to come. He said it in the voice he uses now, with no *mine* and no command. A question with a shape: *Would you come to this? I'd like you beside me.*

He's learning. God help us both.

I wear a dress I found in the west wing closet. Someone put it there — Bastien, probably, or one of the staff who's decided I'm staying. Black. Simple. The kind of thing that says *I belong here* in one language and *I dare you to tell me I don't* in the other.

---

Cassian is across the room.

He's talking to two Elders and a woman I don't know — warm, attentive, asking about somebody's mother. His voice carries like warm things carry, and the people around him lean in like plants.

He says something about Aurelian. Something fond — a memory about a table at a restaurant, a waiter who called the King by a name nobody else used. The room nods. The room *agrees.* Three centuries of a dead man, and the bastard who killed him is telling the best stories about him.

I watch a woman at the back of the cluster look at the floor.

She's tall and dark-haired and court-raised, and I know her. She's the woman from Ondine's kitchen, the one in the corner with a cup of something that wasn't tea. Isabeau. She's been in this room all evening and she hasn't spoken once.

When Cassian says Aurelian's name, everyone looks at Cassian. She looks at the floor.

I catch her eye, and she looks back at me steadily, without blinking, without looking away, without the small adjustment that everyone else in this room makes when they see me.

She doesn't say a word.

I know that look. Hell, I invented that look. I've been wearing it my whole life — the look of a person who can see what's happening in a room and has chosen not to speak. God knows I spent enough years doing the same.

---

The Aldric brother finds me by the staircase.

He's not the cousin Lucian sent to Baton Rouge — that one is still there, sitting in a room he chose not to leave. This is the brother, louder and taller. The kind of man who takes up space like a building does. Nobody's ever told him to stop.

He's had three glasses of whatever they're drinking, and the third one has loosened the hinge on his big stupid mouth.

"The arrangement," he says. He says it loud enough for six people to hear. "Still here."

"Still here."

"My brother left because of you." He steps closer. "He left this city — this *family's* city — because a half-breed bartender has the King's ear and the King's bed, and nobody in this damn house has the spine to say what that costs."

Six people are listening and pretending not to. That's the court version of listening harder.

Pack teaches you this early. When a man shows you his fear, you have two choices. You can step back and let him keep it. Or you can name it out loud and watch him discover he's naked.

I've been stepping back my whole life.

"You're afraid," I say.

He blinks. "What?"

"You're afraid." I say it like I'd say *it's raining.* Mild. An observation about the weather. "Of what I represent, not of me. A woman who isn't vampire and isn't wolf and isn't human, standing next to the King. Because if I can stand there, then the ladder you've spent three centuries climbing is just a suggestion."

His face changes. Exposure. He's just been undressed in public by someone he assumed couldn't read.

"Your family has been positioning for this succession since before I was born," I say. "Your brother came to this house to test whether the King's attention had a weakness, and he found one — me. And when Lucian removed him, your family lost its closest line to the Crown, and you've been sitting in this room all evening working up the courage to find out if the weakness is real."

Silence. Six people who were pretending not to listen have stopped pretending.

"It isn't," I say. "I'm not his weakness. I'm the only person in this house who can hear what you're actually saying when you open your mouth. And what you're actually saying is: *please let the hierarchy be real, because if it isn't, I've wasted three hundred years.*"

His face goes white.

He wasn't prepared for this. Nobody is prepared for this — for a woman who hears what's under the words and says the underneath part out loud, to a room, in a sentence that sounds like courtesy and cuts like a blade under the ribs.

He opens his mouth. Closes it. Opens it again.

"You—"

"I'm done," I say. "Enjoy the reception."

I take my glass and I walk away from him, and the six people part for me, and I can feel their attention on my back as plainly as a palm.

---

Lucian watched.

He's across the room. He saw all of it, from the approach to the white face and the silence.

He's looking at me like he looks at Thibault. Like he's seen a weapon.

I hold his eyes across the room. I don't smile. I let him see exactly what I am. A woman who has spent her whole life hiding her teeth, because you only get to use them once before people learn to close their mouths around you.

I used them tonight.

And the whole goddamn room heard.

He crosses to me without hurrying, and the room gets out of his way without being asked. He stops behind me, close, and his hand settles on the small of my back. Low. Lower than a king's hand should be in a room full of Elders.

His fingers are cold through the dress. My whole spine lights up.

"Remind me," he says against my ear, "never to argue with you in public, ghost."

"You argue with me in private all the time."

"In private I win."

"In private," I tell him, "I *let* you win."

His hand presses once, hard enough to feel it in my hips. Then it's gone, and so is he, back across the room like nothing happened, and six people are staring at the place where his hand was.

---

The woman who was looking at the floor is by the piano.

I go to her because she's the only person at this reception who didn't flinch when I named the Aldric brother's fear. Everyone else adjusted. A step back, a look away, a room deciding how afraid to be.

She didn't move.

"That was well done," she says. Her voice is formal. Over-formal, the kind of courtesy with a blade in it. "We weren't properly introduced at Ondine's. If you'd be so kind."

"Selene."

"Isabeau." She looks at Cassian across the room. He's still talking. Still warm. Still asking about someone's mother. "You hear things."

"Everyone hears things. Hell, the plants hear things."

"Not the way you do." She turns back to me. "You hear the second version. The one underneath."

My chest goes still. This woman sees me. Lucian sees a beautiful problem. She sees me as I see rooms, underneath the surface of them.

Her hands are at her sides, and I watch the right one without meaning to. She wears a ring on her middle finger, silver, thin, old enough that the engraving has gone smooth, and she turns it. Deliberately. Like turning a dial down.

I've seen that gesture before, in myself. The small physical ritual of a woman deciding not to say what she heard. I used to press my thumbnail into my index finger under the bar at Antoine's when a customer said something that had two meanings and I chose to serve the drink instead of the translation.

She's been doing it longer. I can see the years of it in her shoulders, which hold a permanent quarter-turn away from whatever room she's in. She's declining the room, not hiding from it. A woman who found the exit a long time ago and stands near it out of habit.

Her mouth is set in a line that looks like tension and is actually practice. A face that stopped reacting to what it heard, because the reactions cost more than the silence.

Give me a century of never opening my mouth, and I'd be her.

"You could do it too," I say.

"I can." She sets her glass down. "I choose not to."

"Why?"

She looks at me for a long time. In her eyes there's a look I recognize — the heaviness of a person who knows what a room is doing and has decided that knowing is enough.

"Because I've seen what happens when you name it out loud," she says. "And I've decided the naming isn't worth what it costs."

She walks away, and the piano lid is closed. I don't know when the hell she closed it.

---

I go back to the west wing and shut the door and say *fuck* into a pillow.

I sit on the bed with the ledger in my lap and I look at my hands.

They're steady. They're always steady — that's the problem. I just took a man apart in front of six witnesses and my hands are as calm as they were when I was pouring drinks on Frenchmen Street.

Shit. That should scare me more than it does.

To a wolf, showing your teeth is a permanent decision. Once the pack has seen them, they know. No fucking take-backs. They adjust. They close their mouths.

I showed mine tonight. Holy shit, I showed them.

The room will be different tomorrow. The woman in the west wing has teeth, and the teeth are hers, and nobody in that corridor needs Lucian to walk in for her.

I wanted this. The sentence I was building in the corridor outside the library, when he walked in and took it from me.

I have it now.

And the woman by the piano — Isabeau — chose differently. She can hear the same thing I hear, and she keeps it behind her teeth, and I think she's been doing it for a very long time.

I think about Cassian telling stories about a dead man he killed. The fucking gall of it. I think about the room agreeing. I think about a woman looking at the floor because the floor is the only honest surface in the whole goddamn room.

I open the ledger.

The name is Cassian's. The mark is Cassian's. The money is Cassian's.

Tomorrow I say it out loud.
