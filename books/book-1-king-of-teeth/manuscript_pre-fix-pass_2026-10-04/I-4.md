# I-4 · Cassian

*Placement: immediately after Ch. 25.*

The boy has stopped bringing me things.

He does not know that he has done it, and he has not decided it in a sentence. He is not yet capable of deciding to distrust me, and on the day he becomes capable of it he will be finished and so will I.

But he used to come and find me, at any hour. He would arrive with a thing in his hands and set it down in front of me and wait while I looked at it, and he has done that since he was young enough to need lifting onto the desk to do it.

This week he has come to me, and each time I asked the first question.

---

He lied to me in his father's study, weeks ago now, and he did it badly, and I let him.

I asked what Ambroise had found. I had asked every few days since the body came up out of the cold room, and every time he had told me a little and been glad to tell it, because a man carrying a thing like that wants hands underneath it.

That evening he said: wolf bites, heavy damage, and the worst of it at the hip.

Then he put both hands flat on the desk and kept them there, which he does not do.

Why is he such a poor liar? He has never had to practice. Aurelian raised him to be direct and I trained him to be still, and between us we made a man who cannot dress a falsehood.

His voice was perfect, and his hands were too perfect, pressed to the wood so they would have nowhere to go. The woman on the window seat never once looked at me, which is how I knew she was listening.

So he has seen the wound. How long has he known? Weeks, and I missed it.

He has sat in a room with me most mornings since and asked after my house. He has let me put my hand on the back of his neck, and said nothing.

That is my boy.

That is not Aurelian's boy. Aurelian would have stood up in a full chamber on the first evening and said *one of you was in this room,* and taken whatever came, and called it honesty.

The son kept it in his pocket and went hunting.

I have never been prouder of him in my life. That is not a figure of speech and I am not going to soften it into one.

---

What did it cost me, in that room?

He said *wolf bites* and looked at me, and I felt my own face arrange itself.

I did it well, and that is the whole of the cost, and I did not know it was going to be the cost until it was already done.

My eyes went where they go, and my hands stayed where they were. And something came up in my chest that was almost entirely real, because he was telling me that his father had been savaged by animals, and I have been listening to that sentence in this city since the funeral and it has not stopped working on me yet.

Then I said the things a man says. I told him he was growing into it, which is true. I told him to choose the people he can tell the truth to, and choose them soon.

I meant it. I was standing in his doorway when I said it, the one man in the city he should not choose.

Every part of that was genuine, and every part of it was in the service of the lie.

There is no longer a seam between the two, and I have been doing this for a long time without ever having to feel it as a skill.

---

I could tell him tomorrow.

The thought arrives with its own furniture now, as a habit does. It came to me first in that doorway, and it has come every night since.

He is closer than he knows. The woman is cleverer than he is about a certain kind of thing, and she has been quiet in a way I do not like. What is she sitting on?

What if I said it first? Set it down myself, in a warm room, and let him have the whole of it out of my own mouth instead of off a page?

He would remember that I told him. Whatever he becomes, there would be a version of it in which the old man did not make him dig.

I have thought about that for longer than I thought about the blade.

---

At the next full council I will ask the court for its war. The boy has been standing in front of it with his arms out, and I will walk around him, gently, as I have walked around him since he could stand.

Is there permission for that? I looked for it, and it is not there.

A man who can no longer find the seam between the genuine and the lie does not get to ask whether he is permitted.

His father is dead.

I am what he has.
