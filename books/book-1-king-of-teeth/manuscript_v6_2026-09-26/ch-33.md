# 33 · Selene

"You'll want to be close enough to hear," Marguerite says. "Not in the room. Close enough."

She's standing in the door of the west wing with her hands folded, and it takes me a second to understand that she's been standing there a while. She hasn't come in. She's never once come in.

"He told you," I say.

"This morning." She looks past me, at the window. "He came to my rooms before the sun was down and sat on the end of my bed like he did when he was a boy with a nightmare, and he told me."

"All of it?"

"Enough of it." Her jaw sets. "Cassian's name. The window. My brother."

I don't know what to say to her. I've spent weeks in this house translating what Marguerite means underneath what Marguerite says, and right now there's nothing underneath. She's just an old woman in a doorway.

"I'm sorry," I say.

"Don't be." She finally looks at me. "I've known Cassian Vale for longer than your country has had a flag. He stood beside me at my brother's funeral and held my hand through the whole of it. He sat in my kitchen last week and asked about my chest."

"He asks everyone about their chest."

It comes out before I can stop it. Her mouth twitches into something that isn't quite a smile.

"Yes," she says. "He does."

---

She comes into the room, just inside the door, for the first time.

"The court will be there," she says. "Two of the Roches. Silas. It has to be witnessed, and it has to be done correctly, and the boy will do it correctly because that's who my brother made him."

"Then why do you need me close enough to hear?"

"Because the court will be witnessing the law." She looks at me very steadily. "Somebody has to witness him."

The sentence goes into me and sits down.

"Why me?"

"Because you're the only person in this house he didn't choose for his father's reasons." She turns back to the door. "Everyone else he keeps because Aurelian kept them. You, he found in a hallway."

"You wanted my ass out by the weekend."

"I did." She doesn't apologize. "I was wrong about the weekend. I'm frequently wrong about weekends."

She goes. Her heels on the hall floor, even and unhurried.

Then she stops, and without turning around she says, "He'll need to eat afterward. He won't want to. Make him."

"How?"

"You're a bartender, aren't you?" Her voice drifts back up the hall. "Figure it out."

---

The house empties out in the afternoon.

The staff are sent home. The linen girl is already in Lafayette. The kitchen is dark except for one lamp, and under the lamp Silas is sitting at the long table with a whetstone and a short plain blade.

He doesn't look up when I come in.

I get a glass of water I don't want and stand at the counter and watch him. The stone goes one way, then the other. The sound is small and wet and steady, and it's the only sound in the whole house.

"That isn't his father's," I say.

"No."

"He asked you for it."

"This morning." The stone goes one way. "He said he wanted plain steel. Nothing with anyone's blood in it."

"Did he say why?"

"He didn't have to." Silas tests the edge on his thumbnail. It shaves a sliver of nail off clean. He goes back to the stone anyway.

I've never had a conversation with Silas that lasted longer than a sentence. I've never seen the man sit. Not once, in all this goddamn time. Not once.

"Can I ask you something?"

"You're going to."

"Did you know? Before. About Cassian. Shit, I'm sorry, you don't have to—"

The stone stops.

For a long moment he doesn't move at all. Then he starts again, slower.

"I knew he was the only door in this house I never checked," he says. "That's not the same thing."

"No."

"It's worse."

He turns the blade over and does the other side.

"When it's done," he says, without looking at me, "he'll sit on the floor. He always sits on the floor, after. Since he was a boy." The stone goes one way. "Don't let him sit there too long."

"I won't."

"Good." The other way. "He listens to you."

"He doesn't listen to anyone. He's a stubborn bastard."

Silas looks up at me for the first time. His face is completely flat, and his eyes aren't.

"He listens to you," he says again.

Then he goes back to the stone, and I understand I've been dismissed, and I go.

---

Lucian finds me in the west hall an hour before dark.

He's dressed in the dark coat, the one that fits his shoulders, with his hair back, and he looks like a king, which is to say like nobody I'd ever want to be in a room with.

He stops in front of me and doesn't say anything.

Then he puts his forehead against mine.

He's cold, as he always is. His palms come up and hold my face on either side, and he stands there in the hall with his forehead on mine and his eyes closed, and I can feel him breathing. Slow. Deliberate. A man counting himself down.

"Ghost," he says.

"I'm here."

"Stay out of the room."

"Marguerite already told me."

"Marguerite told you to stay close enough to hear."

"She did."

His thumbs move on my jaw.

"Then stay close enough to hear," he says. "And stay out of the room."

"Lucian."

"Say it."

"I'll stay out of the room."

He kisses me. Hard, once, with his hand on the back of my neck. There's no heat in it at all. It's a man leaving his hand print on the one thing he wants back before he goes into a fire.

Then he goes down the hall to the study, and I watch the lamplight come on in the open doorway.

---

Cassian comes at the usual hour.

I see him from the west hall window. He crosses the courtyard in the last of the light with his coat buttoned and his hands in his pockets, and halfway across he stops.

He stands in the middle of the courtyard. The stone there is worn in a patch, pale, smoother than the rest. I've walked across it a hundred times and never noticed.

He looks down at it for a while.

Then he looks up at the house. At the windows. At me.

He sees me. I know the bastard does.

And he takes one hand out of his pocket and lifts it. A little wave. The kind you give somebody's granddaughter across a garden party.

I don't know what the hell my body does. My hand comes up and I wave back.

He smiles. It reaches his eyes, because with him it always does.

In English that's a wave. Underneath, it's a man saying goodbye to a house.

He goes in through the east door. It's been open to him since before this city had a name.

---

I sit on the bench.

It's at the end of the west hall, past the turn, where the study door is out of sight and every sound from that room comes down the corridor like water down a gutter. I've sat here a dozen times without knowing what it was for.

Now I know what it's for.

I hear the front hall. The coat coming off, maybe, or not. Footsteps on the boards, slightly heavier than they should be, because he's always walked like the floor was something he was doing a favor.

The footsteps stop outside the study.

And I wait for him to walk in, the way he has walked in every evening.

He doesn't.

I hear him knock.

The first wrong thing. Three knocks, even, deliberate. He's already decided what the knocking means.

He knows. He knows, goddamn it. He knew in the courtyard. Maybe he knew when he buttoned his coat.

"Come in," Lucian says.

The study door is always open. It stays open.

I hold on to the edge of the bench and I listen.
