# 6 · Selene

The bar is full and nobody is drinking.

I know the tell, because I've worked enough Friday nights to know what a crowd that came for the music looks like, and this isn't it. These people came because they didn't want to be home.

Antoine has the news on behind the bar with the sound off. Nobody is watching it, and everybody is watching it.

The crawl at the bottom mentions a disturbance in Barataria, a gas leak, they're calling it: an evacuation of several homes on the south end, two injuries reported, no fatalities.

In English, that's a gas leak.

In the other language, the one nobody in this room speaks and everybody in this room feels in their joints, something came through Barataria last night and the city can feel the bruise.

The old locals don't have the words, but they have the grammar. Who taught it to them?

Don't take the second drink, and if a man at a bar doesn't blink for too long, sit somewhere else.

If the news says *gas leak* in Barataria, stay out of the Quarter for a week.

The woman at the end of my bar, sixty-something, with her purse in her lap all night, knows it without knowing why.

"Another?" I ask her.

"I'm fine, baby. Just don't want to go home to an empty house with the goddamn news on." She pats the bar. "Just sitting, you know?"

"Kitchen's closing in an hour, hell or high water. Antoine's been threatening to go home since eight, and tonight I think he means it. You want anything before the fryer dies?"

"No, baby. I just want the noise."

"I know." She looks at the TV. "They say what started it?"

"Gas leak."

"Gas leak. You believe that?"

"I believe what they put on the television, baby. Don't you?"

"Mm." She turns her glass on its napkin. "My grandmother used to say when the gas leaks in Jean Lafitte, you stay on this side of the canal."

"Smart woman, huh? Sounds like she knew her shit."

"She was a frightened woman, and that's different." She picks up her purse. "Same thing, though, in the end, isn't it?"

She puts a five on the bar for a three-dollar tonic and leaves. I put the five in the jar and wipe the bar and think about the difference between smart and frightened. Does a woman who can hear both languages have any right to be either?

---

Around ten, two wolves from Tremé come in and sit where I can hear them.

I know them like you know regulars you'd rather not have: the heavy one is Luc. The one with the bad teeth is his cousin, and I've never needed his name. They drink Abita and tip in quarters and talk in front of me like I'm furniture that pours.

Tonight they don't even order, they just sit, and why come in at all?

"Nobody died," Luc says.

"I heard."

"Houses left lit. Old Fontenot's kitchen light was on the whole time, man, the whole time."

"I *heard,* Luc. Christ. The whole fucking bayou heard."

"So what's that?"

The cousin stares at his hands. "That's him saying he could have."

"Could have what? Say it, man."

"All of it." He finally looks up. "The son walked through the settlement like he was looking for his keys, put Arceneaux through his own wall with one hand, flat, like this." He lays his palm on the bar. "And then he *left.*"

"That's so much fucking worse."

"That's what I'm saying. Hell of a thing to put in a man's head at night."

I set two Abitas down in front of them that nobody asked for.

Luc looks at me for the first time in a year of drinking here. "On the house? Since when does anything in this shithole come on the house?"

"You look like hell, and you need it. You want to tell me what happened, or you want to drink?"

"Drink."

"Smart man."

He doesn't thank me, because wolves don't thank people who smell like me. Fuck 'em. But he drinks it.

---

The cousin's right, and it's worse than he knows. How much worse?

A raid that kills is simple: you bury your dead and you go to war, because war is the answer to dead. The grammar is old enough that nobody has to look it up.

A raid that leaves your lights on and your people breathing is a sentence the pack has no word for. They're going to spend the next week trying to translate it.

I've been translating it all goddamn day.

What the dead King's son said last night is: *I am not my father. I could have been worse.*

Most people will only hear one of those sentences. Which one will the pack hear?

And I keep thinking about the other thing: the man at my bar, turning a glass he didn't drink, reading my windows like a lease. He walked into Barataria and every wolf there got out of his way.

I said I needed somebody the wolves were afraid of, didn't I?

---

The rush doesn't come, and Antoine cuts the kitchen early and bangs a stockpot against the sink with more force than the grease requires.

"You okay?" I ask through the window. "You're beating the shit out of that pot."

"My cousin's in Barataria."

"Jesus. Is she hurt? Did you call her?"

"She's fine. Everybody's fine. Scared shitless, but fine." He sets the pot down. "You know what scares the shit out of me? Somebody decided everybody gets to be fine, and that somebody gets to decide again tomorrow."

Pack has a word for that.

It means a debt you owe because somebody declined to collect it.

I close at midnight and sit on my crate in the alley with the map open on my phone, Barataria a green smear south of the canal. I went there and sat on a bench, and a girl told me I smelled like her aunt. I left without doing what I've been building toward since the funeral.

---

Here's what I'm going to do instead.

When a city is afraid, people go where they feel safe: wolves go to Barataria and close the gate, and vampires go to the places they own.

There's a club on Perdido Street with no sign and windows painted black from the inside.

Everyone who works a bar on Frenchmen knows about it. Nobody goes, though the drinks are free if the right people like you. The people who go in come out tired in a way sleep won't fix, and the staff doesn't talk about the back hallways.

The dead King's son drinks there. Will he be there tonight? No fucking idea.

Every wolf in Tremé knows it and hates knowing it.

A woman who walks into a room she doesn't belong in might get a few minutes before anyone notices. A few minutes is a conversation, and a conversation is the only way across the water I've got.

---

I go home and shower and stand in front of my closet. What does a person wear to walk into a room full of vampires?

Two years of bartender clothes, black shirts and dark jeans and shoes that don't slip on a wet floor. Nothing in here was chosen, only selected, and that's a whole different language.

At the back there's a dress.

It's the one nice thing I own that I didn't buy for somebody's funeral. Black, simple, and it does a thing to my shoulders that I've never liked and have been told twice is the point.

I put it on and look at myself in the mirror on the back of the bathroom door, the one with the crack in the corner I keep meaning to replace.

The woman in the mirror is small and dark-haired. She's about to walk into a club where the most dangerous things in this city go to drink, because the man she needs is across a body of water she can't cross alone.

God help her. Honestly.

Then the back of my neck goes cold. Shit.

It's below sound, a frequency in the skin, the kind the wolf-half registers before the rest of me gets the memo. My shoulders draw together and the fine hair on my forearms lifts, and for one breath the dress stops being fabric and becomes a membrane, and everything beyond it is paying attention.

I stand very still. Oh, fuck.

Is it him? It's the corner of my bar when he walked in, the air going out of it, except the bar is eleven blocks away and I'm in my bathroom.

It's older than wolf, lower, colder, and I swear to God I can smell it through a wall and a mile of city. Winter in a room with no windows. My body leans toward it as if it's a voice I can almost hear, and the voice isn't fading.

The reaching stretches farther than it's ever gone, and my pulse picks up and my skin goes tight. I can feel the shape of whatever's out there as you feel fingers a breath before they touch you. The heat of it. The weight. The gap about to close.

It doesn't close, it passes. Whatever it was moves on, and the reaching comes back empty, same as always. I'm standing in my bathroom in a black dress with my palm flat on the mirror and my breath fogging the glass.

But it was closer than it has ever been. How close?

My hands are shaking. Holy shit, my hands are shaking.

It isn't fear, because I know what fear does to me, and this is the other thing. This is heat, low and stupid and getting worse. Oh, for fuck's sake.

I'm wet. From *that.* From a frequency. Is this a medical condition? Should I be seeing someone?

"Get it together," I tell the mirror, and the mirror doesn't have notes.

The knife is on the counter, and I look at it. Do I take it?

I leave it there.

Tonight I'm not carrying a blade, only a face and a voice and the ability to hear both sides of any sentence in any room.

That's the inheritance: my father taught me to listen, and my mother gave me something to listen with.

I put my keys in the small bag, the one with no room for anything else, and lock the apartment behind me, and walk out into a city that's afraid of itself tonight.

I'm not afraid. I should be. That's probably important.

Perdido is a long walk in shoes that aren't built for it, in a dress I've worn once, toward a room full of things that drink.

I'm going where they go.
