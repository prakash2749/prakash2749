# I-2 · Cassian

*Placement: immediately after Ch. 9.*

The attempt failed, and the boy walked out on his own feet.

I have it from men who were never in the same room, which is the only way I take information now. He went in through the alley and fed, and he was standing when the trouble started.

He was not standing long after it.

Then he was gone, and what was meant to happen in that room did not happen.

He was never going to die in a club.

I will say this to no one, because it is not the sort of thing a man says aloud. But there is a boy alive tonight who has been the strongest creature in this city since the week his father stopped being it, and a glass of wine and a wolf by the stairs were never going to be the end of him.

I knew that when I paid for the bottle.

What was supposed to happen is that he would be shown teeth.

His father spent his whole long life explaining to him that the animals at our border are neighbors. Then the animals came through a window and took the man apart, and the boy went into a room and told his council which houses they were not to touch.

And the lesson he took?

He drew a line on a map and he made it narrow, on purpose, with his father barely in the ground.

He is being careful.

He is being his father, and he cannot hear himself doing it.

A man leaves his son a city and a philosophy, and the city can be defended. The other thing has to be taken out of him by hand.

I am the only one left who loves him enough to do it.

So: teeth, in a bright room, in front of witnesses. That is all it was ever meant to be.

Instead there is a woman. Who is she?

---

The wolf saw it, and the wolf is a fool. Fools see the useful part, because they do not know which part is useful.

She got him out.

Not carried but steered: she had him upright with his arm across her shoulder and she talked to him the whole way to the street.

At the door she stopped and looked back into the room before she took him through it.

She looked back to see whether anyone was following.

Why would a woman look back? I have turned that over all evening and it will not come apart in my hands.

A woman he has fed from does not look back. She walks to a cab with the evening gone soft behind her, and in the morning she is tired, and she does not remember a hallway.

That is the whole architecture of this city, and that is what the law is *for.*

This one was awake.

She was awake in the room and awake at the door, and she put the King of New Orleans in a car and drove him somewhere I do not have.

The boy does not make mistakes with his mouth, and he never has. He was fastidious about it before he was old enough to be dangerous.

He learned that from me.

So she is something, and I do not know what, and I find I do not enjoy the question.

---

There is a version of tonight in which I stop.

Aurelian is dead, and the war will come or it will not, and the boy will grow into whatever he is going to be.

I could take a house on the coast and be an old man who lost his friend. Who alive would ever put a hand on my shoulder and ask?

I have thought about the coast more often than I would have expected.

What is owed to me?

Nothing. That is the trouble. A man does not get a debt entered against the world for a daughter, nor against a friend who did what the statute told him to do, in a courtyard, with the court watching, while I stood in the grass and said his name.

He held my arm afterward, and he was gentle about it.

He kept his hand there and told me it was the law and not the man, and he meant it. Then he went on being the man for the rest of his life, and every year of it was a year he did not think about it.

He would have gone on not thinking about it until the world ended.

So there is no debt, only what I intend to leave behind.

A species that does not apologize, and a king with his father's hands and none of his father's fence around them.

I am going to find out who she is.

Then I am going to see the boy in the morning and ask after the health of a witch he has never met.

And I will be the last warm room he has.
