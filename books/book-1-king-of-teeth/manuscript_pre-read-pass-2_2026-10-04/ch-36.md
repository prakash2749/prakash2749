# 36 · Selene

My apartment smells like dust and a coffee I made weeks ago and never drank.

I came straight here from Barataria, where this morning I stood at the treeline and watched Delphine walk her brother to the edge of pack ground while the whole pack watched. He didn't look at his father once, and she was the only one who walked with him.

The plant on the windowsill is dead. It was dying when I left, and the leaving finished it. Rest in peace, you little bastard.

The knife is in my bag, where it's been since the shed, blade clean. I set the bag down next to the toaster with the intention of never opening it again.

---

I take it out. It's my father's, a short blade, wood handle, a nick in the bolster where somebody dropped it. The last time I sharpened it I was sitting on this floor with a king on my linoleum and an intention so clean I could see my own face in it.

The intention is gone, like a word you've been using wrong for years, until somebody shows you the real one.

I didn't kill Rémy. He's alive, and my parents are still dead, and the man who ordered it is dead too. I watched every second of that from a doorway.

Revenge promises you a clean floor at the end, and there's no clean floor.

---

I put the knife in the junk drawer, next to the batteries and a menu from a restaurant that closed ages ago, something you keep but stop carrying. That's the whole ceremony.

---

The bar is the same, and Antoine is behind the register. He looks up when I come in and his face lands somewhere between relief and the plain annoyance of a man who's been doing two people's work.

"You're alive," he says. "Where the hell have you been? I had to work a Saturday with Tanya. She sings, Selene. She sings to the limes."

"I'm alive."

"You look different."

"I've been sleeping somewhere else."

"Yeah, I can tell. You've got that look. Like a cat that found the good windowsill." He shuts the register. "Somewhere better?"

"Somewhere with higher ceilings. And worse coffee, holy shit. It's like drinking a tire."

"We're short tonight," he says, "because Tanya quit. Said the bar had a ghost."

"Did it?"

"Only on the nights you worked, apparently. Can you close? Or are you too fancy now?"

"Shut up, Antoine. I can close."

"And some wolf girl came in asking for you last week, young, jacket about three sizes too big."

"Did she leave a name?"

"She left a *tip.*" He looks personally offended. "Five dollars on a club soda, Selene. Who raised these people?"

---

The bar fills like it always does. At nine o'clock the crowd turns over, and the room becomes the machine I know how to operate.

A vampire comes in around ten, young, well-dressed, with the confidence of a man who enjoys what he is. He orders a bourbon and looks at me like they all look at bartenders, briefly, like furniture.

Then he looks again, and his nostrils flare.

"Have we met?" he says. "You smell familiar."

"I get that."

"From where, though? It's driving me crazy."

"Then I guess we've never met, sweetheart. Drink your fucking bourbon."

That's going to keep happening. He drinks and leaves, and the tip is extraordinary. I put it in the jar without looking at it.

---

After midnight the crowd thins and I'm alone behind the bar with two regulars and the television set.

Antoine comes out of the kitchen drying his hands and leans on the bar next to me.

"So," he says. "The tourist."

"What tourist?"

"Don't. Fifty-dollar bourbon, and he didn't drink it." He watches my face. "That's where you've been sleeping."

I don't answer, which is an answer.

"He treat you right? Because if he doesn't—"

I think about it, actually think about it, standing there with a rag in my hand, and he waits.

"He turns pages from the corner," I say.

Antoine stares at me. "I don't know what the fuck that means."

"It means yes."

"Then why do you look like you're about to cry in my bar? You cried once in the walk-in, and you told me it was onions."

"Fuck off, it's allergies."

"To what?"

"You, you nosy bastard."

He shakes his head and goes back to the kitchen, and at the door he stops.

"Girl," he says, without turning around. "You're allowed."

Then he's gone, and I'm standing behind my own bar with my eyes stinging, like an idiot.

The Code held, and that part of the story is finished. The rest isn't. Silas has been in the archive every night since the study, and my father left me a ledger with *for S, when she asks* written on the last page.

I'm not done asking.

---

I close up, count the register, and sit on my crate in the alley with my phone.

Three contacts that aren't the bar: my landlord, my dentist, and the woman in Tremé who sells me the tea. Lucian's already in there, saved under a name I'm not going to share with anyone.

I add a fourth: Delphine. She gave me her number at the treeline on the back of a gas receipt and said, "In case you need a wolf who's not my brother."

I'm not going to call it tonight. But for the first time in my life I have a wolf's number that isn't attached to an intention.

---

I walk home. A man on a corner is playing a trumpet with his case open. I pass the stretch of curb where I climbed into Lucian's lap in Silas's car and the horn went off, and I smile. It's a new one, not the bar smile. Nobody sees it, and that's fine.

---

The apartment is dark when I get home.

I lock the door, deadbolt and chain, same as every night since the window. I stand there with my hand on the chain and think about Lucian's house, where nothing closes all the way.

I take the chain off and leave the deadbolt, one lock, not two, and I go to bed.

---

The bed is mine. The window is the one that opens, and I leave it cracked, because the air outside smells like rain and cut grass.

Cassian's last word is still in the air. Somebody paid a broker named Cray, and I'm going to find out who.

And the last thing I think about before I sleep isn't the man I came here to kill.

It's the man I'm going back to in the morning.
