# I-4 · Cassian

*Placement: immediately after Ch. 25.*

The boy has stopped bringing me things.

He does not know that he has done it. It is not a decision he has made in a sentence — he is not yet capable of deciding to distrust me, and on the day he becomes capable of it he will be finished and so will I.

But he used to come and find me. At any hour. He would arrive with a thing in his hands and set it down in front of me and wait while I looked at it, and he has done that since he was young enough to need lifting onto the desk to do it.

This week he has come to me, and each time I asked the first question.

---

Tonight he lied to me in his father's study. He did it badly, and I let him.

I asked what Ambroise had found. I have asked every few days since the body came up out of the cold room, and every time he has told me a little and been glad to tell it, because a man carrying a thing like that wants hands underneath it.

Tonight he said: wolf bites. Heavy damage, and the worst of it at the hip.

Then he offered me a glass, which he does not do.

He is a poor liar because he has never had to practice. Aurelian raised him to be direct and I trained him to be still, and between us we made a man who cannot dress a falsehood.

His voice was perfect. His hands were perfect. He offered me a drink to give himself somewhere to look.

So he has seen the wound. He has known for weeks.

He has sat in a room with me most mornings since. He has asked after my house. He has let me put my hand on the back of his neck, and said nothing.

That is my boy.

That is not Aurelian's boy. Aurelian would have stood up in a full chamber on the first evening and said *one of you was in this room,* and taken whatever came, and called it honesty.

The son kept it in his pocket and went hunting.

I have never been prouder of him in my life. That is not a figure of speech and I am not going to soften it into one.

---

Here is what it cost me in that room.

He said *wolf bites* and looked at me, and I felt my own face arrange itself.

I did it well. That is the whole of the cost, and I did not know it was going to be the cost until it was already done.

My eyes went where they go. My hands stayed where they were. And something came up in my chest that was almost entirely real, because he was telling me that his father had been savaged by animals, and I have been listening to that sentence in this city for a month and it has not stopped working on me yet.

Then I said the things a man says. I asked whether he had slept. I told him his father would have wanted him to eat, which is true.

He put his hand over his eyes for a moment, the way he did as a child. I let him do it in front of me, because he needs a room where he can.

Every part of that was genuine. Every part of it was in the service of the lie.

There is no longer a seam between the two, and I have been doing this for a long time without ever having to feel it as a skill.

---

I could tell him tomorrow.

The thought arrives with its own furniture now, the way a habit does. It came tonight while he had his hand over his eyes.

He is closer than he knows. The woman is cleverer than he is about a certain kind of thing, and she has been quiet in a way I do not like.

There is a version where I say it first. Where I set it down myself, in a warm room, and let him have the whole of it out of my own mouth instead of off a page.

He would remember that I told him. Whatever he becomes, there would be a version of it in which the old man did not make him dig.

I have thought about that for longer than I thought about the blade.

---

In the morning I am going to move to force the vote. The boy has been standing in front of it with his arms out, and it will take me an afternoon to walk around him.

There is no permission for what comes next. I looked for it. It is not there.

A man who can no longer find the seam between the genuine and the lie does not get to ask whether he is permitted.

His father is dead.

I am what he has.
