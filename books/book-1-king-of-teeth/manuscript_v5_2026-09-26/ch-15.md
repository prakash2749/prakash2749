# 15 · Selene

The pact has terms and I'm already outside them.

The deal was simple. His help reaching Rémy, my blood freely given. Two clean exchanges, one for the other, and the kind of arrangement that makes sense in a room with a lamp on.

It doesn't make a lick of goddamn sense out here.

Out here is the Bywater, midmorning, with a list of addresses Silas gave me and a face that gets me into conversations I have no right to be in. Lucian's name gets me through front doors. Nobody loves him. They've just heard what happens to people who don't open up.

I've been working Rémy's edges for a week, never the man himself, only the space around him. The people who sell him fuel, the woman who stocks his bait, the bar where his crew drinks on the nights they come north of the canal.

Each one of them is a sentence in a language I've been learning for four years, and each one of them gives me a little more of the grammar.

Rémy Thibodeaux doesn't drink. Rémy Thibodeaux goes to church, which makes no sense for a wolf until you know that in pack, a public ritual is a claim. You put yourself in a building on a schedule so everyone who sees you knows you have a schedule and a building.

Rémy Thibodeaux has a sister who brings him lunch on Thursdays.

That one costs me. I sit in my car outside a bait shop on the Lafitte highway and think about Delphine, who told me I smelled like her aunt and gave me water, and who brings her brother lunch on the day he takes the boat out.

She's not part of this. I need that clean in my own head, and it won't stay clean.

---

The wolf at the bait shop knows things.

He doesn't know he knows them, and that's the best kind of source there is. He's old, wide, and he talks about the pack like old men talk about the weather. A permanent condition he's stopped trying to change.

"Thibodeauxs been on that water since before there was a dock," he says. He's cutting bait behind the counter and his knife is practiced and absent. "Before the city. Before all of it."

"Why do they stay?" I ask.

It's an innocent question. Making conversation, buying shrimp.

He stops cutting.

Just a hitch, as if a muscle in his back reminded him it's there, and then he goes on.

"Thibodeauxs have always been here," he says.

I wait for the rest of the sentence. The rest of the sentence doesn't come.

"Right," I say. "But the damn flooding alone — "

"This water's ours." He sets the knife down. "You don't leave water that's yours. That's — that's just how it is."

Flat certainty with nothing behind it, no stubbornness and no pride. A law of physics nobody wrote down.

I've heard this before. In the house, two nights ago, on the back stairs. One vampire telling another that the court doesn't relocate. Not for a season, not for a war, not ever. And the other one saying *of course not,* and neither of them asking why.

Different words, different species, same empty socket where the *why* should be.

Two species planted in this city like teeth in a jaw. Both certain they can't leave. Neither one with a reason they can carry to the end of a sentence.

Then I let it go, because I'm here about a man on a shrimp boat, and I can only carry one thing at a time.

---

Delphine is at the gas station on the corner of the highway.

I see her before she sees me — or before she lets me see her seeing me. She's at the pump, filling a truck that's too old to be worth the gas, and she's watching me like she watched me at the bench. Paying attention.

Should I drive past? I should. I should keep my eyes on the road and my foot on the pedal and add her to the list of things I know and keep going.

I pull in.

She watches me park and get out, and she doesn't move from the pump.

"You smell like him," she says.

"Like who, for Christ's sake?"

"Like the house." She pulls the nozzle out and hangs it up. "You've been sleeping there."

I don't answer, because the answer is in my skin and she already has it.

It's in more than my skin. There's a faint pink print on the inside of each wrist where his father's red tape held me, and I'm sore high between my thighs every time I shift my weight, and yesterday he came across my stomach on a dead king's desk. Can she smell that too? Christ, I hope not. I hope so.

"I could smell it at the bench." She looks at me as if she hasn't decided about me yet. "I thought maybe you'd been inside once. But it's on you now. In the skin."

"What the hell does it smell like?"

"Old. Cold. Like a room that's been closed too long." She screws the gas cap on. "And underneath that, iron. Like something that feeds."

The word *feeds* sits between us on the concrete.

"If I can smell it," she says, "everybody on that dock can smell it. The old man at the bait shop. Gaspard's people. Everybody."

"What the hell are you telling me?"

"I'm telling you that you walk through this territory smelling like whatever raided us three weeks ago, and the only reason nobody's said anything is because they can also smell that you're not one of them." She looks at me. "You're something else. I don't know what. But you walk through here like you belong here and you smell like you belong there, and that's a thing that gets a person noticed."

"I've been noticed?"

"By us. By the dock." She pauses. "Not by him. Not yet."

Rémy. She means Rémy.

"His nose isn't what it used to be," she says. "Too many years on the water. Salt kills it." She gets into the truck. "But it won't matter if you keep coming back. Somebody will tell him."

"Are you going to tell him?"

She starts the engine. She looks at me through the window for a long time.

"I bring him lunch on Thursdays," she says. "It's Wednesday."

She pulls out. I stand at the gas station and watch her go, and the sentence she left me is the clearest goddamn thing anyone in this city has said to me since I arrived.

She's giving me one day.

---

The addresses Silas gave me are careful.

Careful, which isn't the same as safe. Safe means nobody's watching. Careful means the person who put the list together thought about who might be, and routed me around them.

I can feel Silas in the list. The order of the addresses makes a circuit that avoids the main roads through wolf territory, stays in the human zones between pack and court, and doesn't cross any line that would put me in someone's jurisdiction.

He reads the city like I read a conversation.

What does he think of me? I've seen him twice — once at the house, when Lucian told him I was staying, and Silas looked at me as if I were a delivery he wasn't expecting and can't return. Once in the hall, where he handed me the list without a word and went up the stairs.

He doesn't like this arrangement. He doesn't like me in the house, me in the car, me between his king and whatever Silas thinks is coming.

He'll work with it anyway, because Lucian told him to, and Silas does what Lucian tells him to with the same precision he applies to everything — completely, silently, and with a set of opinions he keeps in a room nobody else has access to.

---

The last address on the list is a bar in Tremé. Neutral ground, more or less — the kind of place where wolves and the edges of the vampire world overlap without anyone acknowledging it.

I've been here before. I know the bartender. He's human, and he doesn't know what his clientele is, and he makes a good Old Fashioned, and he talks.

"Rémy? Sure." He wipes a glass. "He was in here last week. Tuesday, I think. He doesn't order. Just sits."

"By himself?"

"Sometimes. Sometimes with the big one — Gaspard's man. The one with the ear."

"Does the son of a bitch meet anybody?"

The bartender gives me a look. It's the look people give when they've been asked a question that sounds casual and isn't.

"He met a woman once," he says. "Month ago, maybe. She was — not his type. Too put-together. Nice coat."

"Not his type how?"

"Rich," he says. "I figured she was a lawyer."

A woman in a nice coat who met Rémy in a bar in Tremé a month ago.

I put it away in the other language, where sentences sit until I know which conversation they belong to.

---

I'm in the car on Esplanade when I notice the truck.

It's been behind me since the bait shop. Dark, no markings, and it keeps the same distance through three turns, which is one more than coincidence and one fewer than proof.

I take a fourth turn. Down Dauphine, which goes the wrong direction for home and the right direction for the Quarter.

The truck takes the turn.

My hands are steady on the wheel. That's a fact I'm going to hold onto.

I know how to be followed. I've known since I was twenty-two, when a wolf in a pickup trailed me from my apartment to the bus stop for three mornings in a row and then stopped. I never found out why the bastard stopped. I never found out why he started.

This isn't a wolf.

How the hell do I know that? I can't explain it. The truck is too far back and the windows are tinted and I can't smell anything through the car. But it moves patient. Unhurried. It knows it doesn't have to close the distance.

Wolves close the distance, because it's in their bodies. A wolf who's tracking you leans forward even in a car.

This is leaning back.

I take another turn, then another. I'm in the Quarter now, narrow streets, one-ways, and the truck has to commit or lose me.

It commits.

I pull over on Royal Street, in front of a shop that's closed, and I sit in the car with the engine running and watch the mirror.

The truck passes without slowing down, turns left on St. Philip, and disappears.

I sit there for a while and say *fuck* out loud to nobody. Twice.

Then I drive home, and I lock the door, and I push the dresser in front of it. I haven't done that since the first year.

The pact came with terms. Shitty ones. I didn't ask about the fucking fine print.

---

In the apartment I sit on the floor with my back against the dresser and think about the list of things I know and the list of things I don't.

I know Rémy is on the water and he doesn't drink and his sister brings him lunch. I know an old wolf can't finish a sentence about why the pack stays in Barataria. I know a woman in a nice coat met Rémy in a bar a month before the King died.

I know someone followed me in a truck that moved the way vampires move.

I don't know who killed the King. I don't know why the house-mark in Lucian's private correspondence is lodged in my head where I can feel it but can't reach it. I don't know why Lucian looks at me like a question he's decided to keep.

He calls me *ghost.* I told him I hate it. Liar. I don't hate it at all.

I don't know what I'm going to do when I finally get close enough to Rémy Thibodeaux to use the knife.

Four years ago the answer was simple. Now Delphine brings him lunch on Thursdays, and a wolf smelled iron on my skin, and I'm starting to wonder what's underneath the intention I've been standing on.

The phone buzzes. Lucian.

*Tomorrow. My father's study. Bring the ledger.*

I stare at the text. He means my father's ledger — the one I've had since the funeral. The one with the handwriting I'm not ready for.

I text back: *Which ledger.*

I know which ledger. I ask because asking is the only control I have left tonight, and the hell of it is he probably knows that too.

His reply is immediate. Of course it fucking is.

*The one you haven't opened.*

And there it is. He knows I have it. He knows I haven't opened it. He's been paying the kind of attention that doesn't require words.

I type one more.

*Someone followed me today. Dark truck. Yours?*

This time it takes him longer.

*No.*

Then, a second later: *Stay inside.*

I put the phone down and look at the dresser against the door.

Then I press my thumb to the pink line on my wrist, where the tape held me, and my whole body answers it like he's in the room. Two words from him on a screen and I'm wet on my own floor with a dresser against the door. What the hell has he done to me?

Someone is following me, and it isn't his.
