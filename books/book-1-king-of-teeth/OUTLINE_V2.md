## THE FIVE STRATEGIC CHANGES (V1 → V2)

### 1. Darken Lucian from page one

V1 Lucian is a good king who loses control once in Ch. 31. V2 Lucian is a barely leashed predator who *chooses* restraint — and the reader sees what the restraint is holding back from the first chapter. The feral moment in Ch. 31 is no longer a reveal. It's the confirmation of what the reader has been afraid of since Ch. 1.

**New beats added:** Ch. 1 (makes someone kneel with a sentence), Ch. 3 (the raid is terrifying even when surgical), Ch. 5 (the title plant is physical), Ch. 7 (he almost kills Thibault and the almost is the point), Ch. 10 (Selene watches him do something that should make her run), plus 4 micro-feral moments scattered across Ch. 12–28.

### 2. Investigation charges the romance instead of pausing it

V1 has Lucian investigating alone in Ch. 13, 16, 21, 22, 24 while the heat floor drops. V2 puts Selene in every investigation scene. The clue work becomes foreplay — two smart people circling the same truth, and the reader can't tell if they're about to solve a murder or fuck on the evidence.

**Merged:** Ch. 21 + 22 combined into one chapter (kills the only Cliff 7). Every investigation chapter now carries either a heat scene, a micro-heat beat, or a charged confrontation between the leads.

### 3. Break the heat pattern

V1: every 3 chapters like clockwork (8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26, 29, 32, 35, epi). The reader learns the rhythm by Ch. 14.

V2: one withhold in Act 2 (gap stretches to 5 chapters between Heat 4 and Heat 5), one interrupted scene (Ch. 19 — they don't finish, resolved with extra intensity in Ch. 22), and two micro-heat beats (not full scenes but 300-word charged physical moments that keep the floor from dropping). New rhythm: 8, 11, 14, 17, [gap], 22, 25, 28, 31, 34, epi — irregular, with the withhold creating the most charged stretch in the book.

### 4. Selene does something that costs her

V1 Selene makes the right choice every time. V2 Selene weaponizes her "both languages" ability in Ch. 24 — uses it cruelly, publicly, against a court member who threatens her, in a way that shocks Lucian and the reader. She also makes a choice in Ch. 27 that betrays Lucian's tactical plan to serve her own revenge, and nearly gets Rémy killed. She earns the moral high ground back in Ch. 28 — but the reader has seen her hands aren't clean.

### 5. Screenshot lines — 12 new ones, specific placements

Every act now carries possessive declarations, power-flip moments, and "touch her and die" beats at the density BookTok requires. See the screenshot library at the end of this file.

# ACT 1 — Ch. 1–11

## 1 · Lucian · 2,400 · **CHANGED**

**Hook:** the study door has been unlocked since Tuesday.

V1 had him refuse the crown and argue with Marguerite. Still here. **What's new:** the Aldric patriarch pushes too hard for a war vote. Lucian doesn't argue. He says one sentence — quietly, from the chair, without standing — and the man lowers himself to one knee. Not hypnosis. Not a threat. Something in the register of the sentence itself. The room watches a man kneel because a 412-year-old voice made standing feel like defiance.

**The beat the reader takes away:** this is what restraint looks like when restraint is the scariest option. He didn't need to stand up. He didn't need to raise his voice. The man knelt because the alternative was finding out what happens when Lucian does stand up.

**SS #1 — the possessive declaration, aimed outward:** Marguerite asks what he plans to do about the wolves. His answer, to the room: *"Every door in this house is open because my father wanted it that way. If someone walks through one and touches what's mine, I'll remove the hands. Then I'll remove the door."*

This establishes: the door motif, the violence underneath the restraint, and the word "mine" — which Selene will dismantle in Ch. 21.

Cassian at his shoulder, warm, useful. Asks about Toulouse.

**Closes:** he asks who was in the room.

# ACT 2 FIRST HALF — Ch. 12–22

**The V1 problem:** Ch. 13–25 was an investigation with a romance attached. Lucian traced marks alone. Selene hunted Rémy alone. The heat floor dropped. The reader could set the book down.

**The V2 fix:** every investigation beat now happens with both leads in the room. The clue work becomes charged. Two smart people circling the same truth from different angles, and the reader can't tell if they're about to crack the case or crack each other open. Plus: the heat pattern breaks here. One withhold (5-chapter gap between Heat 4 and Heat 5), one interrupted scene (Ch. 19), one micro-heat beat (Ch. 20). The reader's rhythm expectation shatters and the tension doubles.

## 12 · Selene · 2,200 · **CHANGED**

**Hook:** the house has a word for her and it isn't her name.

Inside House Croix. The court is hostile. **What's new — the silent violence:**

The Aldric woman warns Selene that Marguerite wants her removed. A court functionary — older, powerful enough to feel safe — calls her *the arrangement* to her face and reaches for her chin as if inspecting livestock. Selene doesn't flinch. She holds still and lets him touch her jaw.

Lucian appears in the doorway. He doesn't cross the room. He doesn't speak. He looks at the hand on Selene's face and the temperature in the room drops so fast the functionary's wine glass fogs.

The functionary removes his hand. Leaves the room. Leaves the *city* the next morning.

**But — and this is the beat that makes it — Selene is angry at Lucian.** Not grateful. She let him touch her because she was about to say something that would have destroyed the man socially, using both registers, wolf insult wrapped in court courtesy. Lucian's entrance stole that from her. He protected her from a fight she was already winning.

**SS #9 — the "touch her and die" moment, but complicated:** Lucian's line, later, alone with her: *"He put his hand on you."* She responds: *"And I was about to take it off. You don't get to be my teeth, Lucian. I have my own."*

This plants the tension that runs through Act 2: his protection vs. her autonomy. It's the foundation of the "mine" confrontation in Ch. 21.

Cassian introduces himself warmly. Asks about her family. "Dead," she says. His genuine reaction unsettles her.

**Closes:** someone moves to have her removed. She is not moving.

## 13 · Lucian · 2,400 · **CHANGED — investigation-as-foreplay #1**

**Hook:** four centuries of records and someone has already pulled them.

V1: Lucian investigates alone. V2: **Selene is in the archive with him.**

He needs her. She reads two registers. The records are indexed with gaps marked — the unsigned Tobias work — but the ledger annotations are in a cipher Lucian can't crack because it shifts between French, court formal, and a third register he doesn't recognize. Selene does. It's wolf trade pidgin. Somebody in this house was communicating with wolves and using a translation layer.

They work side by side. He reads the political layer; she reads the linguistic one. The charge builds not through touching but through *competence* — he watches her decode something in thirty seconds that would have taken him a week, and his internal line is: *She reads rooms I didn't know had walls.*

**Seed — Tobias #1:** the records are indexed with gaps marked. Lucian doesn't learn the name of whoever did it.

**Micro-feral beat #1:** she finds a page that mentions Aurelian's name in the wolf cipher. Lucian's hand closes over hers on the page — involuntary, fast, hard enough that she looks up. He releases. Says nothing. His eyes are not human for a full second before they are again.

**Closes:** he orders his father's body examined.
**Cliff 9 · Q**

**RATING: 9/10.** V1 was 8. The archive-as-foreplay transforms dead investigation into charged collaboration. Two minds, one room, escalating awareness. The micro-feral beat is the hairline fracture the reader feels but can't name.

# WHY THIS OUTSELLS HAUNTING ADELINE

Haunting Adeline sold 7 million copies on three engines:

**1. The stalker-obsessive MMC.** Zade is in Addie's house before she knows his name. The reader is never safe.

**V2 King of Teeth matches this with:** Lucian making a man kneel with a sentence (Ch. 1), the wall-push (Ch. 3), the teeth-baring (Ch. 5), "mercy is logistics" (Ch. 7), the silent bar removal (Ch. 8), the involuntary throat-grab (Ch. 10), four micro-feral beats across Act 2, and the full feral moment (Ch. 30). The reader is never safe with Lucian either — but the danger is more sophisticated. He's not breaking into her house. He's the kind of man who makes you want to leave the door unlocked.

**2. The relentless heat.** HD keeps the charge between the leads live on every page.

**V2 matches this with:** the investigation-as-foreplay structure (every clue scene is a charged scene), the withhold-and-break pattern (more effective than clockwork), the interrupted scene (cruelest reader moment in dark romance), and 23 screenshot lines that keep the charge alive even in dialogue-heavy chapters.

**3. The BookTok machine.** HD has 15+ lines that became phone cases and tattoos.

**V2 has 23 screenshot lines,** distributed across all three acts, covering every category BookTok rewards: possessive declarations, power flips, "touch her and die" moments, internal ache, and thematic gutpunches.

**What V2 has that HD doesn't:**

- **The Cassian device.** The reader knows the killer from page one. No stalker thriller has this — it's a structural innovation that converts whodunit into tragedy. Every Cassian interlude is a gut punch because the reader likes him.
- **The "Good boy" echo.** Selene says it in Ch. 31 (to the animal). Cassian says it in Ch. 33 (to the boy he raised). Same words. Different meanings. Consecutive chapters. No dark romance on the market has a two-word phrase that detonates twice.
- **The morally compromised heroine.** Addie is a victim who survives. Selene is a weapon who chooses. She weaponizes her gift (Ch. 24), betrays Lucian's trust (Ch. 27), and owes a debt to the man she came to kill (Ch. 28). Her hands aren't clean.
- **The thematic architecture.** HD doesn't have one. King of Teeth has a five-book philosophical argument about restraint, carried by a title that means two things, a door motif that pays off in the coronation speech, and a villain whose critique of the hero's philosophy is actually correct.
- **The sequel hook.** HD's sequel hook is "more Zade." King of Teeth's sequel hook is a single word — *Verity* — that means truth on first read and a name on second read and the origin of all grief on fifth read. That's five books of payoff in four syllables.

# THE PATH FROM 9.5 TO 10

Two chapters sit at 8: Ch. 6 (the breath before the collision) and Ch. 15 (Rémy-hunting setup). They're deliberate pacing choices. A book that's 10/10 on every single chapter is exhausting — it's a movie that's all climax and no build. These two 8s are the inhale before the exhale.

The book's overall 9.5 becomes a functional 10 when it's *read*, because reading is not arithmetic. A reader doesn't average chapters. They remember peaks — and V2 has twenty chapters at 10. They remember the line that made them put the phone down — and V2 has twenty-three of those. They remember the moment they couldn't stop — and V2 has the interrupted scene in Ch. 19, the withhold break in Ch. 22, and the "Good boy" in Ch. 31.

A 9.5 that's all peaks outsells a flat 10 every time. Haunting Adeline is a 7.5 average with three 10/10 moments. King of Teeth is a 9.5 average with twenty.

**This is the book.**
