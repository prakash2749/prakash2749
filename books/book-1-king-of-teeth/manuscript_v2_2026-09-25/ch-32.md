# 32 · Selene

I wake up in the west wing with Lucian's cold still on my skin and something wrong in the house.

It's an absence of sound. The silence a building makes when everyone in it has stopped what they were doing and started cleaning up.

I sit up. The bed is empty. He left without waking me. The frame beside the bed is still crooked on the wall where my back hit it. There are marks on my throat I can feel without a mirror, and a bite on my breast that's gone dark, and my thighs ache in a way I'm going to feel every time I sit down today.

For a breath the only thing in my head is the floor. God. The floor. His face with nothing behind it. My blood going back into him.

Then Bastien.

He came in and said one word and it was *Bastien,* and I've been asleep. How the fuck have I been asleep?

---

I smell it before I reach the stairs.

Old blood. Something animal underneath it, wet and resinous, the smell of bodies opened in a room that wasn't built for it.

I pull on the shirt I was wearing and walk barefoot down the corridor, and the house is wrong. Occupied in a new way. Footsteps below, moving with purpose. A door closing that doesn't usually close.

The kitchen door is open — the delivery door, the one that faces the service alley — and there's a man standing in it I don't know.

He's one of Silas's people. I know because he has the stance. Hands loose, weight forward. A man taught to stand in doorways by someone who believed doorways were the point.

He sees me and his mouth tightens.

"Ma'am—"

"What happened?"

"You should—"

"What the fuck *happened?*"

He doesn't answer. He steps aside, which is an answer.

The kitchen is worse. The smell has weight in here — thick, iron-sweet, coating the back of my throat. Blood on tile holds its heat longer than blood on wood, and this floor is still warm with it.

There's blood on the fucking floor. Less a pool than a pattern. It goes from the kitchen door to the dining room in a line that tells a story about how a body moved through a space it was never meant to be moving through.

I follow it.

---

The small dining room is destroyed.

The word isn't enough. The table is against the wall. Thrown there, the legs snapped and the top split down the center. Jesus. The chairs are pieces. There's a hole in the plaster where a body went through it and kept going, and the shape of the hole tells me what made it.

A body. Somebody went through that wall backwards, and the lath behind the plaster is splintered outward, and through the wall is a room I've never been in with a stain on the floor I'll never ask about.

The floor is a map.

I can read it like I read a conversation. What happened, and what's underneath. Five distinct blood patterns, and each one tells a story. One long drag from the door to the wall. Two pooling marks where bodies fell and stayed. One spray across the ceiling that arcs from the fireplace to the window — a carotid, opened at speed, and the body was still turning when it bled out.

And one clean space. Body-shaped. Where Bastien fell and someone, Silas I think, laid him straight.

There are men in the room. Silas's people, moving quietly, doing the work that comes after. One of them has a bucket. The water in it is red. One of them is taking the pantry door off its hinges. It has claw marks gouged into it, deep furrows from a wolf that shifted mid-fight and tried to get through to whatever was on the other side.

A tooth on the floor. Wolf. The root still attached, pulled out of the jaw it grew in.

The door to the pantry is standing open.

And in the middle of all of it, on the floor, with his throat open to the spine, is Bastien.

Someone has drawn a sheet over him, up to the chest.

---

I don't make a sound.

I'm standing in a doorway looking at the only person in this house who ever grinned at me, and he's on the floor with his throat gone, and I don't make a sound.

God. His hands are folded. Somebody arranged them, carefully, like you'd straighten a collar. His eyes are closed. His face has the look of a thing that was still deciding what expression to make when the decision was taken away from it.

He was eighty-nine fucking years old. He worshipped Lucian. He carried papers into rooms where nobody asked for them, and he called me a conduit because I told him it was nicer than a drain.

He held a doorway. I can see it in the scuff marks on the floor, and in how the blood falls around a space where a body stood and didn't move. He put himself between the wolves and whoever was behind him, and he held it.

I go to him.

I kneel on the floor beside him, and the blood soaks into my jeans, and I don't care about the jeans.

"You held it," I say.

He doesn't hear me. He's been gone for hours and the room is cold. Goddamn it, Bastien.

"You held it, Bastien."

One of Silas's men looks at me. He doesn't say anything. He goes back to the bucket.

---

Lucian is in the study.

I know because Silas told me. Silas, who appeared in the kitchen doorway with his hands in his coat and a face I've never seen on him. Under grief. Under anger. Down where the machinery is.

"He's upstairs," Silas said. "Don't go in."

"Is he hurt? Silas. Is he fucking hurt?"

"He's not hurt." A pause. "Don't go in."

I go in. Fuck Silas.

The study door is open. It's always open. The lamp is on and the desk is clean and the chair — the guest chair, the one Lucian turned around this morning — is facing the desk.

There's a hole in the wall.

A fist-shaped depression in the plaster beside the window, clean, precise, with the knuckle marks still visible in the dust. One strike. None of the fury from the dining room. A single measured punch into a wall that gave like paper.

He did this before the kitchen. Before the wolves came through the delivery door, before Bastien held the doorway. He was standing here in the study after some piece of news I wasn't here for, and he put his fist through the wall.

And then he pulled it out, and flexed the fingers, and walked to the next meeting.

I know this because there's no blood on the plaster. No rage in the mark. Just the precise impression of a hand that needed to go through a wall, and the wall was closer than the man who deserved it.

The last crack before the dam.

Lucian is at the window.

His back is to me. There's blood on his hands, most of it not his, and he's still wearing the ruined shirt I tore open last night.

He doesn't turn around.

"Silas told you not to come in, ghost," he says.

"Silas tells me a lot of shit."

"This time he was right."

I cross the room. I stand behind him, close enough to touch him, and I don't touch him.

"Bastien," I say.

"Yes."

"He held the doorway."

"He held it." His voice is level. "He put two humans behind him and he held it, and he'd never been in a fight that mattered, and he held it until I got there."

"And after?"

He doesn't answer. The blood on his hands is drying, and the gray morning through the window puts his face in a light I have to be careful about reading.

"Lucian."

"I killed them all." He says it like he took the garbage out. "Six wolves. One of them had shifted back. He put his hands down."

"And?"

"And I took his jaw off." He turns. He looks like he's looked at himself and found what he expected. "Because he was talking."

Jesus.

I hold his eyes. I don't flinch. I don't step back. I don't do any of the things a body does when it's standing in front of something that just told it what it's capable of.

"And the pantry," he says.

"What about the pantry?"

"There was a woman in the pantry. The linen girl. She was behind Bastien when he held the door." His hands open and close at his sides. "I went for the pantry."

"Did you open it?"

"Silas stopped me."

The room goes still.

"He said my name," Lucian says. "*Sire.* One word. From the hall. And I turned around, and there she was behind the door, and I had my fucking hand on it."

He looks at his hands. The blood is dark on them, almost black in the lamplight.

"I wanted to keep going," he says. "Not that I did it. That I wanted more of it. The pantry door was the next door and I was going to open it."

I take his hands.

He lets me. They're cold and stiff and the blood on them is someone else's, and I hold them like he held my father's ledger. From the edges. Something that matters.

"You stopped," I say.

"Silas stopped me."

"You turned around." I tighten my grip. "A man who can't be stopped doesn't turn around because someone says his name. You turned around because the name was enough."

"That's a generous reading, ghost."

"That's the correct reading." I look up at him. "I'm a translator. Trust the translation."

His jaw loosens. Whatever's been holding since he walked into the dining room lets go, one fraction at a time.

"Bastien was eighty-nine," he says.

"I know."

"He called me *sire* every time I told him not to."

"I know."

"He grinned when I corrected him."

"I know, Lucian."

He puts his forehead against mine. His eyes are closed and his breath is uneven and his hands in mine are shaking.

"They came through the kitchen," he says. "Somebody let them in."

"I know."

"The same hand. Every time. The same hand on the same door."

I hold his hands. The house holds still around us. Somewhere below, men are washing a wall and taking a door off its hinges.

"Tomorrow," I say.

"Tomorrow."

"He comes at the usual hour."

"He comes at the usual hour."

I let go of his hands. I go to the desk and I pick up the blade — the one that was his father's, the one that's keyed to one man's blood — and I put it in his coat, in the pocket where it lives.

"Go sleep," he says.

"You won't."

"No."

I leave him in the study with the lamp on and the chair turned around and the blood drying on his hands. The hallway is dark and the house smells like what happened in it tonight, and I go to the west wing and I shut the door.

For the first time since I moved in, I shut the door all the way.

Then I open it again.

Because a door standing open is a decision, and tonight I'd rather decide than be safe.
