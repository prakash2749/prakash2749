# 1 · Lucian

The door to my father's study has been unlocked since Tuesday.

It goes on being the only thing I can think about. A man died in this house on Tuesday. Nobody has turned a key in this door since.

I could fix it in an afternoon. A new lock, a new key, one instruction to Silas.

I sit down in my father's chair instead and look at the door from the inside. He never once looked at it from in here.

He kept this room open because he wanted people to come in. That was a decision. He made it every morning for three hundred years and never once thought of it as a risk.

The people who could walk through it without knocking could be counted on one hand. Every one of them was chosen. Every fucking one.

Chosen is the word I keep getting caught on.

---

They come for me before noon.

Marguerite first, because Marguerite is always fucking first. She is my father's sister and twice my age. She has held the council together since Tuesday with nothing but a voice and the correct use of the word *traditionally.*

"You look terrible," she says.

"I've been traveling."

"You've been sitting in that chair." She crosses to the window and opens the shutter I closed. "Ambroise says you haven't fed."

"Ambroise talks."

"Ambroise is a physician, and you are the last Croix, and if you'd like a list of the things I've had to say out loud this week to keep that from sounding like a problem, I can provide one."

She doesn't sit. She never sits in here. She stands where she can see the door, and at eight hundred years old it isn't a decision anymore.

Then the rest of them arrive behind her, and the room stops being my father's and becomes a chamber.

---

Aldric is the first one to speak, because Aldric is always the first one to speak when there isn't a king in the room.

He's been old since before I was born. Centuries of occupying chairs and calling it governance, and this week he finally has the room he's wanted — one with no one at the desk.

"The wolves did this," he says. Doesn't sit. Stands where my father's rug meets the hardwood. A small thing, a placement thing, and he doesn't know I see it. "The wolves came in through the gallery and the wolves took the King. The court requires an answer."

"Which court."

"The court that buried him, boy."

The room tightens. *Boy.* From a man who couldn't hold a fucking door against me if I leaned on it with one hand. Every head in this chamber moves, and not toward him. Toward me.

I stay in the chair. I don't need to stand for this.

"Say that again."

He blinks. "Which—"

"The last word."

The silence in this room is the kind you can feel against your teeth. Marguerite hasn't moved. Nobody has. They're waiting to find out which half of my father I got, and this is the moment they get to look.

"I misspoke," Aldric says.

"You didn't." I look at him. Just look. Nothing in my face he can use as a reason and nothing he can use as an excuse. "You wanted to see what would happen."

He doesn't kneel because I told him to. That's the part the room will misremember. Let the bastards. I didn't say a word about it. I didn't raise my voice. I didn't stand.

He kneels because standing started to feel like a decision, and he wasn't ready to make one.

"The wolves are not your concern tonight," I tell him. "They're mine."

---

Here's what they want, and none of them say it in one sentence, so I'll say it for them.

They want a crown on me by the end of the week.

Marguerite makes the case properly. The city has no king. The continent has noticed. Three Houses that owe House Croix nothing have sent condolences worded as questions. And there is a version of next month where somebody tests whether this city has a door on it at all.

All of that is true. She is not being dishonest with me and she never has been.

What she leaves out is the rest of it. A crowned king can be advised. An uncrowned heir sitting in a dead man's chair cannot be advised. He can only be waited on. Marguerite has been waiting since Tuesday and she does not enjoy it.

"There's no contest to your claim," she says. "You're not weighing that. There isn't anyone to contest it *with.*"

"I'm aware."

"Are you? Vale hasn't a son. The Aldrics have centuries of cousins and not one child among them." She says it flatly, like a shipping notice. "You are what this House has. A born heir, and every one of us in this room has been alive long enough to know what that's worth and how rarely it happens."

"Say it again."

She stops. "Which part?"

"That there's nobody to contest it with."

"There is nobody to contest it with."

I turn that over while she watches me do it.

Because it cuts the other way, and I don't think she's looked at the other edge. If there is nobody who gains the city by my father's death, then nobody killed him to get the city.

Which means whoever did it wanted something else.

---

Bastien is in the corner with a folder he hasn't been asked for.

He's the youngest creature in this room by centuries, and he came in behind Marguerite carrying paper. Nobody does that. Paper is for clerks. He's been holding it against his chest for the whole conversation and waiting for a gap.

"Sire," he says, into the first one.

"No."

"Sorry. My lord. Your—" He gives up on it. "I pulled the door log. For the house. Who came and went, the week of."

Marguerite's head turns like a gun.

"Nobody asked you to do that," she says.

"No, ma'am."

"Then why have you done it?"

And he looks at me instead of at her.

He's decided, in the last second, who he answers to. I watch him decide it. I know what it will cost him.

"Because he asked who was in the room," Bastien says. "In the chamber. He asked it on the first night and nobody wrote it down."

The room does a thing I don't like. It closes a little.

"Give it to Silas," I tell him. "Not to me. Not to anybody else in this house. Silas."

"Yes."

"And Bastien."

"Sire?"

"You were right to pull it."

He goes out of the room taller than he came into it.

---

"He's a child," Marguerite says.

"He's a hundred years old."

"He's eighty-nine, and he worships you, and you have just taught him that initiative is rewarded in a house where three people have died this month." She turns from the window. "Lucian. The crown. Damn it, please."

"No."

"Give me a reason I can carry out of this room."

"Grief," I say. "Use grief. They'll take it."

"They will take it." She doesn't blink. "It isn't what it is."

And there it is, and I'd forgotten she's my father's sister and not my father's courtier.

"No," I say. "It isn't."

---

So here is the reason, and she will get one sentence of it and no more.

The moment they put that thing on my head, the first business of my reign is a war vote. It's already drafted. Somebody in this house has had the fucking thing drafted since Wednesday. I could name the men who'd move it, and the ones who'd second it before the wax cooled.

And the moment that vote passes, the question of who killed Aurelian Croix is answered forever. Not investigated — *answered.* The wolves did it. It goes into the record in those words, and every year after that, anybody who reopens it is a man insulting a war.

Uncrowned, I am a son with a grievance and no procedure. I can ask anything. I can ask it of anyone.

Crowned, I'm the one thing in this city that isn't allowed to wonder.

"I'll take the crown when I have a killer," I tell her.

"They'll read that as mourning."

"Yes."

"They'll read it as weakness in about a month."

"Also yes."

She looks at me for a long moment, and her jaw tightens. She's deciding whether to say something she can't take back.

"Your father would have taken it on the first night," she says. "And then done as he pleased."

"My father had four centuries of everybody assuming he was decent. I have a room full of people wondering which half of him I got."

That lands. She lets it.

"One month," Marguerite says. "I can hold them a month. After that I'll be holding them against their own interest, and I am not going to do that for you."

"Understood."

"Do you have anything? Anything at all, that I could tell a man in confidence to keep him quiet?"

"Tell him this." I look at the door. My father's door, open since Tuesday. "Every door in this house is open because my father wanted it that way. If someone walks through one and touches what's mine, I'll remove the hands. Then I'll remove the door."

She says nothing for three seconds, which from Marguerite is a standing ovation.

"That's not evidence."

"It's not meant to be."

"Then find the evidence." She is already moving toward the door. "And Lucian — the Code is going to be cited at you. Repeatedly, and by people who have never read it. Be ready for that."

"By whom?"

"Everyone who wants something." She pauses with her hand on the frame. "Your father talked about the Code as though it were a promise he'd made. Nobody else in this city has ever heard it that way. To the rest of them it's a rulebook with a penalty schedule, and you will be quoted clause and article by men who think the whole of it is a lever."

"And you?"

"I think it kept us alive this long and I have never cared why." She goes out. "Eat something."

---

Cassian comes in after the room empties. He always does.

He waits until the noise has gone out of a place, and then he's in the doorway. He does not knock. He has never had to.

"Boy."

"Come in."

He looks at the chair I'm in and doesn't say anything about it. He crosses and takes the seat across the desk, and he sits down like a man with nowhere else to be today.

My shoulders let go.

"She's been at you about the crown," he says.

"She's right."

"She's very nearly right, which is worse." He settles back. "How is Ambroise? I saw him in the hall and he looked eighty years old."

"He's been in the cold room since Tuesday."

"Somebody should send him home. He had a wife, you know. Long time ago. He doesn't do well in a house where there's been a death and nobody talks to him about anything but the corpse."

I had not thought about that once, all week. Fuck.

"I'll go down and see him," I say.

"Take him upstairs and make him drink something. He'll refuse twice." He waves a hand. "Then he'll tell you more than his report does. He always does. He told your father more than his reports ever did."

That's useful. It's genuinely fucking useful, and he's handed it over like a coat.

"Cassian."

"Mm."

"Where were you? In the chamber."

He doesn't hesitate and he doesn't perform the thinking. "Behind the west range of columns. With Thibault's people coming through the gallery I went for the platform, and by the time I got there your father was already down."

"Who was closest to him?"

"I was." He says it evenly. "I was the first one to reach him. I've been asking myself the same question all week, and the honest answer is that I don't know what happened in the last part of it, because I was looking at the wolves and not at him."

"Nobody was."

"No." A pause. "Nobody looks at anything else, in a room with wolves in it."

He leans forward and puts his hand on my forearm. Briefly. Then he takes it back.

"How is your mother's house?" he asks. "In Toulouse. Do you still keep it?"

"Standing. I haven't been."

"Go, when this is finished."

"I will."

"You won't." He stands, and smiles, and it reaches his eyes, because with him it always does. "But you should."

---

He goes, and the door doesn't close behind him. It has been open since Tuesday, and nobody in this house is going to be the one who shuts it.

I sit in my father's chair with a lamp on in the afternoon and I go through it one more time.

Wolves came in through the gallery windows. That's established, that's witnessed, and three hundred people saw the glass come down.

Wolves did not open the east range from the inside.

Somebody in this house did. That person had a key, or never needed one.

And my father would have walked past him on the way to his own death without putting a hand up.

Marguerite wants a crown. The court wants a war. Cassian wants me to go and be kind to an old physician who lost a wife a long time ago. He's right. I'm going to do it.

And underneath all of it there's one question. I've asked it twice now. Both times somebody has answered me about the fucking wolves.

I'm not going to be crowned over my father's body.

I'm going to be crowned over somebody else's.

So I get up, and I go and find Silas, and I ask him the only thing I want to know.

Who was in the room?
