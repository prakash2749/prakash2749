# 10 · Selene

There's a vampire on my kitchen floor and he's healing wrong.

I know it as you know a sound in your own house is wrong, even though I have nothing to compare it to, because I have never seen one of them hurt. Holy shit.

But his forearm is open where he took it into my doorframe coming through. It was open an hour ago, and it is open now.

He bled on the linoleum and the blood is drying, and that should be impossible. Everything I have ever been told about them says he should have closed before he hit the ground. So why hasn't he?

He's on his side with one arm under him and his eyes shut, breathing, but not on any schedule a person breathes on.

I got him up the stairs by putting his arm over my shoulders and lying to a neighbor about a wedding.

"Big night?" Mrs. Guidry said from her doorway, in her robe.

"Cousin's wedding," I said. "He found the open bar."

"On a Tuesday?"

"They're Catholic. What can you do?"

"Mm. He's very pale, baby. Is he going to be sick on my landing?"

"Not if he knows what's good for him."

God knows what I looked like.

---

Nobody came up behind us. Why not?

I sat in the car at the bottom of my own street with the engine running and watched the mirror until my fingers stopped shaking. Nothing turned in after me, no headlights, no man walking.

That doesn't mean anything good, because a wolf who wanted to follow me wouldn't need to do it tonight.

He has my face. How long before he uses it? He got a long look at it across a room. I gave it to him for free, because I stopped at a door to be sure of what I was already sure of.

Pack separates being seen from being caught. What he did was *place* me, put me somewhere on the map in his head, so that later, when there's a reason, he knows which way to walk.

---

Here's what I keep coming back around to.

He gave the order to his own people and he sent them out the front, loudly, to be looked at.

I've been turning it over since the parking lot. He was going down, he knew it, and the last clean decision he made was to move every set of eyes in that room onto two young men going out through the front door.

Then he went into a service corridor with a bartender. Why a bartender?

A king holds onto his guards and gets carried out through the middle of the floor.

Unless he'd worked out that the problem was inside the building, and that his people were the wrong thing to be standing next to.

I don't like how fast he got there, when I had all night to get there and he had about as long as it takes to put a palm on a table. Who thinks like that while they're dying?

---

I get the first aid box out from under the sink, and then I stand there holding it and feel like a fucking idiot.

There is nothing in this box for a man like that: adhesive bandages, a tube of burn cream, a roll of gauze I bought when I was twenty-two and cut my thumb on a bottle opener.

Well, shit. I take the gauze out anyway, and I fold a towel under his head and put the water where he can reach it if he wakes up and can't sit up.

Then I sit down against the cabinets with my knees up and look at him, a fucking vampire.

"You'd better not die in here," I tell him. "My shitty lease has a clause. Do you hear me?"

He doesn't answer. Why would he?

"It says no pets," I tell him anyway. "I figure you count. Don't you?"

And I go and get the knife.

---

It's my father's, out of a drawer of his things. It's nothing special: a short blade with a wood handle and a nick out of the bolster where somebody dropped it.

I've had it in the top of my bag for years and sharpened it more times than I've used it, and that's a sentence about a person.

It was always for Rémy Thibodeaux.

I sit on my kitchen floor with it in my hand and look at the King of New Orleans laid out like a delivery.

Under the ribs: that's what I know from the funeral talk and the club talk and every drunk at my bar with a theory. That's where they say you have to put it, and you have to mean it, and you have to be able to hold it there.

He can't stop me, and that's not a guess. He couldn't get himself into a car without me folding him into it.

And here's the honest version, because I'm the only one in this room awake enough to say it.

I could put this in him right now and be the person who killed a vampire king.

The wolves would sing about it, and some of them would sing about me and not know they were doing it, because they'd never believe a woman who smells wrong did anything that mattered.

His court would take the city apart to find out who, and they'd be goddamn thorough about it. It would take them a while, and it would not take them forever.

That's a reason, but it isn't the reason, not even close.

---

The reason is that he's the only way across I have. I'm not going to pretend to be better than that.

Four years of knowing exactly where a man lives and never once being able to cross the water to get to him. Four years of learning a pack's whole grammar from the outside, so I'd have the right thing to say to whichever one of them let me in.

Nobody let me in. Not fucking once.

Then tonight the man who owns this entire city put his mouth on my throat and *couldn't take it back out of me,* and now he's on my floor and he needs me.

That's not luck, and I don't believe in it and I wouldn't want it.

It's a boat at the dock with the engine running. I've been standing on the shore long enough to know you get in the one that comes.

So: I don't kill him because I need him. Is that the whole of it?

That's what I'd say if somebody asked, and it's true: clean and ugly and the kind of true you can say out loud at your own kitchen table.

---

Here's the part I wouldn't say.

He looked at me.

Wolves look at me fast, then decide to be somewhere else, because whatever I am reads to them as spoiled.

Humans don't even do that much, because humans look right through the middle of me. They always have, and I'm so tired of it I've stopped noticing I'm tired.

A week ago he sat at my bar and didn't see me either.

Tonight he came across a room to find out what I was. What am I, to him? A problem. A meal that talked back.

And when he couldn't put me back the way he found me, he didn't call anybody or have me taken. He stood in a hallway with my blood on his teeth and looked at me like I'd gone off in his hand.

Then I asked him whether he was a vampire.

He didn't answer, but he turned around to be asked. He had to decide to do that, and I watched him decide it.

To a wolf, turning to face the one who called you is the whole of it: you don't have to answer, because turning *is* the answer. It means *I acknowledge you have standing to ask me something.*

Nobody has ever given me standing, not once, in either language.

Jesus. Not once.

And I am sitting on my own floor with a knife in my hand, deciding not to use it, because a four-hundred-year-old predator turned around in a hallway. How pathetic is that?

That's not a good reason, and I know exactly what it is.

I put the knife on the counter behind me, out of reach of the floor. Sense or cowardice? I'm not sorting that out tonight.

His dick is hard against his hip, and I can see the shape of it through the wool even with him half-dead on my floor.

I make myself stop looking at it, and then I look at it again, and then I look at the ceiling and ask God what exactly He was going for with me. Was this a joke?

---

He wakes up a little before the sky does.

I hear it as a change in the breathing, and then his fingers move on the linoleum, flat, testing the surface.

I go very still against the cabinets. Oh, shit.

His eyes open and they're not right — there's a slowness behind them. It takes him too long to find me, and when he does he doesn't move his head.

He looks at the room first, his eyes going to the front door and then to the window over the sink and then to the one at the end of the hall.

Then to my bedroom door, standing open, the way it always is.

He looks at that one longer than he looked at the windows. Why that door?

Only after all of that does he look at me.

And then he moves.

I don't see the middle part: he's on the floor and then his hand is on my throat against the cabinet and his face is close enough that I can see what's behind his eyes, and it isn't a person.

He doesn't squeeze, his palm sitting over my pulse like he's reading a page he found open.

One second, maybe less, and his nostrils flare and I understand: he smells the wolf in me. His body got there before his mind did, and his body's answer to *wolf* is this, a hand on the nearest throat and the question afterward.

He lets go.

He steps back, or tries to, and barely makes it clear of me before his arm gives and he catches himself on the counter edge. The effort it costs him to stand there and not fall is worse to look at than the hand was.

He doesn't say anything about it, just moves his jaw once and sets it, and whatever he was going to say goes behind his teeth.

I should be on my feet, across the room with the knife in my hand and the deadbolt between us, because a predator older than this country just put his hand on my throat on instinct, and you can't negotiate with instinct.

I don't move. Why don't I move?

Because underneath the cold, underneath the adrenaline, underneath the part of me that is correctly, accurately afraid — my body answered him. The reaching that goes for wolves, that went for Rémy on the dock, that has spent my whole life going for anything that might recognize it, went for *him.*

It met the predator in his grip and didn't pull away.

Every nerve I own said *yes*, and I am sitting on this floor being both things at once, which is all I've ever been.

He slides down the counter and sits on the linoleum across from me, breathing wrong and not looking at his hand.

I'm not looking at it either, only at his face, and what's behind his eyes is a person again. The person is ashamed, and I don't want him to be.

I can't explain it, and I don't mean the hand. I mean that I don't want him sorry for it. Jesus. What kind of woman wants that?

---

"Your window's painted shut," he says.

His voice is wrecked, and it's the first thing he's said since the parking lot, and that's what it is.

"The one in the front's fine," I tell him. "That's the one I'd use. You planning on leaving through it?"

"I'm planning on standing first." Something happens at the corner of his mouth. "The front one. Why that one?"

"Because it's the one nobody expects you to know about."

He tries to sit up and gets about a third of the way and then braces a palm on the floor and stops. I watch him decide not to try again, and I watch him be entirely calm about it.

His arm is still open, and he looks at it for a while. I understand that he is finding out about it at the same moment I'm watching him find out.

"How long?" he asks.

"Most of the goddamn night. You snore, by the way."

"I do not."

"You do now. Hell of a thing to find out on a kitchen floor."

"And this is your apartment?"

"Yes. Where else would I take you?"

"Not a friend's?"

"I don't have that kind of fucking friend. I barely have the regular shitty kind."

He takes that in, and his eyes come up to my face and stay there. I feel how much of a room he is, even flat on the floor, even like this.

"Where's the knife?" he says.

My whole spine goes cold. Fuck.

"On the counter. Do you want it?"

"Mm." He shuts his eyes for a second. "You had it in your hand?"

"For a while. Does that bother you?"

"How long a while?"

"Long enough, Christ. What do you want, a timestamp?"

"Say it again."

"I said long enough."

And he laughs, barely anything, an exhale with a shape to it. It clearly costs him, and it's the single most frightening thing that's happened to me tonight.

Then he opens his eyes and there's nothing loose behind them anymore.

"There's a rule about people like you," he says. "I said it to myself in that hallway and I meant it. Someone who can't be smoothed doesn't walk out of the building."

"You walked me out of the building, didn't you? Well, I walked you out, technically, but you were upright for some of it, and I'm giving you credit. Somebody should."

"I did." A pause. "I've been thinking about that for most of the night, and I was unconscious for a good deal of it, which tells you something."

"Tells me what, exactly? That you sleep like hell on linoleum?"

"That I hadn't finished." He watches me. "You drove me here and put a towel under my head. You had a blade in your hand and you set it down on a counter I can't reach."

"That's right. So what does that make me?"

"Why are you still alive, ghost?"

"Honestly? I was hoping you'd tell me."
