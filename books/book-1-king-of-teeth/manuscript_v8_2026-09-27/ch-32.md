# 32 · Selene

"Bastien."

That's the only word he said when he came through my door last night, and I slept anyway. There's something wrong in the house, and Lucian's cold is still on my skin.

It's an absence of sound, the silence a building makes when everyone in it has stopped what they were doing and started cleaning up.

I sit up to an empty bed, because he left without waking me. The frame beside the bed is still crooked on the wall where my back hit it. There are marks on my throat I can feel without a mirror, and a bite on my breast that's gone dark. My thighs ache in a way I'm going to feel every time I sit down today.

For a breath the only thing in my head is the floor. God, the floor: his face with nothing behind it, my blood going back into him.

Then Bastien. How the fuck have I been asleep?

---

I smell it before I reach the stairs: old blood, something animal underneath it, wet and resinous, the smell of bodies opened in a room that wasn't built for it.

I pull on the shirt I was wearing and walk barefoot down the corridor. The house is wrong, occupied in a new way: footsteps below, moving with purpose, a door closing that doesn't usually close.

The kitchen door is open — the delivery door, the one that faces the service alley — and there's a man standing in it I don't know.

He's one of Silas's people, and I know because he has the stance, loose and weighted forward, a man taught to stand in doorways by someone who believed doorways were the point.

He sees me and his mouth tightens. "Ma'am—"

"What the hell happened?"

"You should— hell, ma'am, you shouldn't be down here— sire said nobody, and sire meant nobody, and I don't want to be the one who tells him I let you past."

"What the fuck *happened?* Who's hurt?"

He doesn't answer. He steps aside, which is an answer.

The kitchen is worse. The smell has weight in here — thick, iron-sweet, coating the back of my throat — and blood on tile holds its heat longer than blood on wood, and this floor is still warm with it.

There's blood on the fucking floor, less a pool than a pattern. It goes from the kitchen door to the dining room in a line that tells a story about how a body moved through a space it was never meant to be moving through.

---

I follow it. The small dining room is destroyed, and *destroyed* isn't enough.

The table is against the wall, thrown there, the legs snapped and the top split down the center. The chairs are pieces. There's a hole in the plaster where a body went through it and kept going. The shape of the hole tells me what made it.

Somebody went through that wall backwards, and the lath behind the plaster is splintered outward, and through the wall is a room I've never been in with a stain on the floor I'll never ask about.

The floor is a map, and I can read it like I read a conversation: what happened, and what's underneath. Five distinct blood patterns: one long drag from the door to the wall, two pooling marks where bodies fell and stayed, and one spray across the ceiling that arcs from the fireplace to the window — a carotid, opened at speed. The body was still turning when it bled out.

And one clean space, body-shaped, where Bastien fell and someone, Silas I think, laid him straight.

There are men in the room, Silas's people, moving without a sound, doing the work that comes after. One of them has a bucket, and the water in it is red. One of them is taking the pantry door off its hinges. It has claw marks gouged into it, deep furrows from a wolf that shifted mid-fight and tried to get through to whatever was on the other side.

A tooth on the floor, wolf, the root still attached, pulled out of the jaw it grew in.

And in the middle of all of it, on the floor, with his throat open to the spine, is Bastien.

Someone has drawn a sheet over him, up to the chest, and I don't make a sound.

---

I'm standing in a doorway looking at the only person in this house who ever grinned at me. He's on the floor with his throat gone, and I don't make a sound.

His hands are folded. Somebody arranged them, carefully, like you'd straighten a collar. His eyes are closed, and his face has the look of a thing that was still deciding what expression to make when the decision was taken away from it.

He was eighty-nine fucking years old, and he worshipped Lucian. He carried papers into rooms where nobody asked for them. He called me a conduit because I told him it was nicer than a drain.

He held a doorway. I can see it in the scuff marks on the floor, and in how the blood falls around a space where a body stood and didn't move. He put himself between the wolves and whoever was behind him, and he held it.

I go to him and kneel on the floor beside him. The blood soaks into my jeans, and I don't care about the jeans.

"You held it," I say. "Didn't you?"

He doesn't hear me, because he's been gone for hours, and the room is cold. Goddamn it, Bastien.

"You held it, Bastien. Do you hear me? You held it."

One of Silas's men looks at me without saying anything and goes back to the bucket.

---

Lucian is in the study. I know because Silas told me. Silas appeared in the kitchen doorway with his hands in his coat and a face I've never seen on him, under grief, under anger, down where the machinery is.

"He's upstairs," Silas said. "Don't go in. For God's sake, don't."

"Is he hurt? Silas. Is he fucking hurt?"

"He's not hurt." A pause. "Don't go in."

"Why not?"

"Because I asked you not to. That has to count for something, doesn't it?"

I go in anyway. Fuck Silas.

The study door is open, as it always is. The lamp is on and the desk is clean, and there's a hole in the wall.

A fist-shaped depression in the plaster beside the window, clean, precise, with the knuckle marks still visible in the dust: one strike, none of the fury from the dining room, a single measured punch into a wall that gave like paper.

It's older than tonight, because the dust in it has settled. Some other day, after some piece of news I wasn't here for, he stood in this study and put his fist through the wall.

And then he pulled it out, and flexed the fingers, and walked to the next meeting.

I know this because there's no blood on the plaster, no rage in the mark, just the precise impression of a man who needed to go through a wall. The wall was closer than the man who deserved it.

Lucian is at the window with his back to me. There's blood on his hands, most of it not his. He's still wearing the ruined shirt I tore open last night.

He doesn't turn around. "Silas told you not to come in, ghost," he says.

"Silas tells me a lot of shit. When have I ever listened? He'd tell me the sky was shit if he thought it'd keep me upstairs."

"This time the bastard was right. Damn him."

I cross the room and stand behind him, close enough to touch him, and I don't touch him.

"Bastien," I say.

"Yes."

"He held the doorway?"

"He held it." His voice is level. "He put two humans behind him and he held it, and he'd never been in a fight that mattered, and he held it until I got there."

"And after? What happened after?"

He doesn't answer. The blood on him is drying, and the gray morning through the window puts his face in a light I have to be careful about reading.

"Lucian."

"I killed them all," he says, like he took the garbage out. "Six wolves, and one of them had shifted back. He put his hands down."

"And?"

"And I took his jaw off." He turns, and he looks like he's looked at himself and found what he expected. "Because he was talking."

I hold his eyes, and I don't flinch, and I don't step back. I don't do any of the things a body does when it's standing in front of something that just told it what it's capable of.

"And the pantry," he says.

"Silas told me."

"Silas told you the door, not the rest." His fingers flex at his sides. "There was a woman in the pantry, the linen girl. She was behind Bastien when he held the door, and I went for the pantry."

"Did you open it? Lucian. Did you?"

"Silas stopped me."

The room goes still, and so do I.

"He said my name," Lucian says. "*Sire,* one word, from the hall. And I turned around, and there she was behind the door, and I had my fucking hand on it."

He looks at his hands, the blood dark on them, almost black in the lamplight.

"I wanted to keep going," he says. "Not that I did it — that I wanted more of it. The pantry door was the next door and I was going to open it."

I take his hands, and he lets me. They're cold and stiff and the blood on them is someone else's. I hold them like he held my father's ledger, from the edges, something that matters.

"You stopped," I say. "Don't you see that?"

"Silas stopped me."

"You turned around." I tighten my grip. "A man who can't be stopped doesn't turn around because someone says his name. You turned around because the name was enough."

"That's a generous fucking reading, ghost."

"That's the correct reading." I look up at him. "I'm a translator. Don't you trust the translation?"

His jaw loosens, and whatever's been holding since he walked into the dining room lets go, one fraction at a time.

"Bastien was eighty-nine," he says.

"I know."

"He called me *sire* every time I told him not to. Every single time. I told him once I'd have him flogged, and he laughed and said *yes, sire.*"

"I know."

"He grinned when I corrected him. Like it was a gift. Like being told he was wrong meant somebody was paying attention."

"I know, Lucian."

He puts his forehead against mine, his eyes closed, his breath uneven, and his fingers in mine are shaking.

"They came through the kitchen," he says. "Somebody let them in."

"I know."

"The same hand, every time, the same hand on the same door."

I hold on, and the house holds still around us. Somewhere below, men are washing a wall and taking a door off its hinges.

"Tomorrow?" I say. "You're sure?"

"Tomorrow."

"He comes at the usual hour?"

"He comes at the usual hour."

I let go of him and go to the desk and I pick up the blade — the one that was his father's, the one that's keyed to one man's blood — and I put it in his coat, in the pocket where it lives.

"Go sleep," he says.

"You won't."

"No. Would you?"

I leave him in the study with the lamp on and the blood drying on his knuckles. The hallway is dark and the house smells like what happened in it tonight. I go to the west wing and I shut the door, all the way, for the first time since I moved in.

Then I open it again, as wide as it goes.

If anybody comes for him tomorrow, they walk past me first.
