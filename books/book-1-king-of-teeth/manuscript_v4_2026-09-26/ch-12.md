# 12 · Selene

The house has a word for me and it isn't my name.

I hear it like I hear everything in here. Said near me, never to me, in a register that assumes I can't tell the difference. A woman on the second-floor landing tells another woman I'm *the arrangement,* and the other woman says *which one,* and they both laugh.

Pack uses *arrangement* for a temporary structure. A lean-to. You build it because you need it and tear it down when you don't.

In this house it means the same thing.

---

Lucian put me in the west wing. He said it without preamble — *there's a room, it's yours, don't go east* — and then he went to the study and shut the door, and I stood in a hallway with a bag over my shoulder and no idea which direction west was.

A boy named Bastien found me. He's young by their calendar, which isn't mine, and he talks as a retriever runs, at everything, all the time.

"This way." He took the bag off my shoulder before I could stop him. "It's not far. There's a bath."

"I don't need —"

"Everybody needs a bath after a night like that." He grinned at me over his shoulder. "You drove the King home in your car. The kid with the tray told Silas. Silas told me."

"Silas told you."

"Silas tells me everything he doesn't want to say out loud. I'm like a — what's the word. A drain."

"A conduit."

"That's nicer. I'll use the hell out of that."

He walked me through a hall that smelled like wood polish and money and old paper, and every person we passed looked at me and then away. It wasn't the human look-through. It was a sort. A quick decision that I'm not worth adjusting for.

Except one.

A man came out of a doorway at the end of the hall. Tall, silver at the temples, with a face that makes you think of somebody's favorite uncle. He stopped when he saw me, and his eyes went over my face like a letter he wasn't expecting.

Then he smiled at Bastien.

"Making friends?" he said.

"She's the — she's Lucian's —" Bastien caught himself. "She's staying in the west wing."

"How lovely." The man looked at me. "How is the room?"

"I haven't seen the damn thing yet."

"You'll find it comfortable. There's a window that sticks — pull up before you pull out." He adjusted his cuffs. "I'm Cassian. You must be the woman from the club."

He said it warmly. The warmth landed on me like heat off a radiator. Steady, and coming from a thing you don't touch.

"Selene," I said.

"Selene. Beautiful name. Is it your mother's?"

"My father's side."

"Ah." He nodded. "How is your family?"

That sentence had a shape I didn't like. The words were fine. They just arrived pre-assembled, like asking after a person's family was a habit, like other people tie shoes.

"Dead," I said.

He blinked. It was the first genuine movement I'd seen on his face.

"I'm sorry to hear it," he said. And he meant it. I could hear that he meant it, and it went into me badly. True things do, when you're not braced.

Then he went down the hall and Bastien took me to the room, and the room was fine, and the window sticks.

---

The house is old and the house is a court.

I've never been inside one. I've worked at the edges of it — poured drinks for the men who come from it, learned the names they drop and the order they drop them in. But being inside it is standing in the water instead of looking at it.

Everything here runs on rank. Money's in every surface, but rank is the engine. Who gets to enter which room, which hallway, which conversation. I can feel it like I feel the second language in any room I've ever stood in.

There's a morning room where the council takes coffee, and I am not in it.

There's a library on the second floor where the Elders read newspapers that are printed in French, and I am not in it.

There's a kitchen where the human staff — the ones who know, the ones in the arrangement — eat together at a long table, and they looked up when I came in and could not decide what to put me in.

Too human for the morning room. Not human enough for the long table.

I ate a piece of toast standing at the counter and went back to the west wing.

---

The court notices me as a body notices a splinter. Fuck them.

Slowly, over the first day, I can feel the attention gathering. Small looks, held a beat too long, and the silence that drops into a room when I walk into it.

It's not hatred. I'd smell hatred.

This is a house full of very old things trying to decide whether I'm furniture or a guest, and the distinction matters, because furniture gets moved and a guest gets fucking fed.

---

On the second morning, one of them decides to check.

He's old — old even by their calendar, with a jaw set in a century that didn't ask women things. An Aldric cousin. I catch the name from the kitchen staff. He finds me in the corridor outside the library and stands too close, and I can smell what he's going to do before he does it.

"So you're the arrangement," he says.

I don't step back. Stepping back is a whole sentence, and I'm not giving him one.

He reaches for my face, two fingers under my chin, and tilts it as you'd turn a horse's head to check its teeth, slowly, in front of four people who don't look up.

I let him touch me.

I'm busy, already building the sentence that will take him apart. I can hear both layers of him, entitlement on the surface and terror underneath. He's terrified that a half-breed with the King's attention will cost his family standing in a succession they've been positioning for since before I was born. The hand on my chin is a man checking whether the bomb is real.

I open my mouth to give him the translation — his own fear, named, in court register, in front of everyone, dressed as courtesy so he can't even flinch without confirming it —

And the hallway goes cold.

Pond-water cold. The kind where the temperature drops through some line and the whole character of it changes. The glass in a woman's hand fogs over. She sets it down without looking at it.

Lucian is in the doorway at the end of the hall.

He doesn't come in or speak. He looks at the hand on my face, and that's all he does, and I watch a man who has been alive for three centuries slowly remove his own fingers from my jaw as though he's just discovered they're touching something that will cost him the hand.

He leaves the corridor, and then the house, and Bastien tells me the next morning he left the city.

Nobody in that hallway says a word about it. A woman picks up her glass and finds it cold, and frowns, and drinks it anyway. Hell of a party trick.

---

I am fucking furious.

At Lucian, because the man barely counts. At the back of him, already gone from the doorway, already somewhere in the east wing doing whatever a king does when he's finished making a point.

I was winning. I had the sentence built. I had both registers loaded and the safety off and I was going to take that man's standing apart with six words and a smile, and every person in that corridor would have known that the woman in the west wing has teeth, and the teeth are *hers.*

Instead they know the King walked in.

They know I'm protected. In both languages that word means *doesn't need to protect herself.*

I go back to the west wing and sit in the chair and think about it until the thinking has a temperature, and the temperature is not going down.

---

A woman named Aldric — I catch the name from Bastien, who narrates this house as if he's calling a game — comes to the west wing door around noon.

She doesn't knock. She stands in the frame and looks at me sitting in the window with a cup of coffee I made myself in a kitchen that doesn't belong to me.

"You're the girl," she says.

In pack, *girl* is a position. In this house it's a size.

"I'm Selene."

"I know what your name is." She leans on the frame. "Marguerite wants you out."

"I'll talk to Lucian. Hell."

"Marguerite doesn't go through Lucian, dear. She goes around him. She's been going around kings since before your species had a written language."

My species. She doesn't know what my species is. She's looking at a bartender in the west wing and putting me under *human,* the smallest fucking drawer in her cabinet.

"I'll talk to Lucian," I say again.

"You won't need to." She straightens up. "You'll be out by the weekend. These things sort themselves."

She leaves.

I drink my coffee.

---

Lucian comes to the room in the evening.

He doesn't knock either, which should bother me and doesn't. He stands in the doorway like he stands in every doorway, reading the room before he enters it. Then he comes in, and he has to pass me to get to the bed, and he doesn't give me an inch of room to let him.

His hip brushes my shoulder. His coat smells like rain and cold stone and, faintly, like my sheets.

That's not even a little bit fucking fair.

"You've eaten, ghost," he says. It's not a question.

"Toast."

"That's not eating."

"It's what there was. Your kitchen's a fucking museum."

His eyes narrow. He's working out who in his house let a woman go hungry, and somebody's about to have a very bad conversation.

"The Aldric woman came to see me," I say.

"Which one."

"Tall. Opinions. A lot of both, Jesus."

"That's three of them." He comes in and shuts the door. "What did she say?"

"That Marguerite wants me out by the weekend."

"Marguerite wants many things by the weekend. She's been wanting things by the weekend for eight hundred years." He sits on the edge of the bed, the only place to sit that isn't the chair I'm in. "She'll keep wanting them."

"And the cousin," I say.

He goes still. The air around him stops moving.

"He put his hand on you," he says.

"And I was about to take it off. I had the bastard, Lucian."

"I saw what he —"

"You saw what he did. You didn't see what I was about to do." I lean forward. "I had it, Lucian. Both registers. His fear underneath his face, and a sentence ready that would have cost him his standing in that corridor for a decade. With an audience. With a smile."

He watches me.

"And you walked in," I say. "And the room went cold, and he ran, and now every person in this house knows that the woman in the west wing is *protected.* You know what that means in wolf? It means can't hunt for herself. You don't get to be my teeth, Lucian. I have my own fucking teeth."

The silence goes on long enough that I can hear the house settling.

"You had both registers," he says.

"Loaded."

"And the sentence."

"Built."

His jaw loosens. His eyes go over my face again. It's the look he gave me in the hallway with blood on his mouth, when he tried to smooth me and it didn't take.

"Next time," he says, "I'll wait."

"Next time you'll wait in the hall until I'm finished."

The almost-smile. The real one, not the court one.

"Yes," he says. "I will."

The silence stretches. He looks at his hands like tools he's deciding whether to put away.

"If he'd gone further," he says, "I would have taken the hand off at the wrist. In the corridor. In front of the four people who didn't look up, so they could watch me do it and learn what not looking up costs. Then I would have gone to the Aldric house in Lyon and explained to his family, in person, with the hand on the table between us, that their name is welcome in this city for exactly as long as every member of it understands that the woman in my house is not a thing they get to touch. Not her face. Not her arm. Not the air around her."

He says it with no heat, like a sequence he's already rehearsed to the end.

"That isn't what I did," he says. "Because you told me you had it."

He looks at me.

"But I want you to know what the other version was."

---

"This is your aunt," I say, after a while.

"My father's sister."

"And she doesn't want me here."

"She doesn't want anything here that she didn't put here herself." He looks at me. "It's not personal."

"It feels like it's about me."

"It isn't."

I want to argue. I want to translate what the Aldric woman said to me and show him what it looked like in the other language, the one he doesn't hear because he's never had to.

But he's sitting on my bed in a room he gave me, and he looks tired in a way that isn't about sleep, and I don't have the right vocabulary for a fight I'd win.

"The pact," I say instead.

"What about it?"

"How does it work, in here? Am I a guest?"

"You're mine."

He says it simply. He's putting me in his house as you'd put a key on a ring. This is where you are, this is what you're attached to, this is who has to be moved to get to you.

"That word is going to cause so many fucking problems," I say.

"It's going to solve them."

"In your language."

"In the only one that matters in this building."

He's wrong about that. There are at least three languages in this house, and he speaks one of them, and the one he doesn't speak is the one that's going to decide whether I wake up here tomorrow.

But he's also right. His name is the only protection that works in this building, and I've been unprotected long enough to know what it costs.

I've taken worse deals for less.

"Fine," I say.

"Fine."

"But I'm going to need to leave the west wing."

"You can go where you like."

"The morning room?"

The space a smile would go, if smiles were permitted.

"The morning room," he says. "Yes."

"And the kitchen."

"Obviously."

"And wherever the hell you keep the information about Rémy Thibodeaux."

The almost-smile goes away.

"That," he says, "is a different conversation."

"I know."

"Have it tomorrow." He stands. "Eat something that isn't toast, ghost."

He goes. The door doesn't close all the way behind him, because nothing in this house closes all the way, and I sit in the window and look at the crack of light in the frame and think about the word *mine* and what it costs in both translations.

Someone moves to have me removed before the week is out.

I'll be right here when they fucking try.
