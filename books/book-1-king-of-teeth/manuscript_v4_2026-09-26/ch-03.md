# 3 · Lucian

The council wants a war and hasn't said so yet.

I can tell because they've dressed for it. There's a formality in the room this morning that wasn't here last week — collars done, seats taken by rank, and nobody's glass has been touched. When vampires stop drinking in a room together they've already decided what the room is for.

Marguerite has given me two days since the last conversation. That's all the patience she has and she's used it properly, and now she's at the long table with a quorum and a face that says she's finished waiting.

"Barataria," she says.

"Yes."

"Six of Gaspard's were seen north of the canal last night. Armed. Three of them shifted."

"In the city?"

"On Tchoupitoulas." She folds her hands on the table. "Tchoupitoulas is not contested ground. It is not neutral ground. It is a street where our people keep houses, and three wolves ran down it with their teeth out."

Somebody at the end of the table says, "Traditionally—"

"Be quiet," I tell him.

He shuts up, and the room adjusts around the silence, and I let it go long enough to mean something.

"How many of ours saw them?" I ask.

"Eight," Marguerite says. "One of them is Aldric's man. He'll want it in writing."

"He'll get it."

"He'll want more than writing, Lucian."

"I know what the bastard wants."

---

Here's what the room wants, and none of them will put it in one sentence.

They want a strike on Barataria, hard enough to hurt, fast enough that the pack can't call it a battle, quiet enough that the humans don't fucking see it.

It's a good plan. Fuck them for that.

If I send the court in tonight, the next thing that happens is a vote for war, and the question of who killed my father becomes a question nobody is permitted to reopen.

"What are you proposing?" she asks.

"A retaliation. Not a war."

The room shifts.

"Targeted," I say. "Fighters only. Nobody who isn't armed. Nobody in a house. Nobody on the docks after the boats are in."

"That's a list of things you're not doing," says a man named Aldric, a cousin of a cousin, who has been waiting for this sentence since before I walked in. "What are you doing?"

"I'm going into Barataria with Silas and taking back what they took from us on Tchoupitoulas."

"That's a raid. That's not a damn retaliation."

"It's a raid at the volume I choose." I look at him. "And it's mine. Not yours."

"The court—"

"The court can witness it or wait for the report. Either way, the scope is mine."

Marguerite watches me over her steepled fingers. She has lived through every argument this room has ever had, and she is measuring this one against all of them.

"You're drawing a line," she says.

"I'm drawing a line."

"On our own retaliation."

"Yes."

"Your father—"

"Would have drawn the same one."

That lands in the room as hard as a palm on a table. A few of them agree with me, and that's useful. A few of them think I'm quoting a dead man's philosophy because I haven't built my own yet. Also useful.

One of them — the Aldric cousin — thinks I'm weak.

Fuck him. I can work with that.

---

Silas is in the hall when I come out. He doesn't ask how it went, because Silas has never asked how anything went. He reads a room by how people come out of it.

"Tonight," I tell him.

"How many?"

"You, me, and six of ours. Light."

He nods. His hands are in his coat and his eyes are on the door behind me, reading who's coming out after and in what order.

"Rules of engagement," he says. It's not a question. He's heard there are some and he wants them before the hall fills.

"Armed wolves only. Nothing civilian. Nothing domestic." I stop at the window. "If a man puts his hands down and goes back, he walks."

"It's a good list."

Silas says it as he says everything, as if he ran out of spare words before the Civil War.

"Aurelian had one."

"I'm aware."

"His was longer."

"I'm aware of that too."

He almost smiles. On Silas that's a loosening around the jaw that lasts about as long as a match.

"The Marchand house," he says.

I look at him.

"Silas."

"It's on the route. Between the canal and the water." His eyes go to the window. "I'd like to skip it."

"Why?"

He doesn't answer. His hands come out of his coat and go back in.

"Because it's neutral ground," he says. "Ondine Marchand has kept it neutral since before you were back in this city, and she's never asked us for a thing, and I'd rather not put men through her yard."

"Fine. Skip it."

"Thank you."

He goes fast, because Silas has never learned to leave a room slowly.

There's a delivery van at the kitchen entrance below. I can hear the driver's pulse from up here. Steady. Warm. Stupid with trust.

I don't let myself count how long it's been since I fed.

---

Marguerite finds me at the stair. Of course she fucking does.

She's alone. She's about to say what the council would want to hear and she doesn't want them to hear her saying it.

"You're right to keep it small," she says.

"Thank you."

"I'm not complimenting you. I'm telling you I agree, so that later, when someone in that room claims I didn't, you can say otherwise." She adjusts her cuffs. The gesture is so like her brother's that for a moment the stairwell has someone else in it. "How long can you hold the line?"

"Depends on what comes back."

"What comes back is what always comes back. Dead wolves and a court that liked the taste of it." She looks up at me. "Your father held it for forty years after the truce. He held it by being in the room every single time someone wanted to open the door wider. If you're not in that room, someone else will be."

"I'll be in it."

"You'll be in Barataria tonight."

"And in the room tomorrow."

She considers that. Then she says a sentence that has no business in a conversation about a raid, and it passes between us without either of us stopping to look at it.

"We don't leave," she says. "This city. This house. Whatever happens with the wolves, we are not going anywhere. If someone in that room suggests relocating the court, even temporarily, you put that down. Immediately. Do you understand me?"

"Nobody's going to suggest—"

"Someone will." Her face is calm about it. "They always do, when there's blood. Someone will say it would be prudent to move operations to Baton Rouge for a season, or to the Roche estate, or to anywhere that isn't here. And you will tell them no."

"Why would they want to leave?"

"They won't want to. That's not the danger." She's already moving past me down the stair. "The danger is that someone proposes it who sounds reasonable, and the court agrees because the court is frightened, and then we are not here. And we have always been here."

She says it like the walls are stone. A fact so old it's stopped needing a reason.

I don't ask for one. It doesn't even fucking occur to me to ask.

---

By evening Silas is in the courtyard with our people, and the fucking court is watching from the upper windows.

He's chosen well. Not one of them has the look of a man who wants to be seen being brave.

He gives me my coat. There's a weight in the left pocket that's been there since Tuesday, and my hand goes to it. I keep doing that.

My father's blade. Keyed to his blood, useless against anyone but him, and he is already dead.

I carry it because I cannot put the fucking thing down.

"Route?" I say.

"Canal to the bayou. We go in by water. The dock at Lafitte's." Silas doesn't look at a map because Silas has never looked at a map. "Straight through the settlement, south to the levee, and out the same way."

"The Marchand house?"

"Wide."

"Good."

I look at the six of them, young, most of them, young for our kind. They've spent their whole lives being told the truce mattered.

"Armed wolves," I tell them. "Nothing else. Nobody in a house. Nobody who puts their hands down."

One of them nods, and the others wait.

"If you can't tell," I say, "don't."

Nobody asks what happens to the ones who can.

We go.
