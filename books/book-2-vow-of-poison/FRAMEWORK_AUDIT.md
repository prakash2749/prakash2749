**Where the book deliberately doesn't chase the comps:**
- **Profanity.** It runs 430 per 100k against the comps' 627–972. The per-voice bands (Ondine 550–750, Silas 300–420, Cray 0) are what keep the three narrators distinct, and all three are in band.

Bringing it inside would take a line edit that lengthens sentences across the whole book. That's open for LO to decide.

## The 12 factors

### 01 · Discoverability: get noticed
- **Built:**
  - Seven keywords in `KDP_UPLOAD.md`, all true of the book: witch × vampire, touch her and die, forced proximity, New Orleans gothic, scarred hero, debt/bargain deadline, dual POV.
  - Three categories, with a smaller Witches & Wizards shelf for easier ranking.
  - Series metadata (The Blood Code, Book 2).
  - A+ Content plan.
  - A hashtag (#VowOfPoison) on the Thank You page.
- **Depends on LO:** the launch plan itself, meaning ads, ARC readers and social posting. `market/LAUNCH_PLAN.md` covers Book 1. Reuse its calendar for Book 2.

### 02 · Clickability: create immediate interest
- **Built:**
  - The tagline: *He would give his name for her. She'd never let him.*
  - A cover brief (series-matched layout, object-led imagery, poison-green key, readable at thumbnail size).
  - A blurb that opens on a year of him walking past her gate.
- **Depends on LO:** commissioning the cover. The designer spec is in `KDP_UPLOAD.md`: 344 pages, 0.860 in spine, 13.110 × 9.25 in full wrap.

### 03 · Convertibility: turn interest into sales
- **Look Inside:**
  - Ch. 1 opens on *"The witch who nearly killed my king is making soup."*
  - The first 2,000 words already read hot (first hot stretch at 0%), with his year of wanting her on the page before the envelope.
- **Blurb:** stacks the tropes readers search for and ends on the screenshot line *Touch her again. Please.*
- **Content list:** written as a menu, not a defense.
- **Pricing funnel:** Book 2 at $4.99, with Book 1 dropped to $0.99 for launch week.

### 04 · Hookability: capture readers early
- **First page:** charged contact (her thumb on his scarred palm), the envelope, the collateral line (*Ondine*), and Cray at the door. Ch. 1 ends on a cliff.
- **Openers:** none of the 40 units opens on weather, waking or room description.
- **Endings:** every chapter ends on a hook. Distribution: 67% of final paragraphs are 10 words or fewer, 27% are 5 or fewer, and 25% end on dialogue, all inside targets.
- **Early heat:** it reads hot by Ch. 2 and is explicit by Ch. 3.

### 05 · Chemistry: make the central relationship compelling
- **Devices unique to this book:**
  - Silas can't finish a sentence about himself. He finishes exactly one, *"I was a dog. Never yours."* (Ch. 33), then says *I love you* whole in Ch. 34.
  - The pet-name asymmetry resolves: *witch* 60 times, *keeper* once, in the epilogue.
  - The coffee she pours but never asks how he takes, answered in the epilogue.
- **Dialogue in the sex:** 44%, more than double every comp. The scenes argue, bargain, beg and laugh.
- **Consent drawn on purpose:**
  - Explicit yes before the first time (Ch. 9).
  - *Knock twice* stated and repeated back before Ch. 15 and Ch. 30, with him asking *"Was that right?"* afterward.
  - The binding in Ch. 26 happens because he holds out his wrists.

### 06 · Bingeability: keep readers turning pages
- **Clocks:** five relayed clocks, none resolving into dead air:
  1. what Honorine bought
  2. will Silas give his name
  3. the new moon
  4. who is the account
  5. are we a couple
- **Heat:** hot stretches in 60% of the book, and never more than a few chapters between them.
- **Pace:** chapters average about 2,200 words, sentences are short, and each chapter ends on its hook.
- **Cray interstitials** (all 600–900 words) give the reader a secret one chapter before the heroine gets it. I-2 lands before Ch. 13, and I-3 before Ch. 19.

### 07 · Emotional resonance: make the story matter
- **Every working costs Ondine something she can't get back:** her mother's voice (Ch. 20) and her grandmother's face (Ch. 32).
- **After the loss:** Silas describes the portrait to her every morning, a piece at a time (Ch. 34, epilogue).
- **Silas's past:** the fire, being turned in the road, the yard, told inside her in Ch. 18. Burning through the ward reopens the burns that never healed (Ch. 31).
- **Bonus scene *Drink*:** he tastes that she loves him before she has said it.

### 08 · Unpredictability: maintain curiosity

  - Ch. 19: Cray sold Silas.
  - Ch. 20: the veto.
  - Ch. 24: the account signs with the names of the grieving dead, and Honorine's grief may have been bought twice.

### 09 · Reader satisfaction: deliver a worthwhile payoff
- **The ending:**
  - A complete HEA.
  - The heroine saves herself with her own craft: salt for bone, his hand, his name.
  - The villain pays on the page, lawfully witnessed.

- **Callbacks paid off:** *Stay in the room*, the board that gives, the portrait, *Get off my step, broker*, and *Nobody owns the water*.

## Master line edit (2026-09-27)

Full read-through of all 40 units plus the bonus scene. Snapshot of the pre-edit text: `manuscript_pre-master-pass_2026-09-27/`. 88,944 words, 350 pages.

- **Split dialogue fixed.** An earlier script (`splitdlg.py`) had broken one speaker's line into two paragraphs, so the second half read as though the other character said it. Every instance the detector and the read found is merged, and the detector now finds only real alternations. `split4.py` had also halved narration paragraphs mid-thought, and many of those are rejoined. The book has 315 fewer paragraphs overall.
- **Broken markup fixed:** italics split across paragraphs (Ch. 6, 24, 28) and two quotes run together (Ch. 7, 26).
- **Continuity fixed:**
  - Which night Silas stayed (Ch. 14, 15, 17).
  - The pencil pulled from her hair twice (Ch. 13).
  - The ledger's hiding place (Ch. 22).
  - When Honorine agreed (Ch. 19 vs I-2).
  - Sunrise vs afternoon (Ch. 25).
  - Summer weather (Ch. 30).
  - Her position in bed (Ch. 18).
  - "I've never paid for my own working" vs the reading (Ch. 32).
  - Cray's ledger vs the Bywater register (Ch. 33).
  - A scene in Ch. 25 that never happened.
- **Heat kept, loops cut.** Every sex scene is intact. Cut:
  - repeated "say it / whose is this" ownership loops (Ch. 12, 23, 26)
  - bolted-on explicit lines inside crisis beats (Ch. 22, 24, 31)
  - a third near-identical solo scene (Ch. 4)
  - "cock so hard it hurts" ×4
- **Book 1 canon phrases removed:** "tell me/say that again" (Ch. 15, 21, 23, 34).
- **Ch. 1 left untouched** so it still matches the teaser printed in Book 1.
- **Sentence rhythm.** Narration median is 7 words, measured the same way as the comps: God of Malice 6, Kiss the Villain 9, Haunting Adeline 11. That's inside the comp range, so it wasn't lengthened.
- **Profanity.** Ondine is at 542 and Silas at 298, each a hair under band after the cuts. Deliberately not padded (see memory: metric passes break dialogue).
