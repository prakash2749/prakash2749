## THE BOOK IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The night Silas finally brings Ondine the writ he's carried in his coat for three months, Absalom Cray calls her grandmother's debt. The collateral is Ondine's own name, signed away before she was born, and it falls due at the new moon. Cray will accept a substitute, any name of equal weight freely given, and he's looking at Silas when he says it. Six weeks. A man who has never finished a sentence about himself, a woman who has never let anyone read her, and a paper that can't be broken by killing the man who holds it.

## RULES THIS BOOK RUNS ON

### The paper (plot mechanics, state them plainly on the page)
1. Honorine Marchand signed the debt before Ondine was born. **Collateral: *Ondine*** — the name, and with it every name the Marchand line holds. Witches hold debts in named jars; half the obligations of both courts sit on Ondine's shelves. That is the **access** the account is buying.
2. The paper can be **discharged by substitution**: a name of equal weight, freely given.
3. **It cannot be broken by killing the holder.** If the holder dies with the paper in force, it passes up to the principal, the account. That's why Book 1 kept Cray "in sight, not in custody," and why Silas can't just kill him. State the rule in Ch. 2 (Cray) and have it confirmed in Ch. 14 (Ondine's craft).
4. **The solution, planted before it's used:** the collateral line is in Cray's own hand, not Honorine's (Ch. 28). A name written by the broker can be substituted by the broker's name, salt for bone. If the holder *is* the collateral, the paper has no one to pass to and voids. It costs Ondine a memory to do it.

### Voice (from VOICE_BIBLE §4)
- **Silas:** sign and trail. He never finishes a sentence about himself. He finishes **exactly one**, in Ch. 33 (*"I was a dog. Never yours."*); Ch. 18 gets him most of the way there. He says *I love you* whole only in Ch. 34. He looks at the **window** when he's hiding something. Menacing profanity (*fuck*, *bastard*), ~300–420/100k.
- **Ondine:** substitution, a cook's mind. *"That'll cost"* **in dialogue only.** Burns rosemary when she's worried. Funny-modern profanity (*shit*, blasphemy), ~550–750/100k. She calls him *Silas*, *Silas Roche* when angry, and ***keeper*** once, in the epilogue.
- **Cray interstitials:** never names the client or gives it a pronoun · never swears · never uses a number · shows himself **choosing**, never explaining · no strategy monologue · courteous to the end. 600–900 words.
- **Series:** *Verity* is **never named** (the account is "the account", "the principal", "the client", "something older than the house"). Nobody asks why neither species leaves New Orleans. Present tense. American English.
