His silence fills the doorway better than he does.

"Well." Cray bends and sets the bottle on my top step, squared neatly to the edge. "For the kitchen. It's a good year. Goodnight, Miss Marchand. Goodnight, Mr. Roche."

He goes down my walk without hurrying. The board that gives creaks under him once, and then he's gone into the dark past the gate.

My lungs start working again. I hadn't noticed they'd stopped.

"Inside, witch," Silas says, without turning around.

He doesn't come back to the table. He goes to the window over the sink and stands to one side of it, reading the street.

"Pour it out," I say.

Silas is already moving.

He brings the bottle in and pulls the cork with his teeth and spits it into the sink. Then he upends the whole thing into the gutter outside my back door. The smell of it rolls back in on the night air, dark cherry and oak with pepper underneath.

"That'll cost," I tell him. "It was a good year."

"It was his year."

"It was still a good year. You just poured somebody's grandfather's vineyard into my gutter, and the slugs are going to be drunk for a week."

He looks at me. His jaw does its almost, the loosening that never turns into anything.

He takes a folded page out of his other pocket and lays it on my table.

"What I asked my king for," he says. "This morning."
