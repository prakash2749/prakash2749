# 27 · Silas

I ask my king for help, which in two hundred years I have never done.

---

He's in the small study at dawn. He's always in the small study at dawn now. He doesn't sleep much since the crown, and when he does it's in the afternoons, with the shutters closed and Selene's coat thrown over his feet.

She's in the window seat. She's always in the window seat. She has a cup of coffee going cold on the sill and a stack of Aldric letters in her lap, and she's reading them with a pencil in her teeth.

They both look up when I come in.

I stand in the doorway, and my coat is heavy as hell. Every pocket of it has a grave in it now. The receipt. The index card. Tobias's list with *Marchand, H.* halfway down. Cray's note. They sit against my chest like stones.

"Silas," Lucian says. "You're early."

I don't answer.

I look at the window behind his head.

Then I make myself look away from it.

What happens to a man who stops hiding? I don't know. I've never tried.

---

It's the hardest thing I've done since I stood up out of the mud in that yard. I feel it in my neck, every muscle that's been turning my head toward glass my whole life, pulling, and I don't fucking let it.

I look at my king instead.

He's gone very still. He's watching my face. He's learned what I do with windows, and he's watching me not do it, and I see him understand that something is about to happen that's never happened before.

Selene takes the pencil out of her teeth.

"Sire," I say.

"Yes."

"I lied to you."

"I know."

"More than once."

"More than you're counting," Selene says from the window seat, low. "You're forgetting the one about the brandy."

I close my eyes for a second.

"More than I'm counting," I say.

"Sit down," Lucian says.

I don't sit. I've never sat in this room.

But I come in, and I stand in front of his desk, as I've stood more times than I can count with a report in my mouth, and this time there's no report. There's just me.

"Tell me," Lucian says.

So I do.

---

I tell him everything.

I tell him the collateral is Ondine. Her own name, signed away by her grandmother before she was born, in the broker's hand. Every name her house holds goes with it. Half the debts of both courts, sitting on her shelves.

I tell him the paper can't be broken by killing Cray. I tell him why. I tell him what's above Cray, and that it has no face and a column of names and a patience older than the city.

I tell him Cray offered me the substitute. My name for hers. I tell him I went to Cray's house and sat in his chair and said yes.

Lucian's face doesn't change. But his fingers go flat on the desk.

I take the receipt out of my coat and lay it in front of him. The brown ink. The clerk's bad hand. The catcher's word.

He reads it. He reads it twice. When he looks up, his eyes are black.

"Cray," he says.

"He stood at the edge of the pen."

"He sold you to my father."

"He introduced me," I say, and my voice cracks, and I hate the bastard for it. "He'd want me to be accurate."

"He's going to want a lot of things," Lucian says softly. "He won't get them."

Selene makes a sound in the window seat. I don't look at her.

I lay the index card beside the receipt. Then Tobias's list, with the dead names down one side and the graves down the other, and *Marchand, H.* halfway down.

"The account buys grief," I say. "And access. Every time. Tobias found it. It arrives with a fresh grief and buys it off whoever's holding something it wants. It bought Honorine Marchand's. And her grief may have been bought before she ever lost anything."

Lucian looks at the list.

"And the veto," Selene says.

I turn.

She's put down the Aldric letters. She's looking at me with her translator's eyes, and she's heard it. She heard it before I opened my mouth.

"Tell him about the veto," she says. "You haven't yet. It's in your face."

---

"When I opened her grandmother's ledger," I say, "I bled into the lock."

"Silas," Lucian says.

"It took the blood. It made a promise. I didn't read the terms." My jaw goes tight. "The terms are that my name is tied to her. I can't give it away without her yes."

Lucian goes very still.

"She knows," I say. "She read me. It cost her the memory of her mother's voice, and she read me, and she found out. She's held my name for a week and didn't tell me."

"And she won't give you her yes," Selene says.

"No."

"She told you no."

"She told me no a great many times," I say, "very clearly," and Selene's mouth twitches.

"So you can't give your name," Lucian says slowly. "And she won't let you."

"Yes."

"And she means to go in herself."

I stop.

I hadn't said that. I hadn't let myself think it. *Then it'll be you*, I said, on the pantry floor, and she said *yes*, and I heard what I wanted to hear, and I've been carrying it around since, a yes I thought meant she'd stand beside me while I gave it.

"She said yes," I say. "Last night. I said *then it'll be you*, and she said yes."

Selene closes her eyes.

"Oh, Silas," she says. "Oh, honey. She didn't mean yes to *that*."

---

I stand in front of my king's desk with my coat full of graves, and for the first time in my life, I don't know what to do.

I've always known. I've been a tracker since before I was a man. There's always a trail. There's always a direction and an intent and a place where the ground tells you which way it's going. I read it and I follow it and I do what I'm told.

There's no trail here. Where the fuck is the trail? There's a woman who holds my name and won't give it back, and a paper that can't be broken, and a moon getting thinner every night, and I've run out of ground.

"Sire," I say.

Lucian waits.

I've asked him for almost nothing. A week, once, that he didn't notice. A writ, that he signed without reading. An account, the morning all this started.

I've never asked him for this.

"Help me," I say. "Sire. Help me."

---

The room goes very still.

Selene doesn't move. Lucian doesn't move. The plane tree outside the window doesn't move.

Then my king stands up.

He comes around the desk. He doesn't hurry. He comes and stands in front of me, close, and he puts his palm on my shoulder.

Never the back of my neck. That's where his uncle used to put his, on Lucian's, as you'd hold a dog by the scruff. I watched Cassian do it more times than I can count, and I watched Lucian not flinch from it.

On my shoulder. Flat. As a man puts his palm on another man, to hold him up.

"You asked me for a week once," Lucian says. "When all this started. You said you were still confirming a name. I didn't notice."

His grip tightens.

"I'm noticing now."

---

After that, it goes fast.

Lucian sends for Tobias, and Tobias comes up from the registry with ink on his fingers and his useless spectacles pushed up in his hair. Lucian sends for the Roche brothers, and three of them come, big and silent, and stand at the study door. Selene gets up out of the window seat and takes a pen and starts writing.

It's the crown mobilizing. I've seen it before. I've been part of it before, standing at the edge, reading the room while other men planned.

This time I'm in the middle of it.

"Cray's house," Lucian says. "Watched. Every door, every window. Nobody in or out that we don't see."

"Already," Anatole says. "Since the jars."

"Good. The Marchand house. The same."

"The watchers at her ward line," Selene says. "Cray's men."

"Leave them," I say. "They'll tell him if we move them."

Lucian nods. "Tobias. The paper. The original terms. Everything you can find about how a debt like this is discharged. Every precedent. Every loophole. If it can be broken by a witch, I want to know how."

"It can't be broken by decree," Tobias says, very low.

Lucian stops.

"I checked," Tobias says. "The night Silas brought me the register. A witch's debt sits outside the Code. It was made on neutral ground, between a witch and a broker, and the Code has no jurisdiction over it. You can't void it, sire. Not by writ, not by law, not by crown."

Nobody says anything for a moment.

"Then what can I do?" Lucian says.

"You can witness," Tobias says. "Under the Code, a King's witness makes anything done at a signing lawful. Whatever happens in that room, if you're there, it happened under the crown."

Lucian looks at me.

"Then I'll be there," he says.

I look at him. My king, who stood in a chamber and killed his own uncle with a blade I sharpened for him. Who has never asked me why I look at windows.

What does it cost a king to stand in a broker's front room and watch? What will it cost him in the court, in the letters from the Aldric house?

He doesn't ask. He's decided.

I know that look. I taught it to him.

---

It takes most of the morning. I stand in my king's study while he and Selene and Tobias and the Roche brothers pull the city tight around one house in Bywater and one house on the canal, and I read all of it, and for once none of it is mine to carry alone.

Selene catches me at the study door before he does.

She puts her palm on my arm, and her voice is very low.

"She's going to walk into that room alone," she says. "You know that. She's a Marchand. She thinks this is her grandmother's bill and she's the only one who gets to pay it."

"I know."

"Then don't be a fucking idiot about it." Her grip tightens. "Don't lock her in. Don't try to carry her out over your shoulder. She'll never forgive you, and she'll be right."

"Then what?"

"Be in the room," Selene says. "Just be in the goddamn room."

When she lets go, Lucian comes back to me.

"She means to go in herself," he says. "You know it."

"Yes."

"Then go home." His palm comes back to my shoulder, heavy and flat. "Go home, Silas. Don't let her out of your sight."
