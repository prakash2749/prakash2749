## Before You Begin

This is a dark romance. It is not a safe book, and it is not trying to be one.

Silas Roche is the King's enforcer. He breaks men's necks on doorsteps and doesn't lose sleep over it, because he doesn't sleep. He was owned once, and he has never said so out loud. He is not going to become a gentle man. He's going to become a man who stays.

Ondine Marchand is a witch who brewed the poison that nearly killed a king, and she'd do it again for the right reasons. She saves herself. Nobody carries her out.

What you'll find inside, so you can decide:

- Blood drinking, on the page. A first feeding that is voluntary, aware, and remembered, and very explicit.
- Explicit sex, a lot of it, described plainly. Oral both ways. Rough sex and tender sex. Sex outdoors, on the water. Sex in a bathtub.
- A hand over her mouth, with a signal she can use at any time: knock twice on the wall and he stops. He says it out loud before, and he asks afterward whether it was right.
- Restraints: she binds his wrists with a working, and he offers them.
- Graphic violence. A neck broken on a porch. A beheading carried out under the law.
- The hero's past: he was turned in the road after a fire and forced to track people for a slave catcher. It is told in his own words, briefly and without looking away.
- Grief. A heroine who pays for her magic with her memories, including the ones she can't spare.
- A villain who is unfailingly polite. That's the worst part.
- Strong language. She's a witch with a temper. He's two hundred years old and was raised in a yard.

Every character in every sexual situation is an adult.

The cat lives. The cat is fine. The cat has opinions.

There is a happy ending. It is earned, it is not soft, and it leaves a grave in the woods for the next book.

If you're still here, you already know what you are.

Welcome back to New Orleans. Mind your name.
