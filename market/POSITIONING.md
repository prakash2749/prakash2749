# Positioning — *King of Teeth* (The Blood Code, Book 1)

Written 2026-09-25 against the finished manuscript (~100k words) and the comps measured in `craft/COMPS_ADELINE.md` and `.claude/CLAUDE.md`.

---

## The one-line promise

**Every woman he feeds on forgets him. She's the one who stayed.**

That line carries the whole book. It's the fantasy of being the one thing a monster can't erase, and it's the brand name (*ghost*) in the reader's mouth before she's opened the cover.

## Shelf

Dark paranormal romance. Vampire. Revenge. Dual POV (hero and heroine) plus villain interstitials. HEA, with the series continuing on a new couple.

**Say what it actually is (revised 2026-09-27 after a full read-through):** a dark vampire romance with a court murder mystery at its spine, and a consensual power-play romance at its heart. The darkness lives in the violence (the dining room, the execution) and in a hero who is dangerous to everyone except her. It is *not* a captive or stalker book. He never hunts her, and the consent device (*You say stop, I stop*) is load-bearing. Selling it as Carlton-style obsession would promise a book readers won't get, and the reviews would say so.

It has more plot and a darker villain than most vampire romance, and more heat per page than most vampire romantasy.

## Trope stack (for blurb, ads, A+ content, TikTok captions)

In order of how hard they sell:

1. **Vampire king** — and he refuses the crown until he finds his father's killer
2. **He can't erase her** — the only person in four hundred years who remembers him
3. **Touch her and die** — ch. 12, ch. 29, and the Laurent scene in particular
4. **Revenge** — she has spent four years planning to kill the wolf who murdered her parents
5. **Morally black hero** — he feeds on his city and doesn't apologize. The massacre chapter stays in.
6. **Blood pact / forced proximity** — his help reaching her target, in exchange for her blood, freely given
7. **Age gap** — 412 / 25
8. **Villain POV** — the reader knows who the murderer is from page one; the hero doesn't
9. **Heat markers:** breath play, blood play, restraint, degradation and praise, dirty talk, *good girl* / *good boy*
10. **New Orleans gothic** — Frenchmen Street, the bayou, a court that never leaves the city

## Comps

- **Rina Kent**, *God of Malice* and the *Legacy of Gods* books: the primary comp. Possessive hero, dual POV, ownership dialogue, and matching heat and dialogue share (`compare.py`).
- **H. D. Carlton**, *Haunting Adeline* / *Hunting Adeline*: heat volume and the pet-name brand only. Use it as an ad-targeting **test**, not as the promise in copy.
- **Vampire-court romance** (ad product targeting only, never KDP keywords): Ali Hazelwood's *Bride* (vampire × werewolf heroine, cross-species politics) and Carissa Broadbent's *The Serpent and the Wings of Night* (vampire court, deadly politics). These readers want exactly the court-intrigue half of this book.

For ads and "readers also enjoyed": *"For readers of Rina Kent who want the monster to have fangs, and a murder in the court."*

Run the Carlton and vampire-court targets as separate ad groups. Scale whichever converts, and cut whichever spends 2–3× the price without a sale (`LAUNCH_PLAN.md` §6).

## What we don't say in marketing

- The killer's name, and that the killer is his uncle
- *Verity*
- That the heroine doesn't get her revenge. The fact that she chooses not to take it is the book's best turn; let readers find it.
